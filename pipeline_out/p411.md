# The dharma eye defined by "conventional truth other than form" — refuted with a stream-enterer's uninterrupted path

## 1 · Passage & Context

**Source Text:**

> ཁ་ཅིག ། རང་གི་བདག་རྐྱེན་དུ་གྱུར་པའི་ཞི་ལྷག་ཟུང་འབྲེལ་གྱི་ཏིང་ངེ་འཛིན་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས་གཟུགས་ལས་གཞན་པའི་རང་ཡུལ་དུ་གྱུར་པའི་ཀུན་རྫོབ་བདེན་པ་མངོན་སུམ་དུ་རྟོགས་པའི་རིག་པ། ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་གྱི་མཚན་ཉིད། ཟེར་ན། རྒྱུན་ཞུགས་ཞུགས་པའི་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་ཉན་ཐོས་ཀྱི་མཐོང་ལམ་བར་ཆད་མེད་ལམ་ཆོས་ཅན། མཚོན་བྱ་དེར་ཐལ། མཚན་ཉིད་དེའི་ཕྱིར། འདོད་ན། ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་ཐོབ་པའི་རྒྱུན་ཞུགས་ཞུགས་པ་ཡོད་པར་ཐལ་ལོ། ། འདོད་ན། འཕགས་པའི་གང་ཟག་གི་དབང་པོ་རྣོ་རྟུལ་གྱི་རིམ་པ་ཇི་ལྟ་བ་བཞིན་དུ་མངོན་སུམ་དུ་རྟོགས་པའི་རྒྱུན་ཞུགས་ཞུགས་པ་ཡོད་པར་ཐལ་ལོ། ། འདོད་ན། གཞན་སེམས་ཤེས་པའི་མངོན་ཤེས་རྒྱུད་ལྡན་གྱི་དེ་ཡོད་པར་ཐལ་ལོ། །

**Preceding Section:** p410 (pages 300–304) is long and does three things. It finishes the sūtra-and-commentary exposition of the five eyes with the Buddha eye (the vajra-like concentration passage and Haribhadra's gloss ཆོས་ཐམས་ཅད་རྣམ་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་དུ་མངོན་པར་རྫོགས་པར་བྱང་ཆུབ་པའི་ཡུལ་ཅན་རྣམས།). It then opens the third heading, the analysis (མཐའ་དཔྱད་པ), which it divides into five: definition, divisions, boundaries, causes, and purpose. It gives the general definition of an eye — [one that arises in dependence on an actual dhyāna as dominant condition](quote:p411-q03) — and the definitions of the five, including the dharma eye as [the knowledge that realizes, just as they are, the grades of sharp and dull faculties of ārya persons](quote:p411-q04). Then comes a string of disputes: whether the flesh eye must be a consciousness, whether the flesh-eye organ "sees", whether the first eye is a sense consciousness, an objector who says the dharma eye's definition fails because the Mahāyāna uninterrupted path and a "scriptural dharma eye" (the twelve branches of scripture) are both dharma eyes, why the flesh eye's range is fixed at a hundred yojanas up to a trichiliocosm, and finally a ཁོ་ན་རེ who defines the flesh and divine eyes as sense consciousnesses based on the physical eye-organ and is refuted with a bodhisattva's flesh eye on the accumulation path and the Pramāṇavārttika line བར་ཆོད་ལ་སོགས་ཡོད་ན་ཡང་། ། དབང་ཤེས་བློ་ནི་སྐྱེ་བར་འགྱུར། །.

**Following Section:** p412 opens the third heading, the boundaries (ས་མཚམས). It quotes Gyaltsab Je's rnam bshad — flesh eye from the accumulation path, divine eye from the preparation path, wisdom eye from the equipoise of the path of seeing, dharma eye from its subsequent attainment, Buddha eye at buddhahood with a concordant form from the eighth ground — and Tsongkhapa's gser phreng on the concordant Buddha eye. It then says these boundaries describe what is principal, not what is exclusive, because a non-path-enterer can have the divine eye and *a Hearer can have the dharma eye*: there are Hearers who know the grades of āryas' sharp and dull faculties, because there are Hearers who have attained the clairvoyance of knowing others' minds. That is the positive counterpart of the chain this paragraph builds, and it is why the chain must stop at a stream-entry *approacher*, not at Hearers as such.

**What this paragraph is doing.** It refutes one alternative definition of the dharma eye. The opponent drops the own-system's object — the faculties of āryas — and puts in its place a whole category: any conventional truth other than form, realized directly, on the basis of the union of calm abiding and insight. Chökyi Gyaltsen answers with one counter-instance, the Hearer path of seeing's uninterrupted path in the continuum of an approacher to stream-entry. He then pushes the opponent's "I accept" down a chain of three consequences, each drawn from the own-system's account of what having a dharma eye involves, and ends on a person the Abhidharma rules out.

>
> If you accept, it follows that there is such a one [an approacher to stream-entry] who possesses in his continuum the clairvoyance of knowing others' minds (གཞན་སེམས་ཤེས་པའི་མངོན་ཤེས, *paracitta-jñāna-abhijñā*).

**On the unstated steps.** Three things are left for the debater to supply. (1) *Why the reason is established.* The paragraph does not say which conventional truth the Hearer's uninterrupted path realizes directly. The author's own mtha' dpyod, at the parallel place, has just argued for [a Hearer on the path of seeing who does not realize emptiness](quote:p411-q06): whatever that path realizes directly, it need not be ultimate truth, and in this Svātantrika-leaning reading of the Ornament the Hearer's object is counted a conventional truth. That object is not form, and the path rests on calm abiding and insight. This supply is my reading of the sequence, not a sentence of the paragraph. (2) *Why the second and third consequences follow.* The second consequence restates the own-system definition of the dharma eye from p410 as a property of the person. The third follows because realizing others' faculties directly is done by the clairvoyance of knowing others' minds — [the way Hearer āryas come to know the faculties of those of equal or lower attainment](quote:p411-q10). (3) *Why the last consequence cannot be accepted.* The paragraph stops at "an approacher to stream-entry with the clairvoyance of knowing others' minds". The mtha' dpyod version of the same refutation [takes one more step, to an approacher to stream-entry who has attained an actual dhyāna, and declares the opponent without an answer](quote:p411-q07). The premise that shuts the door is stated by the same author in his Twenty Saṅgha: [whoever is an approacher to stream-entry or to once-returning has not attained an actual dhyāna](quote:p411-q08). Since [the first five clairvoyances arise in dependence on an actual dhyāna](quote:p411-q09), the last consequence is impossible.

**On the wording.** The spyi don's report of the definition is lighter than the mtha' dpyod's. There [the same opponent](quote:p411-q05) says "uncommon (ཐུན་མོང་མ་ཡིན་པའི) dominant condition" and restricts the definiendum to knowledge "in an ārya continuum" (འཕགས་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་མཁྱེན་པ). The counter-instance works against either form, since the uninterrupted path of the path of seeing is already ārya. The second consequence here adds གྱི་རིམ་པ ("the grades of") to the mtha' dpyod's wording, which brings it closer to the definition in p410.

## 3 · Structural Context (Dkar Chag)

- **Abhisamayālaṃkāra location:** the pack announces no lemma for this outline path, but the text itself names it at the head of the section: p404 opens with སྤྱན་ལྔ་དང་ནི། ཞེས་པས, and p405 repeats it. This is the sixth of the ten instructions (གདམས་ངག) under the first topic, the mind generation and instructions of the omniscient knowledge chapter — [སྤྱན་ལྔ་དང་ནི་མངོན་ཤེས་ཀྱི། ། ཡོན་ཏན་དྲུག་དང་མཐོང་ལམ་དང་། །](quote:p411-q01). Maitreya gives the name "five eyes" and nothing else, and no definition of any eye can be drawn from the root text.
- **Haribhadra's commentary location:** the aligned place in the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ, which I read. Haribhadra glosses each eye by its object in one clause, and the dharma eye gets four words: [འཕགས་པའི་གང་ཟག་ཐམས་ཅད་རྟོགས་པ་དང་།](quote:p411-q02), "the realization of all ārya persons". He gives no definition, no dominant condition, no path boundary, and says nothing about whether a Hearer can have one. Everything this paragraph argues about — the basis in calm abiding and insight versus actual dhyāna, the scope of "conventional truth", who can hold the eye — is absent from the Indian commentary the yig cha is formally expounding. The only textual hook is Haribhadra's phrase, which the own-system definition develops into "the grades of sharp and dull faculties of ārya persons" — through the sūtra's "this person is a follower by faith" as Tsongkhapa reads it (§4).
- **Text location:** སྐབས་དང་པོའི་སྤྱི་དོན། › སྤྱན་ལྔ་ལ་གདམས་པ། (opens p404) › གསུམ་པ་མཐའ་དཔྱད་པ། (opens p410) › ༢༽ དབྱེ་བ། (opens p410). The analysis has five sub-heads (definition, divisions, boundaries, causes, purpose). The first two are given together in p410 and argued there, and this paragraph is the last dispute under the divisions: p412 begins གསུམ་པ་ནི།, the boundaries. So p411 is where the divisions end. It is also the only place in the spyi don where the fourth of the opponent's five definitions (all five are given together in the mtha' dpyod) is taken up on its own.
- **Section type:** refutation of another's position (གཞན་ལུགས་དགག་པ), by a counter-instance (ཆོས་ཅན) followed by a three-step chain of accepted consequences (འདོད་ན … ཐལ་ལོ).
- **Argumentative role:** it defends the own-system definition from p410 by showing that the natural way of making it more general — replacing "the faculties of āryas" with "conventional truths other than form" — lets in a mind that cannot be a dharma eye. The chain also prepares p412. There the author will say Hearers *can* have the dharma eye, and this paragraph has already fixed the lower limit: not before they can have clairvoyance, hence not while approaching stream-entry.

## 4 · Philosophical Exposition & Commentary

**Core meaning.** A definition must be neither too wide nor too narrow, and this one is too wide. The opponent's wording has three parts: (a) a basis — calm abiding and insight in union; (b) an object — a conventional truth other than form, which is its own object; (c) a mode — direct realization. The Hearer's uninterrupted path of seeing meets all three. It rests on a union of calm abiding and insight, it realizes its object directly, and on the reading this curriculum uses its object is not ultimate truth. Yet no one wants to say that a Hearer who has not yet reached even the first fruit holds the dharma eye. The chain shows why: holding it would mean knowing the faculties of other āryas directly, which means having the clairvoyance of knowing others' minds, which means having an actual dhyāna, which an approacher to stream-entry by definition has not got.

**Where the definition leaks: the basis.** The chain turns on a point the opponent's wording hides. The union of calm abiding and insight does not require an actual dhyāna. The Twenty Saṅgha says, immediately before the sentence quoted in §2, that the first two fruits are attained in dependence on the preparatory stage "not without capacity" (ཉེར་བསྡོགས་མི་ལྕོགས་མེད), there being no actual first dhyāna in that continuum — and then gives the reason: [whoever approaches stream-entry or once-returning has not attained an actual dhyāna](quote:p411-q08). So a stream-entry approacher has calm abiding and insight in union and has no actual dhyāna. The own-system avoids this because its general definition of an eye [rests every eye on an actual dhyāna](quote:p411-q03). The opponent, by naming the union rather than the dhyāna, has chosen a basis too low to carry a dharma eye. That is why the refutation ends on dhyāna and not on some fault in the object. The object clause is what lets the counter-instance in, and the basis clause is what lets the opponent be forced all the way down.

**Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen's own view.** The dharma eye is defined by its object, and the object is narrow: [the grades of sharp and dull faculties of ārya persons](quote:p411-q04). His mtha' dpyod runs [the identical refutation](quote:p411-q07) and adds the dhyāna step. His account of the boundaries shows how Hearers get into the picture at all: [the clairvoyance of knowing others' minds in a Hearer ārya's continuum can know the faculties of persons of equal or lower attainment](quote:p411-q10). The consistency matters. The chain in this paragraph is the boundary argument of p412 run in reverse — Hearers can have the dharma eye, but only by the route of clairvoyance, so only once they have an actual dhyāna.

**Je Tsongkhapa.** The ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་ཕྲེང defines the dharma eye by the same object and ties it to the sūtra: [ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་ནི། འཕགས་པའི་གང་ཟག་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་དབང་པོ་རྣོ་རྟུལ་ལ་སོགས་པ་རྣམས་རྟོགས་པའི་ཡུལ་ཅན་ཏེ།](quote:p411-q11) — the bodhisattva's dharma eye knows "this person is a follower by faith" and the like. The own-system definition is Tsongkhapa's gloss given the form of a definition. Tsongkhapa also reports readings with a much wider object, which he does not adopt as the definition: Vasubandhu's (see §5), and one he attributes to the འགྲེལ་ཆེན, "unimpeded engagement with the twelve branches of scripture". Tsongkhapa does not argue about the dharma eye's basis or its holders.

**Gyaltsab Je.** The རྣམ་བཤད་སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན gives the same object and the path boundary that p412 quotes: [the dharma eye realizes the faculties of all ārya persons, and is had from the subsequent attainment of the path of seeing](quote:p411-q17). Taken as a strict boundary, that would exclude Hearers altogether, and p412 has to read it as describing what is principal. The present paragraph gives the reason that reading is needed and still has a limit.

**Panchen Sonam Drakpa.** The འགྲེལ་ཚིག་དོན་རབ་གསལ, being a word-commentary, gives the dharma eye only by its function — [realizing the faculties of all ārya persons](quote:p411-q18) — and carries no analysis of its definition or holders. So the Loseling position on this dispute cannot be read off the available text. That is a limitation of the evidence.

**Ngok Loden Sherab.** The earliest Tibetan source I found for the premise the chain ends on is Ngok's ལུང་རིགས་ཀྱི་རོལ་མཚོ, which divides the dharma eye into scriptural and realizational and [grounds both in an actual dhyāna](quote:p411-q14). The realizational one directly sees the particular realizations of those of equal or lower continuum. Ngok's realizational dharma eye is, in substance, Chökyi Gyaltsen's definition with the basis spelled out. That the basis is dhyāna and not calm-insight union is what this paragraph needs. Found by my own search. The pack did not surface it.

**Sera Jey specificity.** The own-system here does not depart from Tsongkhapa or Gyaltsab on the dharma eye's object. What is characteristic is the method: a definition is tested by the lowest ārya who could satisfy it, and a chain of accepted consequences leads that person back to the Abhidharma rule about who has an actual dhyāna. The Drepung comparison comes from Gomang (§5), not Loseling, for the reason above.

## 5 · Bones of Contention 🦴

**What is disputed?** Two things, one inside the other. The narrow question is whether the dharma eye can be defined as the direct realization, on the basis of calm abiding and insight, of any conventional truth other than form. The wider question behind it is how broad the dharma eye's object is and what it rests on — which settles who can have one.

**Who argues what?**

- **The ཁ་ཅིག position:** the dharma eye is defined by a broad class of object (conventional truths other than form) and a meditative basis (calm abiding and insight in union). The broad object has Indian support. Vasubandhu's གནོད་འཇོམས makes the dharma eye [nine of the ten knowledges — all but that of places and non-places](quote:p411-q12). The Bṛhaṭṭīkā on the Hundred Thousand calls the bodhisattva's dharma eye the knowledge of [Hearer āryas' abandonments and levels, and of bodhisattvas' conduct, powers and qualities](quote:p411-q13). Neither of these is restricted to faculties. A definition by "conventional truths other than form" reads like a generalisation of them. Who held it is not stated, and I did not find it attributed. The pack surfaced no earlier text with this exact wording, and I did not locate one.
- **Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen:** no. The object is restricted to the faculties of āryas, the basis is an actual dhyāna, and the broad definition fails on the stream-entry approacher.
- **Jamyang Zhepa (Gomang):** a revealing middle position. His definition in the ཕར་ཕྱིན་མཐའ་དཔྱོད་རིན་ཆེན་སྒྲོན་མེ keeps the opponent's frame — [calm-insight union as dominant condition, "its own object", direct realization](quote:p411-q16) (the file's OCR reads རྟོབ། ས་ for རྟོགས་) — but names the object as the grades of faculties of *ordinary and ārya* persons (སྐྱེ་འཕགས). He then divides it by the nine knowledges of Vasubandhu and quotes the twelve-branches line. So the Gomang textbook accepts the basis this paragraph's opponent proposes, restricts the object, and keeps the broad Indian readings in as divisions. Jamyang Zhepa is two centuries later than Chökyi Gyaltsen and is not the ཁ་ཅིག here, but his definition shows that the opponent's basis clause survived in the Drepung curriculum. With a faculties-restricted object, the stream-entry counter-instance no longer touches it.
- **Ngok Loden Sherab:** on the basis, with Chökyi Gyaltsen — [actual dhyāna](quote:p411-q14). On holders, against him: [the presentation of these five is not in the Abhidharma, and there is no explanation that even Hīnayānists, let alone outsiders, have them](quote:p411-q15). That conflicts with p412's claim that Hearers can have the dharma eye. It is a real conflict, and neither tier nor date settles it.

**On the "twelve branches" reading.** Both the objector in p410 (who cites it from the རྒྱན་སྣང) and Tsongkhapa (from the འགྲེལ་ཆེན) report a dharma eye that "engages without obstruction in the twelve branches of scripture". I searched the རྒྱན་སྣང file and the Prajñāpāramitā section of the Tengyur for that line and did not locate it verbatim. Its closest attested form is Ngok's scriptural dharma eye (q14). It is reported here only as the texts report it.

**Why does it matter?** Because the definition decides where the dharma eye starts. If any direct, meditative realization of a non-form conventional truth were a dharma eye, it would begin at the first moment of any path of seeing, Hearer or Mahāyāna. The boundary Gyaltsab gives (subsequent attainment of the Mahāyāna path of seeing) and the one p412 allows (Hearers with clairvoyance) would both be meaningless. Restricting the object to faculties makes the dharma eye a kind of knowledge of other minds, and so ties it to the clairvoyances and to actual dhyāna. That is also what separates the dharma eye from the wisdom eye. The wisdom eye sees the nature of things, and the dharma eye sees the grades of other practitioners. That separation is what lets the Buddha's dharma eye in the sūtra be the knowledge that tells a follower-by-faith from a follower-by-dharma.

**Resolution.** Sera Jey follows Chökyi Gyaltsen: the dharma eye is the knowledge that realizes the grades of āryas' faculties, on an actual-dhyāna basis. The opponent's generalisation is rejected on the stream-entry approacher, and the rejection holds even for readers who take the Indian broad readings seriously, since those readings are kept as descriptions and not as definitions. The paragraph's own weight rests on tier-1 material — the author's mtha' dpyod, his Twenty Saṅgha, and the clairvoyance section of this same text. The Gelug commentarial tier (Tsongkhapa, Gyaltsab, Panchen Sonam Drakpa) agrees on the object but is silent on the basis and on holders. The Indian commentaries are silent on everything argued here.

## 6 · Key Terminology & Technical Concepts

- **ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན།** — the dharma eye, fourth of the five eyes.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *dharmacakṣus*.
  - **How understood here:** knowledge of the grades of other practitioners' faculties, not "an eye for dharmas". The term invites the broad reading — any dharma, any conventional truth — and the paragraph exists to shut it off.
  - **Debates:** as §5. Vasubandhu and the Bṛhaṭṭīkā give it a broad object, Tsongkhapa and Gyaltsab restrict it to ārya faculties, Jamyang Zhepa extends it to ordinary beings' faculties too, and Ngok splits off a scriptural dharma eye. The sūtra phrase ཆོས་རྣམས་ལ་ཆོས་ཀྱི་མིག་རྡུལ་མེད་ཅིང་དྲི་མ་དང་བྲལ་བ — used of those who attain the path of seeing — is a different use of the word, which p410's objector conflates with this one.

- **ཞི་ལྷག་ཟུང་འབྲེལ་གྱི་ཏིང་ངེ་འཛིན།** — concentration in which calm abiding and insight are united.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *śamatha-vipaśyanā-yuganaddha-samādhi*.
  - **How understood here:** the opponent's choice of basis, and the weak point of the definition. The union is available at the level of the preparatory stage "not without capacity", below any actual dhyāna. That is how the Hearer path of seeing can have it while its holder has no dhyāna.

- **བསམ་གཏན་གྱི་དངོས་གཞི།** — an actual (main) dhyāna, as distinct from its preparatory stages (ཉེར་བསྡོགས).
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *maula-dhyāna*.
  - **How understood here:** the own-system basis for every eye and for the first five clairvoyances. It is the premise on which the chain ends, though the spyi don leaves it unstated here.

- **རྒྱུན་ཞུགས་ཞུགས་པ།** — the approacher to stream-entry.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *srota-āpatti-pratipannaka*.
  - **How understood here:** the Hearer on the way to the first fruit, not yet abiding in it — in the Twenty Saṅgha the category reaches back even to the preparation path (སྦྱོར་ལམ་སྟན་གཅིག་པ) — who, on the author's rule quoted in §2, has not attained an actual dhyāna. That is why this person, and not the stream-enterer abiding in the fruit, is the counter-instance. The same subject, often in the form སྤང་བྱ་ཅིག་ཅར་པའི་རྒྱུན་ཞུགས་ཞུགས་པ, serves as a stock counter-instance across Chökyi Gyaltsen's textbooks.

- **གཞན་སེམས་ཤེས་པའི་མངོན་ཤེས།** — the clairvoyance of knowing others' minds.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *paracitta-jñāna-abhijñā*.
  - **How understood here:** the means by which a non-buddha, including a Hearer ārya, can know another's faculties directly. It is the link between the dharma eye and the Abhidharma's rules about dhyāna, and the reason the next instruction of the root verse, the six clairvoyances (མངོན་ཤེས་ཀྱི། ། ཡོན་ཏན་དྲུག), follows the five eyes.

- **གཟུགས་ལས་གཞན་པའི་ཀུན་རྫོབ་བདེན་པ།** — a conventional truth other than form.
  - **How understood here:** the opponent's attempt to separate the dharma eye from the first two eyes (whose objects are forms) and from the wisdom eye (whose object is selflessness) by a single contrast of object-types. The contrast is too coarse. The Hearer's path-of-seeing object falls inside it, even though that object is neither form nor, on the reading this curriculum uses, ultimate truth.
