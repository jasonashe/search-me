# The Dharma eye — the fourth of the five eyes, sūtra and gloss

## 1 · Passage & Context

**Source Text:**

> བཞི་པ་ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་ནི། ཤཱ་རི་དྭ་ཏིའི་བུས་གསོལ་པ། བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་བྱང་ཆེན་ཆོས་ཀྱི་མིག་ཡོངས་སུ་དག་པ་གང་ལགས། བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་ཀྱིས་བཀའ་སྩལ་པ། ཤཱ་རིའི་བུ་བྱང་ཆེན་ཆོས་ཀྱི་མིག་ནི་གང་ཟག་འདི་ནི་དད་པའི་རྗེས་སུ་འབྲང་བའོ། ། ཞེས་སོགས་ཀྱིས་བསྟན། དེའི་དོན་འཆད་པ་ལ། འཕགས་པའི་གང་ཟག་ཐམས་ཅད་རྟོགས་པ་དང་༑ ཞེས་གསུངས།

**Preceding Section:** p408 does the same thing for the third eye, the wisdom eye (ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་སྤྱན). It quotes Śāriputra's question and the Buddha's answer that with the wisdom eye the bodhisattva does not fail to know any dharma at all, whether conditioned or unconditioned, virtuous or non-virtuous, contaminated or uncontaminated, mundane or supramundane. It then gives the commentarial gloss, ཆོས་ཐམས་ཅད་ལ་རྣམ་པར་མི་རྟོག་པ་དང་།, "non-conceptual regarding all phenomena." The wisdom eye is an eye for how every dharma is.

**Following Section:** p410 takes the fifth eye, the Buddha eye. The sūtra says it is the wisdom of a single instant, entered after the vajra-like concentration, that sees omniscience and has the ten powers, four fearlessnesses, four discriminating awarenesses, eighteen unshared qualities, great love and great compassion. Nothing is left that it has not seen, heard, considered or understood. The gloss is that it is the subject that fully awakens to all phenomena in all their aspects. After that the yig cha sums up the five eyes with the sūtra's instruction that a bodhisattva who wants them should train in the six perfections. Then it opens a long analytical part with definitions, divisions, boundaries, causes and purpose.

**What this paragraph is doing.** It is the fourth of five identical moves inside ༢༽ དོན་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་གདམས་པ།. Each move pairs a sūtra line with the commentarial gloss that "explains its meaning". Here the eye is the Dharma eye, and the sūtra line and the gloss point in slightly different directions. The sūtra's example is a single ārya type: the faith-follower (དད་པའི་རྗེས་སུ་འབྲང་བ), the dull-faculty person on the path of seeing. The gloss generalises it to *all* ārya persons. The paragraph argues nothing yet. The argument comes a page later, when Chökyi Gyaltsen turns this gloss into a definition and defends it.

>
> says: "and [the subject] that comprehends all ārya persons (འཕགས་པའི་གང་ཟག་ཐམས་ཅད་རྟོགས་པ)."

**On the grammar of the sūtra line.** The yig cha's ཆོས་ཀྱི་མིག་ནི་ … འབྲང་བའོ reads literally as "the Dharma eye is: 'this person is a faith-follower'". This is a compression. The canonical sentence has an instrumental. In the Kangyur Pañcaviṃśati it is [ཆོས་ཀྱི་མིག་གིས་གང་ཟག་འདི་ནི་དད་པའི་རྗེས་སུ་འགྲོ་བ](quote:p409-q01), "*by* the Dharma eye [he knows] 'this person is a faith-follower'". The eye is the knowing, and the typed person is what it knows.

**On the recension.** The yig cha does not follow the Kangyur's wording. Where the Kangyur has ཤཱ་རདྭ་ཏིའི་བུ and རྗེས་སུ་འགྲོ་བ, the yig cha has ཤཱ་རིའི་བུ and རྗེས་སུ་འབྲང་བའོ. That is the wording of the eight-chapter recension of the Pañcaviṃśati that follows the Ornament's chapter divisions (ཉི་ཁྲི་ལེའུ་བརྒྱད་མ), catalogued here under Haribhadra's name: [ཤཱ་རིའི་བུ་འདི་ལ་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཆེན་པོའི་ཆོས་ཀྱི་མིག་གི་གང་ཟག་འདི་ནི་དད་པའི་རྗེས་སུ་འབྲང་བའོ། །](quote:p409-q03). The Tibetan curriculum quotes the "Ornament-arranged" sūtra, not the Kangyur text.

**On the ཞེས་གསུངས.** The gloss is not a line of the root text. The pack found no canonical source for it, and none should be expected. The words are Haribhadra's, from the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ (see §3). The same pattern of སྤྱན་ལྔ་དང་ནི། for the root verse and a phrase of the don gsal for "explaining its meaning" runs through the whole of p405–p410.

## 3 · Structural Context (Dkar Chag)

- **Abhisamayālaṃkāra location:** the pack gives no lemma for this paragraph, but Chökyi Gyaltsen names it himself when he opens the five-eye section (p404–405): སྤྱན་ལྔ་དང་ནི། ཞེས་གསུངས. That is the first line of the verse on the ten instructions in chapter one, [སྤྱན་ལྔ་དང་ནི་མངོན་ཤེས་ཀྱི། ། ཡོན་ཏན་དྲུག་དང་མཐོང་ལམ་དང་། ། བསྒོམ་ཞེས་བྱ་ལ་གདམས་ངག་ནི། ། བཅུ་ཡི་བདག་ཉིད་ཤེས་པར་བྱ། །](quote:p409-q04). The five eyes are the seventh of the ten instructions (གདམས་ངག), the second of the three topics of mind-generation and instruction under the first chapter's exposition of the knowledge of all aspects. Note what comes next in the root text: [དབང་པོ་རྟུལ་དང་རྣོན་པོ་དང་། ། དད་དང་མཐོང་ཐོབ་རིགས་ནས་རིགས། །](quote:p409-q05). The instruction on the Three Jewels opens with the twenty saṅgha, which begin with dull and sharp faculties and with the faith-follower. The Dharma eye's field, as the sūtra and the gloss describe it, is the same ārya typology that the root text catalogues two verses away.
- **Haribhadra's commentary location:** at the aligned place in the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ, the five eyes are glossed in one run: [ཆོས་ཐམས་ཅད་ལ་རྣམ་པར་མི་རྟོག་པ་དང་། འཕགས་པའི་གང་ཟག་ཐམས་ཅད་རྟོགས་པ་དང་། ཆོས་ཐམས་ཅད་རྣམ་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་དུ་མངོན་པར་རྫོགས་པར་བྱང་ཆུབ་པའི་ཡུལ་ཅན་རྣམས་](quote:p409-q06). Haribhadra then says the student is taught to establish these as of one nature in suchness (དེ་བཞིན་ཉིད་དུ་གཅིག་པ་ཉིད་དུ་བསྒྲུབ་བོ). That is the source of the ཞེས་གསུངས in this paragraph (p408 and p410 take their glosses from the same sentence). What Haribhadra does *not* do there is as telling. He does not say which ārya persons are meant, or whether "all" includes bodhisattvas. He says nothing on faculties, gives no boundary on the path, and does not ask whether śrāvakas have this eye. Every question the yig cha later debates about the Dharma eye (definition, boundary, the śrāvaka's possession of it, the "scriptural" Dharma eye) is absent from the don gsal. The Tibetan apparatus is built on a single five-syllable phrase.
- **Text location:** སྐབས་དང་པོའི་སྤྱི་དོན། → སྤྱན་ལྔ་ལ་གདམས་པ། (opens p404) → དང་པོ་ཚིག་དོན་དངོས་ལ། (opens p405) → ༢༽ དོན་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་གདམས་པ། (opens p405). Chökyi Gyaltsen divides the five-eye topic into མདོ་ལས་གསུངས་ཚུལ། བསྟན་བཅོས་ཀྱིས་འགྲེལ་ཚུལ། མཐའ་དཔྱད་པ. This paragraph is inside the second, the word-meaning exposition, and within that inside the instruction "by way of meaning", which has five parts, one per eye. This is the fourth. The third part, ཇི་ལྟར་གདམས་པའི་ཚུལ, and then the analytical part follow p410.
- **Section type:** preliminary exposition: sūtra citation plus commentarial gloss, with no debate.
- **Argumentative role:** it fixes the textual basis on which the definition in the མཐའ་དཔྱད་པ will rest. Chökyi Gyaltsen will define the Dharma eye by its object, the gradation of ārya faculties. That definition is legitimate only if the sūtra line and Haribhadra's gloss are read as saying the same thing. This paragraph sets them side by side so that they will be read that way.

## 4 · Philosophical Exposition & Commentary

**Core meaning.** The Dharma eye is the bodhisattva's knowledge of *persons on the path*, of who is at which stage and with which faculties. The sūtra shows this by example. The Kangyur answer runs through the faith-follower and the Dharma-follower, the three doors of liberation by which their faculties arise, and the stages from stream-enterer to arhat, each identified by the fetters abandoned. Haribhadra compresses all of it into [the subject that comprehends all ārya persons](quote:p409-q06). The wisdom eye of p408 looks at dharmas and sees them non-conceptually. The Dharma eye looks at practitioners and sees them discriminatingly. The Buddha eye of p410 does both completely.

**The Indian glosses do not agree.** The Indian commentaries do not share a single reading of this eye, and it matters which one the yig cha has chosen.

- *Ārya Vimuktisena*, whom Haribhadra is following, has a genitive where Haribhadra has an object: [ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་གྱི་ཡུལ་ནི་འཕགས་པའི་གང་ཟག་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་རྟོགས་པ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་རོ། །](quote:p409-q07), "the object of the Dharma eye is the realisation *of* all ārya persons". On that reading the eye knows what the āryas have understood, not only which kind of ārya someone is. Whether the difference is intended, or is only an artefact of translation, cannot be settled from the Tibetan. It should be noted and no more.
- *Damṣṭrasena*, in the long commentary on the Śatasāhasrikā, reads the sūtra line by line. His Dharma eye is wider than persons: [འཕགས་པ་ཉན་ཐོས་རྣམས་ཀྱི་ཉོན་མོངས་པ་སྤང་བ་དང་། སའི་རིམ་པ་ལ་སོགས་པ་ཤེས་པ་དང་། བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་སྤྱོད་པ་དང་། མཐུ་དང་ཡོན་ཏན་ལ་སོགས་པའི་ཆོས་རྣམས་མཁྱེན་པ་ལ་ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་ཞེས་བྱའོ། ། དེ་ལ་གང་ཟག་འདི་ནི་དད་པའི་རྗེས་སུ་འགྲོ་བ་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་ནི། འཕགས་པ་ཉན་ཐོས་ཀྱི་གང་ཟག་དབང་པོ་རྟུལ་པོ་དད་པའི་རྗེས་སུ་འབྲང་བ་ལ་བྱའོ། །](quote:p409-q09). It covers the abandonments and grounds of śrāvaka āryas and the conduct, powers and qualities of bodhisattvas. His gloss of the paragraph's own example is also the one the Gelug commentators assume: the faith-follower is a *dull-faculty ārya śrāvaka*.
- The *Gnod 'joms* (the Bṛhaṭṭīkā on the three Mothers, attributed in this archive to Vasubandhu) moves the Dharma eye out of person-knowledge and into the Tathāgata's powers: [གནས་དང་གནས་མ་ཡིན་པ་མཁྱེན་པ་མ་གཏོགས་པའི་ཤེས་པ་དགུ་པོ་དག་ནི་ཆོས་ཀྱི་མིག་གོ། །](quote:p409-q08), the nine powers other than knowing what is and is not possible.
- A fourth gloss, reported by Tsongkhapa from an "Great Commentary" (འགྲེལ་ཆེན), makes it a scriptural faculty: [གསུང་རབ་ཡན་ལག་བཅུ་གཉིས་ལ་ཐོགས་མེད་དུ་འཇུག་པ་ནི་ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་ནོ། །](quote:p409-q11), unobstructed entry into the twelve branches of scripture. **This source was not located.** A search of the Indian commentaries and of the Tengyur's Prajñāpāramitā section for the phrase found it in no Indian text. It turns up only in Tibetan works that cite it: Tsongkhapa, Jamyang Zhepa (who also assigns it to the འགྲེལ་ཆེན) and Chökyi Gyaltsen's own opponent (who assigns it to the རྒྱན་སྣང, Haribhadra's *Āloka*). Haribhadra's *Āloka* as held here does not contain it either. The two Tibetan attributions disagree, and neither can be checked from this archive.

The yig cha takes Haribhadra's reading, the one closest to the sūtra's wording, and leaves the others aside. At p409 it does not even mention them.

**Je Tsongkhapa.** The ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་ཕྲེང makes exactly the pairing this paragraph makes and adds one word: [ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་ནི། འཕགས་པའི་གང་ཟག་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་དབང་པོ་རྣོ་རྟུལ་ལ་སོགས་པ་རྣམས་རྟོགས་པའི་ཡུལ་ཅན་ཏེ། བྱང་ཆེན་གྱི་ཆོས་ཀྱི་མིག་གིས་གང་ཟག་འདི་ནི་དད་པའི་རྗེས་སུ་འབྲང་བའོ། ཞེས་དེ་དག་ལ་སོགས་པ་ཤེས་པར་གསུངས་སོ། །](quote:p409-q10). What the eye comprehends about ārya persons is their *sharp and dull faculties*. That word is not in Haribhadra. It is drawn from the sūtra's own example, since the faith-follower is defined by dull faculties, and from the root text's twenty saṅgha verse. Tsongkhapa then goes on to survey the other Indian glosses (the Gnod 'joms, the འགྲེལ་ཆེན) and takes neither.

**Gyaltsab Je.** The རྣམ་བཤད་སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན gives the form the yig cha will adopt: [ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་ནི་འཕགས་པའི་གང་ཟག་དབང་པོ་རྣོ་རྟུལ་ཐམས་ཅད་རྟོགས་པ་དང་།](quote:p409-q12). He also frames the whole instruction: the five eyes are taught as powers of seeing conventionally and then shown to be without inherent nature ultimately. That is his reading of Haribhadra's "one taste in suchness". He adds a boundary on the path, [ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་མཐོང་ལམ་རྗེས་ཐོབ་ནས་དང་།](quote:p409-q13): the Dharma eye comes from the subsequent attainment of the path of seeing.

**Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen.** A page after this paragraph, Chökyi Gyaltsen turns Gyaltsab's gloss into a definition: [དེ་གང་ཞིག འཕགས་པའི་གང་ཟག་གི་དབང་པོ་རྣོ་རྟུལ་གྱི་རིམ་པ་ཇི་ལྟ་བ་བཞིན་དུ་རྟོགས་པའི་མཁྱེན་པ། ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་གྱི་མཚན་ཉིད།](quote:p409-q14). The དེ་གང་ཞིག carries over the genus, "one of the five eyes", from the definition of the wisdom eye just before it. The Dharma eye is then the knower that comprehends, just as it is, the gradation of sharp and dull faculties of ārya persons. Every element can be traced: "ārya persons" from Haribhadra, "sharp and dull" from Tsongkhapa and Gyaltsab, "gradation" from the sūtra's run from faith-follower to arhat. So p409 is where the definiendum's textual warrant is laid down, and the definition is where it is cashed.

He then qualifies Gyaltsab's boundary in a way that matters for reading this paragraph. Gyaltsab's path-stages, he says, mark where each eye is *chiefly* obtained, not where it first appears, for [ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་ཐོབ་པའི་ཉན་ཐོས་ལ་སོགས་པ་ཡང་ཡོད་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p409-q16). The reason he gives is [འཕགས་པའི་གང་ཟག་གི་དབང་པོ་རྣོ་རྟུལ་གྱི་རིམ་པ་ཇི་ལྟ་བ་བཞིན་དུ་ཤེས་པའི་ཉན་ཐོས་ལ་སོགས་པ་ཡོད་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། གཞན་སེམས་ཤེས་པའི་མངོན་ཤེས་ཐོབ་པའི་དེ་ཡོད་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p409-q17): a śrāvaka with the clairvoyance of knowing others' minds does know the gradation of ārya faculties, so he meets the definition. So the sūtra's "*bodhisattva's* purified Dharma eye" is not, for Chökyi Gyaltsen, a restriction on who can have a Dharma eye. It names the bodhisattva's version of an eye that others share.

**Sera Jey and Drepung.** Panchen Sonam Drakpa (Loseling) keeps the ārya reading: [ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་ནི་འཕགས་པའི་གང་ཟག་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་དབང་པོ་རྣོ་བརྟུལ་རྟོགས་པའི་བྱེད་ལས་དང་ལྡན།](quote:p409-q19). On this point Sera Jey and Loseling stand together. Gomang does not. Jamyang Zhepa defines the Dharma eye with [རང་གི་བདག་རྐྱེན་ཞི་ལྷག་ཟུང་འབྲེལ་གྱི་ཏིང་ངེ་འཛིན་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས་རང་ཡུལ་དུ་གྱུར་པའི་སྐྱེ་འཕགས་ཀྱི་དབང་པོའི་རིམ་པ་མངོན་སུམ་དུ་རྟོབ།](quote:p409-q18) (the file's OCR has རྟོབ for རྟོགས). Its object is the gradation of faculties of *ordinary beings and āryas* (སྐྱེ་འཕགས), and its dominant condition is the union of calm abiding and insight. That difference is the subject of §5.

## 5 · Bones of Contention 🦴

The paragraph itself asserts nothing contestable: it quotes and glosses. The contention is in the choice of gloss, and it surfaces openly a page later when Chökyi Gyaltsen defends the definition he builds from it.

**What is disputed?** What the Dharma eye knows, and so whether "all ārya persons" in the gloss this paragraph cites is an adequate account of the sūtra it cites.

**Who argues what?**

- **Haribhadra, Tsongkhapa, Gyaltsab Je, Chökyi Gyaltsen, Panchen Sonam Drakpa:** the Dharma eye's object is ārya persons, specifically the gradation of their sharp and dull faculties. This is the reading p409 sets up.
- **The sūtra itself** does not stop at āryas. After the śrāvaka stages it goes on to the bodhisattva who is only at first mind-generation, practising the six perfections with faith and effort. Of such a bodhisattva, the Dharma eye knows [ཉན་ཐོས་ཀྱི་སའམ་རང་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་སར་ཡང་མི་ལྟུང་ངོ། །བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཆེན་པོ་འདི་ནི་བླ་ན་མེད་པ་ཡང་དག་པར་རྫོགས་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཏུ་མངོན་པར་རྫོགས་པར་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་བར་དུ་ཕྱིར་མི་ལྡོག་པར་འགྱུར་རོ་ཞེས་བྱ་བར་རབ་ཏུ་ཤེས་ཏེ།](quote:p409-q02): that he will not fall to the śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha grounds and is irreversible until full awakening. It then goes on to who is prophesied and who is not. A bodhisattva at first mind-generation is not an ārya. The paragraph's ཞེས་སོགས ("and so on") hides this: the text it points to extends beyond the gloss it attaches.
- **Jamyang Zhepa (Gomang)** writes that extension into the definition, making the object the faculties of ordinary beings *and* āryas. He also grounds the eye in the union of calm abiding and insight. A ཁ་ཅིག a few lines on in Chökyi Gyaltsen's own analysis proposes a definition with that same dominant condition (ཞི་ལྷག་ཟུང་འབྲེལ་གྱི་ཏིང་ངེ་འཛིན་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས), and Chökyi Gyaltsen refutes it. Jamyang Zhepa writes more than a century after Chökyi Gyaltsen, so he cannot be the opponent. But the position Sera Jey rejects has a later home at Gomang.
- **The opponent in the yig cha's own analysis** attacks the ārya-faculties definition from the other side: [ཁོ་ན་རེ། ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་གྱི་མཚན་ཉིད་མི་འཐད་དེ། ཐེག་ཆེན་གྱི་མཐོང་ལམ་བར་ཆད་མེད་ལམ་ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་དང་། ལུང་གི་ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་ཡོད་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p409-q15). The definition is too narrow, because the uninterrupted path of seeing is called a "Dharma eye" (the sūtra's stainless Dharma eye obtained by the gods) and because there is a "scriptural Dharma eye", the twelve-branches gloss. As the text is held here, the yig cha records this objection and moves on to the boundary question without a formal reply, and I did not find one nearby. The implied answer is that these are homonyms, a "Dharma eye" that is not the fourth of the five eyes. That is my inference, not his words.

**Why does it matter?** Two things ride on it. First, the boundary: if the Dharma eye knows only āryas' faculties, it fits Gyaltsab's placement at the subsequent attainment of the path of seeing, since only an ārya can confirm another's ārya status. If it knows ordinary beings' faculties too, that placement is harder to motivate. Second, the purpose: Gyaltsab's account of what the five eyes are *for* makes the fourth eye the one that knows the sharp and dull faculties of disciples and companions, so that Dharma can be taught to fit them. On that account, restricting it to āryas cuts out most of the people a bodhisattva teaches.

**Resolution.** Sera Jey follows Haribhadra as sharpened by Tsongkhapa and Gyaltsab: ārya persons, sharp and dull faculties, in gradation. The justification this paragraph gives is purely textual. The sūtra's own example is an ārya type, and Haribhadra's gloss says "all ārya persons". Chökyi Gyaltsen absorbs the pressure from the other side by granting that śrāvakas share the eye, not by widening its object. I did not find where, if anywhere, he deals with the sūtra's bodhisattvas at first mind-generation, and the tension should be marked as open rather than resolved.

## 6 · Key Terminology & Technical Concepts

- **ཆོས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན།** — the Dharma eye, fourth of the five eyes.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *dharmacakṣus*.
  - **How understood here:** not an eye for dharmas in general (that is the wisdom eye of p408) but for *persons as practitioners of the Dharma*: which path they are on and with what faculties. The "Dharma" in the name is the realised Dharma in others' continua.
  - **Debates:** it is a homonym trap. The sūtras' "stainless Dharma eye" obtained at a teaching names the path of seeing. The twelve-branches gloss names a scriptural facility, and the Gnod 'joms names nine of the ten powers. The yig cha's definition is written to exclude all three.

- **དད་པའི་རྗེས་སུ་འབྲང་བ།** — faith-follower.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *śraddhānusārin*.
  - **How understood here:** Damṣṭrasena's reading is standard: an ārya śrāvaka of dull faculties on the path of seeing, who proceeds by trust in another's instruction, as against the Dharma-follower (ཆོས་ཀྱི་རྗེས་སུ་འབྲང་བ) of sharp faculties who proceeds by his own examination. It is the first entry in the Ornament's twenty saṅgha (དད་དང་མཐོང་ཐོབ). The sūtra chose as its example of what the Dharma eye knows the very distinction the gloss turns into "sharp and dull".

- **དབང་པོ་རྣོ་རྟུལ།** — sharp and dull faculties.
  - **How understood here:** the word Tsongkhapa and Gyaltsab add to Haribhadra, and the core of Chökyi Gyaltsen's definition. It turns "comprehending ārya persons" from recognising that someone is an ārya into recognising *which kind* of ārya: faith-follower or Dharma-follower, a family-to-family stream-enterer, one who takes birth once more, and so on through the twenty.
  - **Debates:** whether the faculties known are only āryas' (Sera Jey, Loseling) or also ordinary beings' (Gomang, Jamyang Zhepa). See §5.

- **ཡོངས་སུ་དག་པ།** — purified, in "purified Dharma eye".
  - **How understood here:** the sūtra's qualifier for all five eyes as possessed by a bodhisattva. Chökyi Gyaltsen does not take it to exclude śrāvakas from having a Dharma eye. It marks the bodhisattva's version, trained through the six perfections, as the sūtra's summary at the end of p410 says.
