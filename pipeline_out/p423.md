# The seeing path explained, the meditation path begun — where the sūtra is cut

## 1 · Passage & Context

**Source Text:**

> གཉིས་པ་དེའི་དོན་འཆད་པ་ལ། མཐོང་ལམ་དང་། ། ཞེས་གསུངས། དེ་ཉིད་ཚིག་དོན་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་འཆད་པ་ལ། མཐོང་བའི་ལམ་བདེན་པ་བཞིས་བསྡུས་པ་སྐད་ཅིག་མ་བཅུ་དྲུག་གི་ངོ་བོ་ཉིད་ཆོས་དང་རྗེས་སུ་ཤེས་པའི་བཟོད་པ་དང་ཤེས་པའི་བདག་ཉིད་ལ་རྣལ་འབྱོར་པ་ཆོས་ཐམས་ཅད་ངོ་བོ་ཉིད་མེད་པར་རྟོགས་པས་སྒྱུ་མ་མཁན་བཞིན་དུ་ཐམས་ཅད་ལ་མངོན་པར་ཞེན་པ་མེད་པའི་ལུས་ཅན་དེ་སྤང་བར་བྱ་བའི་དངོས་པོའི་གཉེན་པོ་ཉིད་དུ་རྣམ་པར་སྒོམ་པར་བྱེད་དོ། ། ཞེས་སྟོན་པ་མཐོང་བའི་ལམ་ལ་འདོམས་པ་དང་། ཞེས་གསུངས༑ རབ་འབྱོར་བྱང་ཆེན་ཤེར་ཕྱིན་ལ་བརྩམས་ཏེ། ཞེས་སོགས་ཀྱི་མདོ་ཆོས་ཅན། ཐེག་པ་ཆེན་པོའི་མཐོང་ལམ་ལ་གདམས་པ་ཡིན་ཏེ། དེ་སྤང་བྱ་ཀུན་བཏགས་ཀྱི་གཉེན་པོར་སྟོན་པའི་ཚུལ་གྱིས་འདོམས་པའི་མདོ་ཚིག་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། འོ་ན། སྒོམ་ཞེས་བྱ་ལ། ཞེས་སྒོམ་ལམ་ལ་གདམས་པའི་གདམས་ངག་འཆད་ཚུལ་གང་ཞེ་ན། འདི་ལ་ཡང་གཉིས་ལས། དང་པོ་ནི། རབ་འབྱོར་འདུས་བྱས་ཀྱི་དབྱིངས་མེད་པར་འདུས་མ་བྱས་ཀྱི་དབྱིངས་སུ་གདགས་པར་མི་ནུས་སོ། ། འདུས་མ་བྱས་ཀྱི་དབྱིངས་མེད་པར་ཡང་འདུས་བྱས་ཀྱི་དབྱིངས་སུ་གདགས་པར་མི་ནུས་སོ༑ ༑ ཞེས་པ་ནས། རབ་འབྱོར་གྱིས་སྨྲས་པ། ཚེ་དང་ལྡན་པ་ཤཱ་རིའི་བུ་བྱང་ཆེན་གྱི་སྐྱོན་ཞེས་པ་ནི་འདི་ལྟ་སྟེ་ཆོས་ལ་སྲེད་པའོ། ། ཞེས་པའི་བར་གྱིས་བསྟན།

**Preceding Section:** p422 (page 317) does two things. It first closes the sixth instruction, on the superknowledges, by quoting the ཡུམ་གྱི་གནོད་འཇོམས་ (the *Bṛhaṭṭīkā* attributed to Vasubandhu): worldly beings, śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas know only the minds of those equal to or below themselves, while the superknowledges of purified bodhisattvas know unobstructedly the powers of the buddhas and those on higher grounds. It then asks how the instruction on the seeing path — introduced as "the cause that brings practice to its culmination" (སྒྲུབ་པ་མཐར་ཐུག་པའི་རྒྱུ) — is to be explained, and divides the answer into how the sūtra teaches it and how the treatise explains it. Under the first it sets out the sūtra's range: from the Buddha's command to Subhūti to speak on the Perfection of Wisdom, through "this teaching is not the domain of any śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha," which is the introductory setting (ཉེ་བར་འགོད་པ); then the sixteen sūtra sections for the sixteen moments, running as far as "Subhūti, the conditioned element does not see the unconditioned, nor the unconditioned element the conditioned."

**Following Section:** p424 (page 319) is the treatise explanation of the meditation path, the mirror image of this paragraph's first half. It quotes Haribhadra's gloss — the conditioned and the unconditioned cannot be designated as different, so there is no meditation path "in its own right" (མཚན་ཉིད་པ) distinct from the seeing path; yet by the nature of dependent origination it is cultivated as the antidote to its own abandonables — and gives the syllogism: the sūtra is an instruction on the meditation path because it teaches it as antidote to the innate (ལྷན་སྐྱེས) abandonables. It then argues that the Mahāyāna meditation path is not a different kind (རིགས་ཐ་དད) from the seeing path, answers the objection that they would then have equal power to abandon, and closes the whole topic of the ten instructions with the reason they number exactly ten.

**What this paragraph is doing.** It is a hinge between two parallel two-part treatments. Its first half (0–609) is the second part of the seeing-path topic: it cites the root-verse word མཐོང་ལམ་དང་།, quotes Haribhadra's gloss on it, and reduces that gloss to a syllogism about the sūtra passage p422 identified. Its second half (609–996) opens the meditation-path topic with the same two-part plan and gives the first part only — the sūtra's range, from "Subhūti, without the conditioned element…" to "the fault of a bodhisattva is craving for dharmas." The paragraph does not argue for anything contested; its work is identification — which words of the Mother are the instruction on which path — and the choice of boundaries it inherits turns out to be Haribhadra's, not the Kangyur's.

**Variant.** The yig cha reads ལུས་ཅན་དེ་སྤང་བར; Haribhadra in the Tengyur reads [ལུས་ཅན་དེས་སྤང་བར](quote:p423-q02), with the instrumental, and so do the Commentaries copy of the དོན་གསལ and Gyaltsab Je's paraphrase (ལུས་ཅན་དེས་སྒོམ་པར་བྱེད་དོ). Read with དེས, the yogin is the agent of the meditating; read as the yig cha prints it, དེ goes naturally with what follows — "the abandonables of that [path]" — which is exactly how the paragraph's own syllogism takes it a few lines later (དེ་སྤང་བྱ་ཀུན་བཏགས). A search of the collection finds the form without ས only in this yig cha.

**On the range.** In the Tengyur copy of the recension the yig cha is quoting, the stretch from the first of these sentences to the second runs to some twelve thousand characters. "From… to…" here marks off a long stretch of the sūtra, and it includes the passage on not being frightened, on the bodhisattva's "peak fault" (རྩེ་མོའི་སྐྱོན), and more; it does not refer to two adjacent lines.

## 3 · Structural Context (Dkar Chag)

- **Abhisamayālaṃkāra location:** the verse of the ten instructions (གདམས་ངག་བཅུ) in the first chapter. The paragraph's two lemmas are its last two items, which a search of the Tengyur locates in Maitreya's verse: [སྤྱན་ལྔ་དང་ནི་མངོན་ཤེས་ཀྱི། །ཡོན་ཏན་དྲུག་དང་མཐོང་ལམ་དང། །བསྒོམ་ཞེས་བྱ་ལ་གདམས་ངག་ནི། །བཅུ་ཡི་བདག་ཉིད་ཤེས་པར་བྱ། །](quote:p423-q01) — "the five eyes, the six qualities of superknowledge, the seeing path and what is called meditation: the instructions are to be known as tenfold." The pack gives no lemma for this outline path; this placement comes from the lemmas the paragraph itself cites (མཐོང་ལམ་དང་། and སྒོམ་ཞེས་བྱ་ལ།), matched against the root text. It is not inferred.
- **Haribhadra's commentary location:** the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ glosses the last two instructions in a single run: first the seeing-path sentence the yig cha quotes ([མཐོང་བའི་ལམ་བདེན་པ་བཞིས་བསྡུས་པ་…མཐོང་བའི་ལམ་ལ་འདོམས་པ་དང།](quote:p423-q02)), then without a break the meditation-path sentence p424 quotes, then the summary that the ten together are all the causes, from the ground of practice to "the cause of culmination, the paths of seeing and meditation." Reading that place settles three things. (1) The Vṛtti glosses kārikās and names no sūtra passage, so the sūtra ranges in this paragraph cannot come from it. They come from Haribhadra's rearranged *Twenty-Five Thousand* (the ལེའུ་བརྒྱད་མ), which inserts labels into the sūtra: [འདི་དག་གིས་ནི་མཐོང་བའི་ལམ་ལ་གདམས་པའོ། །རབ་འབྱོར་འདུས་བྱས་ཀྱི་དབྱིངས་མེད་པར་…](quote:p423-q03) at the start, and [ཆོས་ལ་སྲེད་པའོ་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་ནི་སྒོམ་པའི་ལམ་ལ་གདམས་པའོ། །གདམས་ངག་བཤད་ཟིན་ཏོ། །](quote:p423-q04) at the end. The yig cha's ཞེས་པ་ནས … ཞེས་པའི་བར reproduces those two labels exactly. (2) Haribhadra says only "the things to be abandoned by that" (དེས་སྤང་བར་བྱ་བའི་དངོས་པོ). He does not say "imputed," and he does not say "Mahāyāna." Both qualifications in the paragraph's syllogism are Tibetan additions (see §4). (3) Haribhadra frames the two paths as the cause of *culmination* (མཐར་ཐུག་པའི་རྒྱུ), and p422 and p424 keep that framing. Gyaltsab Je and Panchen Sonam Drakpa instead head the pair as instructions on the *antidotes to abandonables*.
- **Text location:** the pack gives the outline path སྒོམ་ལམ་ལ་གདམས་པ། › སྒོམ་ཞེས་བྱ་ལ། ཞེས་སོགས་ཀྱི་སྐབས་སུ་གཉིས། › ༡༽ མདོ་ལས་གསུངས་ཚུལ་དང་།, all opening in this paragraph at 609. That covers only the second half. The first half (0–609) is the "second" of the two-part division p422 announced for the seeing path (མདོ་ལས་གསུངས་ཚུལ་དང་༑ བསྟན་བཅོས་ཀྱིས་འགྲེལ་ཚུལ་གཉིས), so its heading lies in p422, outside the path the pack supplies. The higher headings above these two, and the ten instructions' own numbering in this text, were not checked here and are not asserted.
- **Section type:** word-commentary and identification of scripture (ཚིག་དོན and མདོ་སྦྱོར). It contains one syllogism and no objection; there is no ཁ་ཅིག and no ཟེར་ན.
- **Argumentative role:** to complete the mapping of the ten instructions onto the sūtra. The first half fastens the Indian gloss onto a sūtra passage by a syllogism, so that the passage becomes the subject of which "instruction on the seeing path" is predicated. The second half fixes the start and end of the meditation-path passage, which p424 will then treat the same way. The closing boundary, ཆོས་ལ་སྲེད་པའོ, is also the seam with the next topic: Chökyi Gyaltsen's own treatment of the Mahāyāna path of preparation opens by saying that the preparation-path sūtra arises from exactly this exchange: [ཤཱ་རིའི་བུས་སྐྱོན་དེ་དྲིས་པའི་ལན་དུ། … ཆོས་ལ་སྲེད་པའོ། ། ཞེས་གསུངས་པ་ལས་འཕྲོས་ནས། སྦྱོར་ལམ་གྱི་མདོ་འདི་གསུངས་པ་ཡིན་ཏེ།](quote:p423-q11).

## 4 · Philosophical Exposition & Commentary

**Core meaning.** The seeing path is sixteen moments — the four forbearances and knowledges (dharma-forbearance, dharma-knowledge, subsequent forbearance, subsequent knowledge) for each of the four truths. It is cultivated by a yogin who has realised that nothing has own-nature and who therefore clings to none of it, as a magician does not cling to the illusion he has made. It is cultivated as the antidote to what the seeing path abandons. The sūtra instructs on it by *teaching it as that antidote*. For the meditation path the paragraph gives only the scriptural range. Its content — why a sentence about the conditioned and unconditioned elements should instruct on meditation at all — is left to p424.

**Where the two Tibetan qualifications come from.** The paragraph's syllogism adds two things to Haribhadra: ཐེག་པ་ཆེན་པོའི (the seeing path is the Mahāyāna one) and ཀུན་བཏགས (its abandonables are the imputed ones). Both come from Gyaltsab Je, writing within the same Gelug transmission. The yig cha's sentence is his sentence, lightly shortened: [རབ་འབྱོར་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཆེན་པོ་རྣམས་ལ་ཤེར་ཕྱིན་ལ་བརྩམས་ཏེ་སྤོབས་པབ་སྐྱེད་ཅིག་ཅེས་པས་མཐོང་བའི་ལམ་ལ་འདོམས་པ་ཡིན་ཏེ། མཐོང་ལམ་དེས་སྤང་བར་བྱ་བའི་ཀུན་བརྟགས་ཀྱི་དངོས་པོའི་གཉེན་པོ་ཉིད་དུ་རྣམ་པར་བསྒོམ་པར་བྱེད་དོ་ཞེས་སྟོན་པའི་ཚུལ་གྱིས་འདོམས་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p423-q07). Gyaltsab Je heads the pair "instruction on the antidote to imputed abandonables, and on the antidote to innate ones." For the meditation path he says, of the very sūtra line this paragraph quotes: [ཞེས་སོགས་ཀྱིས་དཔེའི་སྒོ་ནས་སྒོམ་པའི་ལམ་ལ་འདོམས་པ་སྟེ་སྒོམ་ལམ་དེས་སྤང་བར་བྱ་བའི་སྤང་བྱ་ལྷན་སྐྱེས་ཀྱི་དངོས་པོའི་གཉེན་པོ་ཉིད་དུ](quote:p423-q08). That second half is what p424's syllogism reproduces. Chökyi Gyaltsen is therefore glossing Haribhadra through the རྣམ་བཤད་སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན, not directly.

**Tsongkhapa's different gloss.** The ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་ཕྲེང explains the same words of Haribhadra with a different classification. For him, what the seeing path abandons is [མཐོང་སྤང་གི་དངོས་པོ](quote:p423-q09): the abandonables are classed by *the path that removes them*. He does not class them by their mode of arising, imputed or innate. On the meditation path he likewise speaks of སྒོམ་སྤང. In the Svātantrika reading under which the *Ornament* is taught these two classifications line up (seeing-abandonables are the imputed ones), so this is a difference of wording rather than of doctrine. It is still worth noticing that the yig cha takes Gyaltsab Je's wording rather than his teacher's. Tsongkhapa also records that the ལེའུ་བརྒྱད་མ and Vimuktisena's ཉི་ཁྲི་སྣང་བ agree on how the seeing-path sūtra is fitted to the sixteen moments (ལེ་བརྒྱད་མ་དང་ཉི་སྣང་གཉིས་སྦྱོར་ཚུལ་ལ་ཁྱད་མེད་དོ). On that the tradition is unanimous.

**Why a sentence about the elements is an instruction on meditation.** The sūtra line on its face is ontology: neither the conditioned nor the unconditioned element can be posited without the other. The Indian and Tibetan commentators make it an instruction on the meditation path in two related ways. Ārya Vimuktisena, at the greatest distance from this curriculum but also the earliest, glosses it directly: [དེ་ལ་འདུས་བྱས་ནི་ལམ་མོ། །འདུས་མ་བྱས་ནི་སྤངས་པ་སྟེ། དེ་གཉིས་ཕན་ཚུན་ཐ་དད་པར་གདགས་པར་མི་ནུས་པ་ཉིད་ཀྱིས་ན་སྒོམ་པའི་ལམ་རྣམ་པར་གཞག་པ་ནི་བཏགས་པ་ཡིན་གྱི། མཚན་ཉིད་པ་ནི་མ་ཡིན་ཏེ།](quote:p423-q06) — "the conditioned is the path, the unconditioned the abandonment; because the two cannot be designated as different, the meditation path is posited only nominally, not as something in its own right." Tsongkhapa offers this as his second reading: [ཡང་ན་དཔེ་འདི་མདོ་ལས་རབ་འབྱོར་འདུས་བྱས་ཀྱི་དབྱིངས་མེད་པར་…ཞེས་གསུངས་ལ། དེས་སྒོམ་ལམ་དཔེའི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟན་པའི་རྒྱུ་མཚན་དུ་སྦྱར་ཏེ](quote:p423-q10). On that reading, the "just as the conditioned and unconditioned cannot be designated as different" in Haribhadra's gloss *is* the sūtra line, used as an example (དཔེ). Gyaltsab Je's དཔེའི་སྒོ་ནས says the same thing. So when this paragraph fixes the sūtra's starting point at འདུས་བྱས་ཀྱི་དབྱིངས་མེད་པར, it is not merely marking where a section begins. It is picking out the sentence that Haribhadra's gloss, read in p424, will turn into the reason why seeing and meditation do not differ in what they realise.

**Whose sūtra.** The yig cha's quotation has the wording of Haribhadra's rearranged sūtra: it opens with a vocative རབ་འབྱོར་ and uses the locative དབྱིངས་སུ་གདགས་པར. In the Kangyur's *Twenty-Five Thousand* (the only copy in this archive, vol. KA), the same content stands inside the preceding sentence as its reason: [དེ་ཅིའི་ཕྱིར་ཞེ་ན། འདུས་བྱས་ཀྱི་དབྱིངས་མ་ཡིན་པར་ཡང་འདུས་མ་བྱས་ཀྱི་དབྱིངས་གདགས་པར་མི་ནུས་སོ། །…](quote:p423-q05). There the Buddha is explaining *why* the conditioned element does not see the unconditioned (the sixteenth moment, which p422 quoted last). He is not beginning a new teaching. Vimuktisena and Haribhadra both cut the sūtra at this point and assign what follows to the meditation path, and the Tibetan tradition follows them. That is ordinary for the ཉི་ཁྲི་ལེའུ་བརྒྱད་མ, which was edited precisely to make the sūtra's divisions match the *Ornament*. But it means the paragraph's claim "this is taught by the sūtra from … to …" is a claim about the sūtra *as Haribhadra divided it*. Only one Kangyur edition was checked here. Whether other editions read differently was not examined.

**Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen's own view.** Within this paragraph his contribution is choice rather than argument. He keeps Haribhadra's framing of the pair as the cause of culmination (p422, p424). He takes Gyaltsab Je's imputed/innate specification into the syllogisms. And he takes the ལེའུ་བརྒྱད་མ's boundaries for the sūtra. His preparation-path overview confirms the closing boundary from the far side (quoted in §3). No passage was found in his སྐབས་དང་པོའི་མཐའ་དཔྱོད that debates these two instructions. A search for the paragraph's distinctive phrases (ཐེག་པ་ཆེན་པོའི་མཐོང་ལམ་ལ་གདམས, ཀུན་བཏགས་ཀྱི་གཉེན་པོར་སྟོན) turns them up only in this yig cha.

**Sera Jey specificity.** There is none on this point. Panchen Sonam Drakpa's Loseling textbook gives the same imputed/innate division under a heading of its own, [གསུམ་པ་སྤང་བྱ་སེལ་བ་ལ་གདམས་པ་ལ། སྤང་བྱ་ཀུན་བཏགས་ཀྱི་གཉེན་པོ་ལ་གདམས་པ་དང་། ལྷན་སྐྱེས་ཀྱི་གཉེན་པོ་ལ་གདམས་པའོ། །](quote:p423-q12), and then the same syllogism in near-identical words. Both yig chas depend on Gyaltsab Je. The only difference in *framing* is that Chökyi Gyaltsen keeps Haribhadra's "cause of culmination" heading, while Panchen Sonam Drakpa and Gyaltsab Je head the pair by what it removes. On this evidence, that is a difference of presentation, not of position.

## 5 · Bones of Contention 🦴

The paragraph is exposition, not debate: it has no ཁ་ཅིག, and on what it asserts the Gelug commentators consulted agree. Three questions nonetheless run through it. The third is the real one, and the text hands it forward to p424.

**1. Is the instruction's sūtra text where the paragraph says it is?**

- **What is disputed:** whether "without the conditioned element the unconditioned cannot be designated…" begins a new instruction, or completes the last seeing-path moment.
- **Who argues what:** the Kangyur's *Twenty-Five Thousand* presents it as the reason (དེ་ཅིའི་ཕྱིར་ཞེ་ན) for the sixteenth-moment sentence. Vimuktisena (སྒོམ་པའི་ལམ་ལ་གདམས་པའི་དབང་དུ་མཛད་ནས) and Haribhadra's ལེའུ་བརྒྱད་མ (with its inserted label) start the meditation-path instruction there. Gyaltsab Je, Tsongkhapa, Panchen Sonam Drakpa and this yig cha follow the Indian commentators.
- **Why it matters:** the division is not neutral. Detached from the sixteenth moment, the sentence becomes the example on which p424's thesis rests: meditation realises nothing that seeing did not.
- **Resolution:** Sera Jey follows Haribhadra's recension without comment. No Tibetan author found here raises the Kangyur reading. This is an observation about the sources, not a controversy anyone in the tradition conducts.

**2. How are the abandonables of the two paths to be named?**

- **Who argues what:** Haribhadra says only "what is abandoned by it." Tsongkhapa: seeing-abandonables and meditation-abandonables (མཐོང་སྤང / སྒོམ་སྤང). Gyaltsab Je, followed by Panchen Sonam Drakpa and Chökyi Gyaltsen: imputed and innate (ཀུན་བཏགས / ལྷན་སྐྱེས).
- **Why it matters:** the two ways of naming them coincide only if everything the seeing path removes is imputed and everything left for meditation is innate. That identification is exactly what the syllogisms here assume.
- **Resolution:** the yig cha takes Gyaltsab Je's wording. I have not established whether that identification is contested by any of these authors for this context. The passages examined do not raise it, and it is marked here as unexamined, not as settled.

**3. Is there a meditation path "in its own right"?** This is the substantive question, and the sūtra line quoted here is its locus.

- **Who argues what:** Vimuktisena says the meditation path is posited only nominally (བཏགས་པ), not in its own right (མཚན་ཉིད་པ་མ་ཡིན). Haribhadra says no meditation path in its own right is posited distinct from seeing, "but nonetheless" (འོན་ཀྱང) by dependent origination it is cultivated as an antidote. Tsongkhapa limits the denial: there is no meditation path in its own right *whose way of realising reality differs from the seeing path's* (མཐོང་ལམ་གྱི་ཆོས་ཉིད་རྟོགས་ཚུལ་དང་མི་མཐུན་པའི་སྒོམ་ལམ་མཚན་ཉིད་པ … མེད་ཀྱི་བཏགས་པ་བ་ཡིན). That makes the "nominal" status a claim about its object and mode of realisation, not about whether the path exists.
- **Why it matters:** read without Tsongkhapa's limit, Vimuktisena's sentence would make the Mahāyāna meditation path unreal as a path. That would sit badly with the extended treatment the meditation path receives in the *Ornament*'s later chapters.
- **Resolution:** Chökyi Gyaltsen's answer is in p424, not here. The meditation path is not a different *kind* (རིགས་ཐ་དད་པ་མ་ཡིན) from the seeing path, because it meditates on reality in the same way. It still removes the innate obscurations to knowledge that the seeing path cannot, because it has been cultivated longer. That power comes to it by the nature of dependent origination. This is Tsongkhapa's limit, turned into a debate. This paragraph's share in it is to fix the scriptural sentence from which the question starts.

## 6 · Key Terminology & Technical Concepts

- **གདམས་ངག།** — instruction (*avavāda*), the second of the seventy topics; tenfold in the *Ornament*.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *avavāda*.
  - **How understood here:** an instruction is not a separate doctrine. It is sūtra wording picked out by the commentator, and the syllogisms in this paragraph have that wording (མདོ་ཚིག) as their subject. The paths are what the instruction is *about*. What instructs is the sūtra, "by way of teaching" (སྟོན་པའི་ཚུལ་གྱིས) the path as an antidote.
  - **Debates:** where each instruction's sūtra begins and ends is fixed by Haribhadra's ལེའུ་བརྒྱད་མ labels and is not independently re-derived by the Tibetan authors.

- **སྐད་ཅིག་མ་བཅུ་དྲུག།** — the sixteen moments of the seeing path: for each of the four truths, dharma-forbearance, dharma-knowledge, subsequent forbearance and subsequent knowledge (ཆོས་བཟོད་, ཆོས་ཤེས་, རྗེས་བཟོད་, རྗེས་ཤེས་).
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *ṣoḍaśa kṣaṇāḥ*; *dharmajñānakṣānti*, *dharmajñāna*, *anvayajñānakṣānti*, *anvayajñāna*.
  - **How understood here:** they are what the sixteen sūtra sections of p422 are assigned to. Haribhadra's compressed ཆོས་དང་རྗེས་སུ་ཤེས་པའི་བཟོད་པ་དང་ཤེས་པ is spelled out by Panchen Sonam Drakpa as ཆོས་ཤེས་པའི་བཟོད་པ་དང་རྗེས་སུ་ཤེས་པའི་བཟོད་པ … ཤེས་པའི་བདག་ཉིད.
  - **Debates:** whether these are sixteen successive moments of mind is treated at length in the fourth chapter, not here.

- **སྒྱུ་མ་མཁན་བཞིན་དུ … མངོན་པར་ཞེན་པ་མེད་པ།** — non-clinging, like a magician's.
  - **How understood here:** Tsongkhapa spells the image out as the magician's lack of clinging to the illusion he himself has emanated. The magician sees the illusion without being taken in by it, which is how the yogin's post-realisation appearances are meant. Gyaltsab Je and Panchen Sonam Drakpa add the qualifier "as truly existent" (བདེན་པར་མངོན་པར་མ་ཞེན་པ). The clinging removed is clinging to true existence, not to appearance.

- **ཀུན་བཏགས་ / ལྷན་སྐྱེས།** — imputed (acquired through tenets and reasoning) versus innate abandonables.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *parikalpita* / *sahaja*.
  - **How understood here:** the pair that, following Gyaltsab Je, distinguishes the objects of the seeing and meditation paths in this yig cha's syllogisms. Haribhadra does not use it at this place.
  - **Debates:** Tsongkhapa's gloss divides the same abandonables by the path that removes them (མཐོང་སྤང / སྒོམ་སྤང) instead; see §5, question 2.

- **འདུས་བྱས་ཀྱི་དབྱིངས་ / འདུས་མ་བྱས་ཀྱི་དབྱིངས།** — the conditioned and the unconditioned element.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *saṃskṛta-dhātu* / *asaṃskṛta-dhātu*.
  - **How understood here:** in the sūtra, two elements that cannot be designated without each other. In Vimuktisena and Tsongkhapa's second reading, the path (conditioned) and the abandonment it brings about (unconditioned, the cessation). That mapping is what lets an ontological sentence serve as an instruction on meditation.

- **གདགས་པ།** — to designate or posit.
  - **How understood here:** the sūtra's མི་ནུས is a claim about what can be *designated* apart from its correlate, not about what exists. Vimuktisena draws from it that the meditation path is *designated* (བཏགས་པ) and not in its own right. The whole of p424 is an effort to keep that inference from proving too much.

- **ཆོས་ལ་སྲེད་པ།** — craving for dharmas, named as "the fault of a bodhisattva."
  - **How understood here:** the last words of the meditation-path passage. The sūtra goes straight on to say what that craving is — clinging even to "form is impermanent," "empty," "selfless" — and Chökyi Gyaltsen makes the preparation-path teaching grow out of that answer. So the meditation-path instruction ends on the danger that the next topic, the path of preparation, is designed to meet: clinging to the very aspects of the truths that a practitioner is taught to meditate on.
