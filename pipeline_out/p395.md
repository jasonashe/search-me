# Is King Ngalenu a sentient being now? — tense, identity and the resultant refuge

## 1 · Passage & Context

**Source Text:**

> ཁོ་ན་རེ། རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུས་ཆོས་ཅན། ད་ལྟར་དུས་སུ་སེམས་ཅན་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། སེམས་ཅན་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། ཁྱབ་སྟེ། སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་ཡིན་ན། ད་ལྟར་གྱི་དུས་སུ་སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་ཡིན་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཟེར་ན། མདུན་གྱི་སྐྱེས་བུ་ལན་འདེབས་པ་པོ་སངས་རྒྱས་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་ཆོས་ཅན། དེར་ཐལ། དེའི་ཕྱིར། འདོད་ན། མདུན་གྱི་སྐྱེས་བུ་ལན་འདེབས་པ་པོ་ད་ལྟར་གྱི་དུས་སུ་སངས་རྒྱས་ཟིན་པར་ཐལ༑ འདོད་པའི་ཕྱིར། ཁ་ཅིག་གིས། གང་ཟག་དེའི་འབྲས་སྐྱབས་སུ་གྱུར་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་ཡིན་ན། གང་ཟག་དེའི་དུས་སུ་མེད་པས་ཁྱབ། ཟེར་ན། སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་ཆོས་ཅན། ཁྱོད་རང་དང་དུས་མཉམ་དུ་གྱུར་པའི་བྱང་སེམས་ཚོགས་ལམ་པའི་དུས་སུ་མེད་པར་ཐལ། ཁྱོད་དེའི་འབྲས་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། ཁྱབ་པ་ཁས།

(p395, page 283 of སྐབས་དང་པོའི་སྤྱི་དོན།.)

**Preceding Section:** p394 finishes a chain that began in p393 over whether Śākyamuni is "of one continuum" (རྒྱུད་གཅིག) with his former births. The yig cha has asserted that King Ngalenu (རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུས, Māndhātṛ, one of the Teacher's former lives) *is, at the present time, the Teacher Śākyamuni* (ད་ལྟར་གྱི་དུས་སུ་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་ཡིན). An opponent tries to turn this round: then Śākyamuni was Ngalenu at Ngalenu's time, and so would be his own cause. The yig cha refuses that, and then answers a ཁ་ཅིག who says the Buddha who is of one continuum with "the person in front, the respondent" (མདུན་གྱི་སྐྱེས་བུ་ལན་འདེབས་པ་པོ) does not exist now. Its reply is that he does exist now, *because the three jewels that are that respondent's ultimate resultant refuge exist now* (དེའི་འབྲས་སྐྱབས་མཐར་ཐུག་ཏུ་གྱུར་པའི་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་པོ་དེ་ད་ལྟར་ཡོད་པའི་ཕྱིར།). Both halves of p395 attack those two commitments: "Ngalenu is Śākyamuni now" and "a person's resultant refuge exists now."

**Following Section:** p396 opens a new heading, གསུམ་པ, the division into conventional and ultimate refuge (ཀུན་རྫོབ་པའི་སྐྱབས་དང་། དོན་དམ་པའི་སྐྱབས). It quotes the *Uttaratantra* (སྤང་ཕྱིར་བསླུ་བའི་ཆོས་ཅན་ཕྱིར། …), and explains why the twelve branches of scripture are not a final refuge, citing the *Sūtrālaṃkāra*'s raft simile and the *Uttaratantra* commentary's two kinds of dharma, scriptural and realized. So p395 is the last paragraph of the debate section on causal and resultant refuge. It ends at ཁྱབ་པ་ཁས། without a closing formula.

**What this paragraph is doing.** It contains two short exchanges, both about *when* a refuge-object is what it is. In the first (ཁོ་ན་རེ), the opponent argues that Ngalenu is a sentient being now, simply because he is a sentient being. He supports the pervasion with the principle that whatever is a Buddha must be a Buddha now. The reply finds a Buddha who is not a Buddha now: the Buddha the respondent will become. In the second (ཁ་ཅིག), someone holds that a person's resultant refuge cannot exist at that person's time. The reply points to the Buddha and the path-of-accumulation bodhisattva who are *contemporaneous* (དུས་མཉམ) with each other.

**On what is left unsaid.** The second exchange stops at ཁྱབ་པ་ཁས།. The paragraph does not prove the reason, and it does not say why the consequence cannot be accepted. The author's own mtha' dpyod supplies both steps (§4 below): the reason holds because the Buddha is the bodhisattva's final attainment. The consequence is unacceptable because two things that are contemporaneous exist at each other's time. In the spyi don that is left to the student.

**Variant readings.** The edition's apparatus records that the ག witness reads ད་ལྟར་དུས་སུ in the first line, as printed here, while ཏ and ཀ read ད་ལྟར་གྱི་དུས་སུ. It also records that ཀ reads འདོད་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཟེར། at the end of the first exchange, where ག and ཏ have only འདོད་པའི་ཕྱིར།. With ཟེར, the reply to the first objection would itself be marked as something "said." That matters for §5, and I report it without choosing between the readings. For the closing debate, the apparatus says ཏ agrees with ག, while ཀ agrees with a witness siglum ཚ, which reads instead [ཚ་༡༩༧་༥་སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་དང་དུས་མཉམ་དུ་གྱུར་པའི་བྱང་སེམས་ཚོགས་ལམ་པ་ཆོས་ཅན། དེར་ཐལ། དེའི་ཕྱིར། རྟགས་གསལ་སླའོ། །](quote:p395-q09). That reading takes the contemporaneous bodhisattva as subject and closes with "the reasons are clear and easy." The sense is the same counter-instance, approached from the bodhisattva's side.

## 3 · Structural Context (Dkar Chag)

- **Abhisamayālaṃkāra location:** chapter one, the ten instructions (གདམས་ངག་བཅུ), specifically the instruction on the three jewels: [སངས་རྒྱས་ལ་སོགས་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ། ། མ་ཞེན་ཡོངས་སུ་མི་ངལ་དང་། །](quote:p395-q11). The yig cha hangs its whole refuge excursus on this line, from the Buddha jewel at p376, through the Saṅgha jewel from p383, to the general presentation of refuge in which this paragraph falls.
- **Haribhadra's commentary location:** at the same lemma the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ། glosses the Buddha jewel as an instruction on non-apprehension: [སངས་རྒྱས་ལ་སོགས་སངས་རྒྱས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་དག་མཚན་ཉིད་ཀྱིས་སངས་རྒྱས་སུ་བྱེད་པའི་ཆོས་ཀྱི་མཚན་ཉིད་རྣམ་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པ་ཉིད་མི་དམིགས་པ་ལ་གཟུགས་ལ་སོགས་པ་མི་སྦྱོར་བས་དམིགས་པར་བྱ་བ་དང་དམིགས་པར་བྱེད་པ་ཉིད་དུ་ཤེས་པ་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་དང་།](quote:p395-q12). He then treats the dharma as lacking own-nature and the Saṅgha as the twenty. I read the passage around the lemma, and Haribhadra says nothing about causal as against resultant refuge, and nothing about whether a refuge exists at the refuge-seeker's time. Gyaltsab Je's རྣམ་བཤད་སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན། and Tsongkhapa's ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་ཕྲེང་ at the aligned lemma are also silent on the question. The gser phreng uses the place for the twenty Saṅgha, and the causal/resultant distinction enters Gyaltsab's text only later, under སྐྱབས་སྤྱིའི་རྣམ་གཞག, from the *Uttaratantra*. The whole controversy is therefore a Tibetan development attached to this lemma, with no Indian commentarial counterpart.
- **Text location:** under སྐྱབས་གསུམ་ལ་གདམས་པ། (from p376), then གསུམ་པ་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་བཤད་པ་ལ། (from p383), then ༢༽ སྐྱབས་སྤྱིའི་རྣམ་གཞག་བཤད་པ།. The pack places this last heading at p388. In my reading, the text's own གཉིས་པ་ནི། with the *Uttaratantra* verse སྟོན་པ་བསྟན་པ་སློབ་དོན་གྱིས། actually falls at p390, and p388–389 still read as Saṅgha-jewel debates. I mark that boundary as uncertain. After p390's exposition, p391–p395 are a run of debates on causal and resultant refuge, and p395 is the last of them before p396's གསུམ་པ.
- **Section type:** objection and response. The first exchange is a ཁོ་ན་རེ objection answered by counter-instance. The second is a ཁ་ཅིག thesis refuted by a ཐལ་འགྱུར whose pervasion the opponent has already granted.
- **Argumentative role:** it is defensive. p393–p394 committed the yig cha to two claims about time: that Ngalenu is Śākyamuni now, and that a person's resultant refuge exists now. p395 protects both from the natural objection that tense cannot be ignored like this.

## 4 · Philosophical Exposition & Commentary

**The notion in play.** Both exchanges depend on the yig cha's own definition of resultant refuge at p390: [ཐེག་ཆེན་ལམ་དུ་ཞུགས་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་རང་གི་ཐོབ་བྱའི་སངས་རྒྱས་འབྲས་བུའི་སྐྱབས་དང་། གཞན་རྒྱུད་ལ་གྲུབ་ཟིན་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་རྒྱུའི་སྐྱབས་དང་།](quote:p395-q01). For those who have entered the Mahāyāna path, the Buddha to be attained oneself is the resultant refuge, and the Buddha already accomplished in another's continuum is the causal refuge. This follows Gyaltsab Je almost to the word: [ཐེག་པ་ཆེན་པོའི་རིགས་ཅན་ལམ་ལ་ཞུགས་པ་དེ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་རང་རྒྱུད་ལ་ཐོབ་བྱའི་སངས་རྒྱས་འབྲས་བུའི་སྐྱབས་དང་། གྲུབ་ཟིན་གྱི་སངས་རྒྱས་རྒྱུའི་སྐྱབས་སུ་ཤེས་པར་བྱ་བ་དང་།](quote:p395-q02). The pair ཐོབ་བྱ / གྲུབ་ཟིན, "to be attained" as against "already accomplished," is what makes both objections feel natural. If a resultant refuge is something *to be* attained, it seems it cannot be there yet, and that is the second ཁ་ཅིག's thesis. And if Śākyamuni is Ngalenu's resultant refuge, Ngalenu seems to belong to the time before it, as a sentient being, and that is the first objection.

**Why Ngalenu.** The mtha' dpyod shows why the king is the subject. Chökyi Gyaltsen argues there that causal and resultant refuge are not incompatible, since Śākyamuni is both: [ཁ་ཅིག རྒྱུའི་སྐྱབས་དང་འབྲས་བུའི་སྐྱབས་གཉིས་འགལ་ཟེར་བ་མི་འཐད་དེ། སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་དེ་དེ་གཉིས་ཀ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། དེ་རྒྱུའི་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་པ་གང་ཞིག དེ་འབྲས་བུའི་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། དང་པོ་གྲུབ་སྟེ། སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། གཉིས་པ་དེར་ཐལ། རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུས་ཀྱི་འབྲས་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། དེའི་ཐོབ་བྱ་མཐར་ཐུག་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p395-q03). Śākyamuni is a resultant refuge because he is King Ngalenu's final attainment. Once that is said, Ngalenu is both a sentient being, since a king is one, and a person whose final attainment has already been reached. The Gomang textbook uses the same stock example for the same purpose. Jamyang Zhepa makes Śākyamuni the resultant refuge of the boy Snang byed and of Ngalenu: [སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་ཆོས་ཅན། རྒྱུ་སྐྱབས་མ་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། འབྲས་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། དེར་ཐལ། ཁྱེའུ་སྣང་བྱེད་དང་ང་ལས་ནུའི་འབྲས་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། དེ་གཉིས་ཀྱི་ཐོབ་བྱ་མཐར་ཐུག་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། འདོད་མི་ནུས་ཏེ། རང་གི་གདུལ་བྱ་ཐེག་པ་གསུམ་གྱི་གང་ཟག་གི་རྒྱུ་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p395-q04). Jamyang Zhepa does not follow the example into questions of tense. The premise is shared across Sera Jey and Gomang, but the temporal debate belongs to Chökyi Gyaltsen's texts.

**The first exchange.** The opponent's inference, "a sentient being, therefore a sentient being now," treats a predicate that holds of a person as holding at every time the person exists. Its support is a principle that looks safe for Buddhas, since a Buddha does not stop being one: whatever is a Buddha is a Buddha now. The reply shows this is not safe even for Buddhas. The respondent's future Buddha is an ārya Buddha. If every ārya Buddha were one at the present time, the respondent's Buddha would be one now, and the respondent would already be enlightened. The general point is that "X is F" does not entail "X is F at the present time." That leaves room for the yig cha's position that Ngalenu is a sentient being and yet, at the present time, is Śākyamuni. The mtha' dpyod states the own-system result in so many words: [ཁོ་ན་རེ། རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུས་ཆོས་ཅན། མ་འོངས་པ་ན་འཚང་རྒྱ་འགྱུར་གྱི་གང་ཟག་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། སེམས་ཅན་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། རྟགས་ཁས། འདོད་ན། དེ་མ་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། དེ་ད་ལྟ་སངས་རྒྱས་ཟིན་པའི་གང་ཟག་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ན་མ་ཁྱབ་བོ། །](quote:p395-q07). There Ngalenu is "a person who has already become a Buddha now" (ད་ལྟ་སངས་རྒྱས་ཟིན་པའི་གང་ཟག), while still being a sentient being.

**The second exchange.** The ཁ་ཅིག thesis did not come from nowhere. In the mtha' dpyod, Chökyi Gyaltsen reports it as the weapon of an unnamed "learned one" (མཁས་པ་ཁ་ཅིག). It is used against the view that the omniscience due to arise in the end-of-continuum bodhisattva's continuum is that bodhisattva's resultant refuge: [འདི་ལ་མཁས་པ་ཁ་ཅིག བྱང་སེམས་རྒྱུན་མཐའ་བའི་རྒྱུད་ལ་འབྱུང་རུང་གི་རྣམ་མཁྱེན་ཆོས་ཅན། ཁྱོད་བྱང་སེམས་རྒྱུན་མཐའ་བའི་དུས་ན་མེད་པར་ཐལ། ཁྱོད་དེའི་འབྲས་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p395-q10). The spyi don stages the same exchange at p391. There the yig cha grants that the future omniscience is the end-of-continuum bodhisattva's resultant refuge, and it denies that an omniscience "now arising" exists. So for a *particular* future omniscience, not existing at the bodhisattva's time is not the problem. What p395 refutes is the *pervasion*, the claim that this holds for every resultant-refuge Buddha. It does so with a pair defined by their contemporaneity: the Buddha and a path-of-accumulation bodhisattva living at the same time. The mtha' dpyod runs the argument to the end with "sentient being" in place of the bodhisattva: [ཁ་ཅིག གང་ཟག་དེའི་འབྲས་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་ན་གང་ཟག་དེའི་དུས་ན་མེད་པས་ཁྱབ་ཟེར་ན། སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་ཆོས་ཅན། སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་དང་དུས་མཉམ་དུ་གྱུར་པའི་སེམས་ཅན་གྱི་དུས་ན་མེད་པར་ཐལ། དེའི་འབྲས་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། དེའི་ཐོབ་བྱ་མཐར་ཐུག་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། མ་གྲུབ་ན། དེ་ཆོས་ཅན། སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་དེ་ཁྱོད་ཀྱི་ཐོབ་བྱ་མཐར་ཐུག་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། ཁྱོད་སེམས་ཅན་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། རྩ་བར་འདོད་ན། དེ་ཆོས་ཅན། དེའི་དུས་ན་ཡོད་པར་ཐལ། དེ་དང་དུས་མཉམ་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p395-q08). The reason holds because Buddhahood is every sentient being's final attainment. The consequence fails because whatever is contemporaneous with something exists at its time.

**The logic underneath.** Both replies depend on a feature of this yig cha that p392 makes explicit. Terms like སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ and བྱང་སེམས་ཚོགས་ལམ་པ are allowed to stand as general subjects, and relations such as "of one continuum" or "resultant refuge" are asserted of them as generals. p392 argues that *every* ārya Buddha is of one continuum with the path-of-accumulation bodhisattva, because every sentient being is. On that logic, "the Buddha" is the resultant refuge of "the bodhisattva" and exists alongside him, even though no particular bodhisattva's own enlightenment has yet occurred. The ཁ་ཅིག's pervasion fails because it was stated for any person and any resultant-refuge Buddha, and the general pair is a counter-instance to it. I think this is the right reading of why the counter-instance works, but it is my reading. The text does not spell it out.

**Panchen Sonam Drakpa.** His འགྲེལ་ཚིག་དོན་རབ་གསལ། is a word-gloss and does not run this debate. My searches for the Ngalenu example and the resultant-refuge pervasion found nothing in it, so the Loseling position was not located here.

**Sera Jey specificity.** The use of Ngalenu to show that Śākyamuni is a resultant refuge is common ground with Gomang (Jamyang Zhepa, above). The distinctively Chökyi Gyaltsen material is the pursuit of the temporal question, with "at the present time" (ད་ལྟར་གྱི་དུས་སུ) treated as a separate qualifier that a predicate may lack. In the searches I ran, those exchanges turned up only in his two first-chapter works, the spyi don and the mtha' dpyod.

## 5 · Bones of Contention 🦴

**What is disputed?** Two things. First, whether a predicate true of a person, "sentient being" or "Buddha," must be true of that person *at the present time*. Second, whether a person's resultant refuge must be absent at that person's time.

**Who argues what?**

- **The ཁོ་ན་རེ objector** holds that it must: Ngalenu, being a sentient being, is one now. That would make the yig cha's claim that Ngalenu is now Śākyamuni contradictory.
- **The ཁ་ཅིག of the second exchange** holds that a resultant refuge is always absent at its seeker's time. In the mtha' dpyod, that pervasion belongs to an unnamed མཁས་པ་ཁ་ཅིག, who aims it at the view that the end-of-continuum bodhisattva's future omniscience is his resultant refuge.
- **Chökyi Gyaltsen** denies both pervasions. He answers the first with the respondent's future Buddha and the second with the contemporaneous Buddha–bodhisattva pair.

**A tension inside the author's own corpus.** The reply to the first objection in the spyi don is almost exactly an argument that the mtha' dpyod *rejects*. There the same objection is raised and answered "no pervasion" (q05): [མ་ཁྱབ་བྱས་པ་ལ་ཁོ་ན་རེ། དེ་ཆོས་ཅན། ད་ལྟར་གྱི་དུས་སུ་སེམས་ཅན་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། སེམས་ཅན་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ན་མ་ཁྱབ། ཁོ་ན་རེ། དེ་ལ་ཁྱབ་པ་ཡོད་པར་ཐལ། སངས་རྒྱས་ཡིན་ན་ད་ལྟར་གྱི་དུས་སུ་སངས་རྒྱས་ཡིན་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཟེར་ན།](quote:p395-q05). Then comes a counter-argument of the same form as p395's, with an old dog in place of the respondent. It is introduced with ཁ་ཅིག and dismissed: [དེ་ལ་ཁ་ཅིག ཁྱི་རྒན་རྒྱ་བོ་སངས་རྒྱས་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་ཆོས་ཅན། ད་ལྟར་གྱི་དུས་སུ་སངས་རྒྱས་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། སངས་རྒྱས་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། འདོད་ན། ཁྱི་རྒན་རྒྱ་བོ་ད་ལྟ་སངས་རྒྱས་ཟིན་པར་ཐལ། འདོད་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཟེར་ན། མ་ཁྱབ་བོ། །](quote:p395-q06). In the mtha' dpyod, then, the inference from "the old dog's Buddha is a Buddha at the present time" to "the old dog has already become a Buddha now" is refused. In the spyi don, the same inference, from the respondent's Buddha to the respondent, is the whole force of the reply. This fits the yig cha's own p394, which holds that the respondent's Buddha *exists now*: an author who says that cannot easily treat "his Buddha is a Buddha now" as absurd. I can see three ways to read this and cannot choose between them on this evidence. (a) The two works record different stages of the author's handling. (b) The spyi don reply works only as an ad hominem against an opponent who already grants the move. (c) As the ཀ witness's འདོད་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཟེར། might suggest, the spyi don reply is itself reported speech rather than the author's answer. The tension is real in the texts as they stand, and a reader should not take the spyi don's first reply as settled own-system doctrine without seeing the mtha' dpyod.

**Why does it matter?** The yig cha's presentation of refuge depends on being able to say that one and the same Buddha is cause for others and result for me, and that my resultant refuge is available now as an object of refuge. If resultant refuges were always future and absent, going for refuge to one's own future Buddhahood would have no present object. Then p394's claim that the respondent's ultimate resultant refuge, the three jewels, exists now would collapse. The dispute over tense is how the curriculum makes the Mahāyāna resultant refuge coherent.

**Resolution.** On the second question, Sera Jey's position is clear and argued in both of Chökyi Gyaltsen's works. A resultant refuge need not be absent at its seeker's time, as the contemporaneous Buddha and bodhisattva show. On the first question, the rejection of the objector's pervasion is also clear in both works: being a sentient being does not make Ngalenu one now. But the two works justify that rejection differently, and on the old-dog/respondent argument they disagree. That disagreement is reported above, not resolved.

## 6 · Key Terminology & Technical Concepts

- **འབྲས་སྐྱབས། / འབྲས་བུའི་སྐྱབས།** — resultant refuge.
  - **How understood here:** the Buddha (or three jewels) that a given person is to attain in their own continuum, their ཐོབ་བྱ་མཐར་ཐུག. It is contrasted with the causal refuge (རྒྱུའི་སྐྱབས), a Buddha already accomplished. The definition comes from Gyaltsab Je via p390. The paragraph's second exchange is about whether "to be attained" implies "not yet existing."
  - **Debates:** whether causal and resultant refuge are incompatible (Chökyi Gyaltsen and Jamyang Zhepa both say not, citing Śākyamuni). Also whether a particular future omniscience, or only the general Buddha, can be a present resultant refuge (p391 and the mtha' dpyod's མཁས་པ་ཁ་ཅིག).

- **ད་ལྟར་གྱི་དུས་སུ།** — "at the present time."
  - **How understood here:** a qualifier that does not follow automatically from an unqualified predicate. "Ngalenu is a sentient being" is true, and "Ngalenu is, at the present time, a sentient being" is false, because at the present time he is Śākyamuni. The paragraph's first exchange is a lesson in keeping the qualified and unqualified predications apart.
  - **Debates:** see §5. Whether "X's Buddha is a Buddha at the present time" entails "X has become a Buddha at the present time" is answered differently in the spyi don and the mtha' dpyod.

- **སངས་རྒྱས་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས།** — "the Buddha that [X] becomes when X attains Buddhahood."
  - **How understood here:** a device for referring to a particular person's future Buddhahood as a subject in its own right. It counts as an ārya Buddha, which is what lets it break the objector's pervasion.

- **དུས་མཉམ།** — contemporaneous.
  - **How understood here:** the relation that defeats the second ཁ་ཅིག. Whatever is contemporaneous with a person exists at that person's time, and the Buddha contemporaneous with a path-of-accumulation bodhisattva is still that bodhisattva's resultant refuge. So being a resultant refuge and existing at the seeker's time are compatible.

- **རྒྱུད་གཅིག།** — of one continuum.
  - **How understood here:** not used in p395 itself, but it is the background of both exchanges (p392–p394). In this yig cha it is predicated of general subjects: every ārya Buddha is of one continuum with the path-of-accumulation bodhisattva. That is why "the Buddha" can serve as a present-tense counter-instance without naming any particular enlightened person.
