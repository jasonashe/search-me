# No Buddha of another continuum: the cause refuge of the path-of-accumulation bodhisattva

## 1 · Passage & Context

**Source Text:**

> ཁ་ཅིག ། བྱང་སེམས་ཚོགས་ལམ་པ་དང་རྒྱུད་ཐ་དད་དུ་གྱུར་པའི་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་པོ་དེ་དེའི་རྒྱུའི་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་ཟེར་བ་མི་འཐད་དེ། དེ་དང་རྒྱུད་ཐ་དད་དུ་གྱུར་པའི་དེ་མེད་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། དེ་འདྲའི་སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་མེད་པའི་ཕྱིར། སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་ཡིན་ན། དེ་དང་རྒྱུད་གཅིག་ཡིན་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། བྱང་སེམས་ཚོགས་ལམ་པ་དེ་དང་རྒྱུད་གཅིག་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། བྱང་སེམས་ཚོགས་ལམ་པ་ཡིན་ན། དེ་དང་རྒྱུད་གཅིག་ཡིན་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། སེམས་ཅན་ཡིན་ན་དེ་ཡིན་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར།

**Preceding Section:** p391 is also about cause and result refuge, but approaches it through time. A ཁ་ཅིག says the omniscient mind that will arise in the continuum of a bodhisattva at the end of the continuum (རྒྱུན་མཐའ་བ) is that bodhisattva's result refuge. A second ཁ་ཅིག objects that a result refuge would then have to be absent at the time of the one it is the refuge for. That objection is argued through the seed and the sprout: is the sprout that is arising from the seed present at the time of the seed? The seed ceasing and the sprout arising are simultaneous. The text's resolution is that there is no such thing as "the sprout arising from the seed" taken as an existent. So the omniscient mind that *will arise* in the end-of-continuum bodhisattva is his result refuge, and the omniscient mind *arising* in his continuum is not, because nothing of that description exists. Behind both paragraphs stands p390, the positive statement of the section. There the Ratnagotravibhāga's refuge verse is glossed. For those who have entered the Mahāyāna path, the Buddha they are to attain is the result refuge, and the Buddha already accomplished in another's continuum is the cause refuge.

**Following Section:** p393 opens with ཁོ་ན་རེ and turns the paragraph's final reason against it. If every sentient being is of one continuum with the Buddha, then a śrāvaka arhat is too. Then that Buddha would be a person preceded by a śrāvaka arhat's realization. Chökyi Gyaltsen answers "not pervaded." Indra, lord of the gods, would then be certain to attain Śākyamuni's state, because Śākyamuni is of one continuum with him: Śākyamuni once took birth as Indra. The debate then turns to King Māndhātṛ (རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུ), Śākyamuni in a former life, and whether he is Śākyamuni "now." p394–395 carry the same question into whether the Buddha of one continuum with "the person answering in front of us" (མདུན་གྱི་སྐྱེས་བུ་ལན་འདེབས་པ་པོ) exists now. The own-system presentation of conventional and ultimate refuge begins at p396.

**What this paragraph is doing.** The opponent tries to make p390's "the Buddha already accomplished in another's continuum" more precise. He says the cause refuge of a path-of-accumulation bodhisattva is the Three Jewels that are *of a different continuum* (རྒྱུད་ཐ་དད) from that bodhisattva. Chökyi Gyaltsen rejects the refinement, and not because the cause refuge must lie in the bodhisattva's own continuum. His objection is that the qualifier picks out nothing. There is no Buddha of a different continuum from the path-of-accumulation bodhisattva, because every Buddha is of one continuum with it. The paragraph is a lesson in how a relational predicate behaves when its terms are general, and the next three paragraphs work through what follows from it.

**On the pronouns.** The paragraph runs on དེ, and the referent changes twice. In དེ་དེའི་རྒྱུའི་སྐྱབས the first དེ is the Three Jewels and the second is the bodhisattva. In the reasons, དེ་དང་རྒྱུད་ཐ་དད / དེ་དང་རྒྱུད་གཅིག is "of a different / one continuum with the bodhisattva" as far as 201–263. From 263 onward it is "with the Buddha." The last དེ་ཡིན་དགོས means "must be of one continuum with the Buddha." The mtha' dpyod version of the same argument (§4) writes the terms out in full and confirms this reading.

**On the unstated step.** The chain reaches its first reason (201–263: every Buddha is of one continuum with the bodhisattva) through the converse (263–313: the bodhisattva is of one continuum with the Buddha). The paragraph treats "one continuum with" as symmetric, which it is. But the converse is proved only at the level of the kind: the path-of-accumulation bodhisattva, and indeed every sentient being, will become *a* Buddha. The step from that to "*whatever* is a Buddha is of one continuum with it" holds only if the bodhisattva is also taken as a kind, as "the path-of-accumulation bodhisattva" rather than this one. The paragraph does not say so. Its companion text does (§4, and §5 on why this matters).

## 3 · Structural Context (Dkar Chag)

- **Abhisamayālaṃkāra location:** chapter 1, the ten instructions (གདམས་ངག), at the line [སངས་རྒྱས་ལ་སོགས་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ། ། མ་ཞེན་ཡོངས་སུ་མི་ངལ་དང་། །](quote:p392-q13). The second of the ten is the instruction on the Three Jewels. The yig cha hangs a long refuge excursus on it (p376 onwards), of which this paragraph is a small part.
- **Haribhadra's commentary location:** at the same lemma, the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ། takes the instruction on the Three Jewels as an instruction to *know them without apprehending*: [སངས་རྒྱས་ལ་སོགས་སངས་རྒྱས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་དག་མཚན་ཉིད་ཀྱིས་སངས་རྒྱས་སུ་བྱེད་པའི་ཆོས་ཀྱི་མཚན་ཉིད་རྣམ་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པ་ཉིད་མི་དམིགས་པ་ལ…](quote:p392-q12). He then passes to the Dharma Jewel and to the twenty-fold Saṅgha. Reading the passage and a page either side, Haribhadra says nothing about cause and result refuge, nothing about the Ratnagotravibhāga's refuge verse, and nothing about whose continuum a Jewel is in. The cause/result distinction reaches this part of the Ornament curriculum from the Ratnagotravibhāga through the Tibetan commentaries (Ngok's school, then Gyaltsab Je). The dispute in this paragraph, over whether a Buddha can be "of a different continuum," has no Indian counterpart at this lemma at all.
- **Text location:** སྐབས་དང་པོའི་སྤྱི་དོན།, in the section attached to the lemma སྐྱབས་གསུམ་ལ་གདམས་པ། སངས་རྒྱས་ལ་སོགས་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ། (opening at p376), under གསུམ་པ་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་བཤད་པ (from p383). Within that it falls under ༢༽ སྐྱབས་སྤྱིའི་རྣམ་གཞག་བཤད་པ (from p388). That heading is Gyaltsab Je's own: the རྣམ་བཤད་སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན། carries a section titled སྐྱབས་སྤྱིའི་རྣམ་གཞག་བཤད་པ at the corresponding place. The own-system sentence in p390 follows Gyaltsab's closely (§4). This paragraph belongs to the run of objections (p391–p395) that follows that own-system statement. The pack's outline does not show a separate heading for that run, and I have not found one. So "a string of mtha' dpyod-style debates appended to p390" is my description, not a heading of the text.
- **Section type:** refutation of another's position (ཁ་ཅིག … ཟེར་བ་མི་འཐད་དེ), proved by a chain of four nested reasons with no ཐལ་འགྱུར. It is closer to a positive demonstration than to a consequence.
- **Argumentative role:** it guards the own-system formulation in p390 against a tempting paraphrase. "Accomplished in another's continuum" (གཞན་རྒྱུད་ལ་གྲུབ་ཟིན) is said of particular Buddhas already accomplished. "Of a different continuum from the bodhisattva" (རྒྱུད་ཐ་དད་དུ་གྱུར་པ) is a relation between kinds. The paragraph shows that the second is empty. It also lays down the premise (every sentient being is of one continuum with the Buddha) that p393–p395 go on to test.

## 4 · Philosophical Exposition & Commentary

**Core meaning.** The opponent's thesis sounds almost like a paraphrase of this text's own p390. There the cause refuge of those on the Mahāyāna path is [གཞན་རྒྱུད་ལ་གྲུབ་ཟིན་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་རྒྱུའི་སྐྱབས](quote:p392-q07): the Buddha already accomplished in another's continuum. Put "accomplished in another's continuum" as "of a different continuum from the bodhisattva," and you have the opponent. Chökyi Gyaltsen denies that the second is a harmless restatement. The phrase "Three Jewels of a different continuum from the path-of-accumulation bodhisattva" names nothing. Every Buddha has come up through the path of accumulation, and every path-of-accumulation bodhisattva, like every sentient being, will become a Buddha. So at the level at which the opponent is speaking, of the path-of-accumulation bodhisattva and the Buddha as kinds, the two are of one continuum and never of different ones.

**The mtha' dpyod version.** Chökyi Gyaltsen's own སྐབས་དང་པོའི་མཐའ་དཔྱོད། runs the same debate at the same place, and runs it more fully. First, it gives the opponent's whole position, of which the spyi don quotes only half: [ཁ་ཅིག བྱང་སེམས་ཚོགས་ལམ་པ་དང་རྒྱུད་ཐ་དད་དུ་གྱུར་པའི་གང་ཟག་གཞན་རྒྱུད་ལ་གྲུབ་ཟིན་གྱི་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་བྱང་སེམས་ཚོགས་ལམ་པའི་རྒྱུའི་སྐྱབས་ཡིན། བྱང་སེམས་ཚོགས་ལམ་པའི་ཐོབ་བྱར་གྱུར་པའི་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ། བྱང་སེམས་ཚོགས་ལམ་པའི་འབྲས་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་ཟེར་ན།](quote:p392-q01). The opponent was offering a pair of definitions. The cause refuge is the Jewels in a person of another continuum; the result refuge is the Jewels to be attained. The two are distinguished by continuum. The mtha' dpyod opponent also says "*the person* of a different continuum," which the spyi don drops.

Second, it proves the key step by a different reason: [དེར་ཐལ། སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་ཡིན་ན་བྱང་སེམས་ཚོགས་ལམ་པ་དང་རྒྱུད་གཅིག་ཡིན་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར། དེ་ཡིན་ན་བྱང་སེམས་ཚོགས་ལམ་པའི་རྟོགས་པ་སྔོན་སོང་གི་གང་ཟག་ཡིན་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p392-q02). Every Buddha is of one continuum with the path-of-accumulation bodhisattva because every Buddha is a person *preceded by that bodhisattva's realization*. This reason runs from the Buddha's side and settles the pervasion "whatever is a Buddha…" directly. The spyi don instead argues from the bodhisattva's side, through sentient beings. Its reason is correct but takes the long way round, which is why §2 had to supply a step.

The mtha' dpyod also runs the converse, and grounds it in the relation of seeker to goal: [འདོད་མི་ནུས་ཏེ། བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡིན་ན་སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་དེ་རང་ཉིད་དང་རྒྱུད་གཅིག་ཡིན་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། དེ་ཡིན་ན་སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་དེ་རང་ཉིད་ཀྱི་ཐོབ་བྱ་མཐར་ཐུག་ཡིན་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p392-q03). Whatever is a bodhisattva is of one continuum with the Buddha, because the Buddha is its own final attainment (ཐོབ་བྱ་མཐར་ཐུག). This shows what "one continuum" is doing in the argument. It is the relation that makes a Buddha someone's *result* refuge. So the opponent's scheme undoes itself: the relation he uses to set the cause refuge apart from the result refuge is the one that joins every Buddha to the bodhisattva.

**The last reason, and its ground.** The spyi don closes on སེམས་ཅན་ཡིན་ན་དེ་ཡིན་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར།: whatever is a sentient being is of one continuum with the Buddha. In the mtha' dpyod this proposition is not Chökyi Gyaltsen's reason. It is an opponent's attempted absurdity: [ཁ་ཅིག སེམས་ཅན་ཡིན་ན་སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་དང་རྒྱུད་གཅིག་ཡིན་དགོས་པར་ཐལ། བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡིན་ན་དེ་དང་རྒྱུད་གཅིག་ཡིན་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར། རྟགས་ཁས། འདོད་ན། སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་ཆོས་ཅན། ཁྱོད་ཉན་ཐོས་དགྲ་བཅོམ་པའི་རྟོགས་པ་སྔོན་དུ་སོང་བའི་སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། … མ་ཁྱབ་སྟེ།](quote:p392-q04). Chökyi Gyaltsen accepts it ("the reason is conceded… if you accept") and blocks only the next consequence, with the Indra example. The spyi don has simply promoted the accepted consequence into a premise, and p393 is that same rejoinder put back into the mouth of a ཁོ་ན་རེ. The two texts agree on substance; what differs is who says the line.

The spyi don gives no ground for "every sentient being will become a Buddha." Chökyi Gyaltsen gives one in his second-chapter mtha' dpyod: [ཁྱོད་སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པར་འགྱུར་བའི་དུས་སྲིད་པར་ཐལ། ཁྱོད་སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པར་འགྱུར་རུང་གི་རིགས་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། རྟགས་གྲུབ་སྟེ། དེར་འགྱུར་རུང་གི་རིགས་གཅིག་ཡོད་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p392-q05). There is a time when all sentient beings become Buddhas, because they are of a type (རིགས) that can become one, and such a type exists. Behind that stands the Ratnagotravibhāga, whose words are Maitreya's, not the commentators': [རིགས་ཡོད་ཕྱིར་ན་ལུས་ཅན་ཀུན། །རྟག་ཏུ་སངས་རྒྱས་སྙིང་པོ་ཅན།](quote:p392-q06), "because the lineage exists, all embodied beings always have the buddha-nature." The paragraph does not cite the verse. I give it as the canonical ground of the premise the paragraph assumes, not as something the paragraph quotes.

**Where the cause/result pair comes from.** The pair is the Tibetan reading of the Ratnagotravibhāga's refuge verse, which in the Tengyur reads [སྟོན་པ་བསྟན་པ་བསླབ་དོན་གྱིས། །ཐེག་པ་གསུམ་དང་བྱེད་གསུམ་ལ། །མོས་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་དབང་བྱས་ནས། །སྐྱབས་གསུམ་རྣམ་པར་བཞག་པ་ཡིན། །](quote:p392-q09). (The yig cha at p390 has སློབ་དོན for the canon's བསླབ་དོན; Gyaltsab Je has སློབ་དོན too. The difference does not bear on the argument.) Gyaltsab Je's reading, which p390 follows, is: [ཐེག་པ་ཆེན་པོའི་རིགས་ཅན་ལམ་ལ་ཞུགས་པ་དེ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་རང་རྒྱུད་ལ་ཐོབ་བྱའི་སངས་རྒྱས་འབྲས་བུའི་སྐྱབས་དང་། གྲུབ་ཟིན་གྱི་སངས་རྒྱས་རྒྱུའི་སྐྱབས་སུ་ཤེས་པར་བྱ་བ་དང་།](quote:p392-q08). Gyaltsab speaks of a Buddha "to be attained in one's own continuum" against a Buddha "already accomplished." He sets the pair apart by *time*, already accomplished against yet to be attained, and does not use continuum for the cause refuge at all. Chökyi Gyaltsen's p390 adds གཞན་རྒྱུད་ལ་, "in another's continuum." That addition is what gives the opponent his opening, and this paragraph is Chökyi Gyaltsen closing it.

The "other continuum" wording is much older than either of them. The commentary catalogued here under Ngok Lotsawa (it reports "the great translator" in the third person, so its exact authorship is uncertain) uses it as a standard term: [བསོད་ནམས་ཆ་མཐུན་གྱི་དགེ་བ་ཙམ་དང་དུ་ལེན་ཅིང་མངོན་པར་མཐོ་བའི་གོ་འཕང་དོན་དུ་གཉེར་བ་དག་ནི། གཞན་རྒྱུད་ལ་གྲུབ་ཟིན་གྱི་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་ལ་སྐྱབས་སུ་འགྲོ་ལ།](quote:p392-q10). There it is set against "the Three Jewels that can arise in one's own continuum in the future," the refuge of those seeking liberation. In that early scheme "another's continuum" against "one's own continuum" is the whole point of the division. The opponent of p392 is not inventing a formulation; he is keeping to an old one. That is why the refutation is worth making.

**Jamyang Zhepa (Gomang).** The ཕར་ཕྱིན་མཐའ་དཔྱོད་རིན་ཆེན་སྒྲོན་མེ། uses the same stock figures as p393–p395, Śākyamuni and his former lives Khye'u Nangje and Māndhātṛ, to make a different point. It argues that one and the same Buddha is both kinds of refuge: [འདོད་ན། སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་ཆོས་ཅན། རྒྱུ་སྐྱབས་མ་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། འབྲས་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། … འདོད་མི་ནུས་ཏེ། རང་གི་གདུལ་བྱ་ཐེག་པ་གསུམ་གྱི་གང་ཟག་གི་རྒྱུ་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p392-q11). Śākyamuni is the result refuge of his own former lives and the cause refuge of his disciples. This does not contradict p392. It shows the Gomang textbook making the non-exclusivity of the two refuges its main point, whereas Sera Jey argues about how "one continuum" behaves with general terms. I did not find Jamyang Zhepa discussing "of a different continuum from the path-of-accumulation bodhisattva" as such.

**Tsongkhapa and Panchen Sonam Drakpa.** At this lemma the ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་ཕྲེང་ turns straight to the twenty-fold Saṅgha. I did not find a cause/result refuge discussion there. It quotes the Ratnagotravibhāga refuge verse elsewhere (the verse was located in the gser phreng at a different place), but I have not read that passage closely enough to report what it does with it. A search of Panchen Sonam Drakpa's འགྲེལ་ཚིག་དོན་རབ་གསལ། for the verse found nothing. So the Loseling comparison cannot be made from the sources checked here.

**Sera Jey specificity.** The conclusion is not sectarian: nobody holds that the cause refuge must be in one's own continuum. What is distinctive is the method. Chökyi Gyaltsen takes a doctrinal paraphrase and tests it as a claim about kinds. He finds that "of a different continuum from X", with X general, cannot be satisfied by any Buddha. He then spends three more paragraphs (p393–p395) on the puzzles his own premise raises: śrāvaka arhats, Indra, Māndhātṛ, and the Buddha who is "of one continuum with the person in front of us." The student is being trained to see that "one continuum with" behaves differently when its terms are particular and when they are general.

## 5 · Bones of Contention 🦴

**What is disputed?** Whether the cause refuge of a Mahāyāna practitioner can be defined as the Three Jewels *of a different continuum from* that practitioner, with the result refuge defined as the Three Jewels to be attained.

**Who argues what?**

- **The ཁ་ཅིག:** yes. The cause refuge is the Three Jewels in a person of a different continuum; the result refuge is the Jewels to be attained. He has old support in the wording: the text catalogued under Ngok already divides refuges by "another's continuum" against "one's own" ([quote](quote:p392-q10)). This yig cha's own p390 says "accomplished in another's continuum" ([quote](quote:p392-q07)).
- **Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen:** no. Taken as a relation between the path-of-accumulation bodhisattva and the Buddha, "of a different continuum" is empty. The spyi don proves this from the bodhisattva's side, through the buddha-nature of all sentient beings. The mtha' dpyod proves it from the Buddha's side: every Buddha was preceded by that bodhisattva's realization ([quote](quote:p392-q02)).
- **Gyaltsab Je:** does not take a side in the debate, but his formulation avoids it. He distinguishes the refuges as "already accomplished" against "to be attained in one's own continuum," with no continuum qualifier on the cause refuge ([quote](quote:p392-q08)).

**The weak point in the refutation.** The paragraph's own chain moves from "the bodhisattva (as a kind) is of one continuum with the Buddha" to "every Buddha is of one continuum with the bodhisattva." That step holds only if "the path-of-accumulation bodhisattva" is read as a kind and "one continuum" is satisfied by *some* instance of each. On that reading the argument works. It is the reading p393 immediately needs: Indra is "of one continuum with Śākyamuni," yet not every Indra will become Śākyamuni. The opponent could reply that he meant a particular bodhisattva and a particular Buddha, say Śākyamuni and one disciple. Of that pair, "different continuum" is plainly true. The spyi don does not address that reply. The mtha' dpyod's version, which puts the opponent's position as "*the person* of a different continuum" and argues through "preceded by the bodhisattva's realization," is the stronger text. A student defending the spyi don should reach for it. So the refutation is not of the idea that a Buddha outside oneself is a cause refuge, which p390 affirms. It is of that idea stated through a relation to a *kind* of person.

**Why does it matter?** The point concerns which relation separates the two refuges. If continuum separated them, every result refuge would be in one's own continuum and every cause refuge in another's. Then the same Buddha could not be both, which Jamyang Zhepa ([quote](quote:p392-q11)) and this text (p394, on Māndhātṛ and Śākyamuni) both affirm he can be. The mtha' dpyod also shows that "one continuum" is how the tradition expresses the relation of a practitioner to his *final attainment* ([quote](quote:p392-q03)). So the result refuge is defined through that relation, and the cause refuge cannot be defined as its negation.

**Resolution.** Sera Jey keeps p390's "already accomplished in another's continuum" and rejects "of a different continuum from the bodhisattva." The first speaks of particular Buddhas who are already accomplished. The second is a relation between kinds, and between these kinds it never holds, because every sentient being will become a Buddha (the ground Chökyi Gyaltsen gives elsewhere through རིགས, [quote](quote:p392-q05)). What separates the two refuges is whether the Jewels are already accomplished or yet to be attained, not whose continuum they belong to.

**A note on the evidence.** The case rests almost entirely on tier 1: Chökyi Gyaltsen's own mtha' dpyod, which runs the identical debate. Gyaltsab Je is the only other Gelug witness to the formulation, and he does not engage the debate. The Indian commentaries are silent, and so is the pack at tiers 3 and 4. That silence is a finding. The question is a Tibetan scholastic refinement of the Ratnagotravibhāga's refuge classification, pursued inside the Sera Jey mtha' dpyod literature.

## 6 · Key Terminology & Technical Concepts

- **རྒྱུད་གཅིག / རྒྱུད་ཐ་དད་དུ་གྱུར་པ།** — of one continuum / of different continua.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *ekasaṃtāna* / *bhinnasaṃtāna* (the Tibetan pair is what matters here).
  - **How understood here:** when the terms are particular persons, "one continuum" is plain: Śākyamuni and his past life as Indra. When the terms are kinds, as with the path-of-accumulation bodhisattva and the Buddha, it holds if some member of the one is in the same stream as some member of the other. The paragraph's whole force depends on the second reading; p393's Indra example makes it explicit.
  - **Debates:** whether the spyi don's chain is valid without that reading (§5). In the mtha' dpyod, "one continuum" also expresses the relation of a practitioner to his final attainment ([quote](quote:p392-q03)).

- **རྒྱུའི་སྐྱབས་ / འབྲས་བུའི་སྐྱབས།** — cause refuge / result refuge.
  - **How understood here:** the cause refuge is the Three Jewels already accomplished, whose example one follows. The result refuge is the Three Jewels one will oneself attain. Gyaltsab Je distinguishes them by "already accomplished" against "to be attained" ([quote](quote:p392-q08)). This text's p390 adds "in another's continuum" to the cause refuge, and this paragraph establishes that the addition must not be read as "of a different continuum."
  - **Debates:** the older formulation in the text catalogued under Ngok splits refuges by another's against one's own continuum, and assigns them to different kinds of practitioner ([quote](quote:p392-q10)). Jamyang Zhepa stresses that one Buddha can be both refuges to different people ([quote](quote:p392-q11)).

- **གཞན་རྒྱུད་ལ་གྲུབ་ཟིན།** — already accomplished in another's continuum.
  - **How understood here:** a description of the particular Buddhas who serve as cause refuge: Śākyamuni is already accomplished, and not in the practitioner's own stream. It is not a relation to a kind. The paragraph's point is that this description cannot be turned into "of a different continuum from the path-of-accumulation bodhisattva."

- **རྟོགས་པ་སྔོན་དུ་སོང་བ།** — preceded by the realization of (a given kind of practitioner).
  - **How understood here:** the mtha' dpyod's criterion for a Buddha's being of one continuum with a kind of practitioner ([quote](quote:p392-q02)). Every Buddha was preceded by the realization of a path-of-accumulation bodhisattva. Not every Buddha was preceded by a śrāvaka arhat's realization, which is why p393's objection needs the answer "not pervaded."

- **རིགས།** — lineage, type; here the buddha-lineage.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *gotra*.
  - **How understood here:** the unstated ground of the final reason, that every sentient being is of one continuum with the Buddha. Chökyi Gyaltsen grounds "all beings will become Buddhas" in the existence of a type that can become one ([quote](quote:p392-q05)). The Ratnagotravibhāga grounds all beings' buddha-nature in the existence of the lineage ([quote](quote:p392-q06)).
