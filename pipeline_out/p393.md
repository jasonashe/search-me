# Indra, Māndhātṛ and the Teacher — what "one continuum" does and does not carry

## 1 · Passage & Context

**Source Text:**

> ཁོ་ན་རེ། ཉན་ཐོས་དགྲ་བཅོམ་པ་ཆོས་ཅན། དེར་ཐལ། དེའི་ཕྱིར། འདོད་ན། སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་དེ་དང་རྒྱུད་གཅིག་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ་ལོ། ། འདོད་ན། དེ་ཆོས་ཅན༑ ཉན་ཐོས་དགྲ་བཅོམ་པའི་རྟོགས་པ་སྔོན་དུ་སོང་བའི་གང་ཟག་ཏུ་ཐལ། དེ་དང་རྒྱུད་གཅིག་ཏུ་གྱུར་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ན་མ་ཁྱབ་སྟེ། ལྷའི་དབང་པོ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་ཆོས་ཅན། སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པའི་གོ་འཕང་མངོན་དུ་བྱེད་ངེས་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། དེ་དང་རྒྱུད་གཅིག་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། ཁྱབ་པ་ཁས། དེར་ཐལ། སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་དེ་དང་རྒྱུད་གཅིག་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། དེ་སྔོན་ལྷའི་དབང་པོ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་གྱི་སྐྱེ་བ་བླངས་མྱོང་བའི་ཕྱིར། རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུས་དེ་ད་ལྟར་གྱི་དུས་སུ་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་ཡིན་བྱས་པ་ལ། ཁ་ཅིག ། དེ་ཆོས་ཅན། སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ༑ ད་ལྟར་གྱི་དུས་སུ་དེ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ན་མ་ཁྱབ། དམ་བཅའ་དེར་ཐལ། དེ་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པའི་དུས་སུ་དེ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། དེ་དེའི་དུས་སུ་དེར་གྱུར་པའི་ཕྱིར།

**Preceding Section:** p392 (page 282) refutes a ཁ་ཅིག who holds that the Three Jewels in a continuum *other* than that of a bodhisattva on the path of accumulation are that bodhisattva's cause refuge. Chökyi Gyaltsen answers that there are no such Three Jewels, because there is no Buddha superior of a different continuum from that bodhisattva. The pervasions he gives are the ones this paragraph attacks. Whatever is a Buddha superior must be of one continuum with the bodhisattva on the path of accumulation, because that bodhisattva is of one continuum with it. Whatever is a bodhisattva on the path of accumulation must be of one continuum with a Buddha superior, because **whatever is a sentient being must be so** (སེམས་ཅན་ཡིན་ན་དེ་ཡིན་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར།).

**Following Section:** p394 (page 283) continues the ཁ་ཅིག's pressure on the Māndhātṛ claim. It argues that if Māndhātṛ is Śākyamuni at Śākyamuni's time, then Śākyamuni is Māndhātṛ at Māndhātṛ's time. In that case Śākyamuni, at that time, would be the cause of Śākyamuni, which is impossible because at that time Śākyamuni is Māndhātṛ's cause. The argument closes with ཞེས་ཟེར and no answer. A new ཁ་ཅིག then says that the Buddha superior of one continuum with the respondent does not exist now, and is refuted: it does exist now, because the Three Jewels that are the respondent's final result refuge exist now.

**What this paragraph is doing:** It defends p392's claim that every sentient being is "of one continuum" with Buddha superior against two attempts to make the relation absurd. The first attempt comes through the śrāvaka arhat: if the arhat is of one continuum with Buddha superior, then Buddha superior must have the arhat's realization in its past. Chökyi Gyaltsen denies the pervasion and gives a counter-instance, Indra, of one continuum with the Teacher. The second attempt comes through King Māndhātṛ: if Māndhātṛ is Śākyamuni "at the present time", he must simply be Śākyamuni. Chökyi Gyaltsen again denies the pervasion. Both answers rest on one principle: a relation stated between general terms, or indexed to a time, does not hand over the properties of the particular individual or moment it was derived from.

## 3 · Structural Context (Dkar Chag)

- **Abhisamayālaṃkāra location:** chapter one, the ten instructions (གདམས་ངག་བཅུ). The lemma is the second line of [སྒྲུབ་དང་བདེན་པ་རྣམས་དང་ནི། ། སངས་རྒྱས་ལ་སོགས་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ། ། མ་ཞེན་ཡོངས་སུ་མི་ངལ་དང་། ། ལམ་ནི་ཡོངས་སུ་འཛིན་པ་དང་། །](quote:p393-q15). The Ornament names the Three Jewels as one of the ten things a bodhisattva is instructed in, and says nothing more about them.
- **Haribhadra's commentary location:** the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ glosses the same line at the same place. It reads the instruction on the Buddha jewel as an instruction in non-apprehension: [སངས་རྒྱས་ལ་སོགས་སངས་རྒྱས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་དག་མཚན་ཉིད་ཀྱིས་སངས་རྒྱས་སུ་བྱེད་པའི་ཆོས་ཀྱི་མཚན་ཉིད་རྣམ་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པ་ཉིད་མི་དམིགས་པ](quote:p393-q14). It then turns to the Saṅgha jewel as the twenty kinds of irreversible noble bodhisattvas. Having read the passage in full, I found nothing in it about cause and result refuges, about whose continuum a Buddha belongs to, or about jātaka identities. Gyaltsab Je's རྣམ་བཤད་སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན at the lemma sorts the ten instructions into support and supported, and Tsongkhapa's ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་ཕྲེང there moves directly to the divisions of the Saṅgha. Neither raises the question either. Everything from p388 to p395 is a Tibetan debate with no Indian commentarial counterpart at this lemma.
- **Text location:** སྐབས་དང་པོའི་སྤྱི་དོན, under the heading སྐྱབས་གསུམ་ལ་གདམས་པ (opening at p376, page 273), within གསུམ་པ་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་བཤད་པ (from p383), in the sub-section ༢༽ སྐྱབས་སྤྱིའི་རྣམ་གཞག་བཤད་པ (from p388, page 280). Inside that sub-section, p390 gives the positive account from the Uttaratantra. p391–p395 then argue over its central distinction between cause refuge and result refuge.
- **Section type:** objection and response. A ཁོ་ན་རེ attacks the author's own pervasion and is answered by a counter-instance. A second exchange follows, opened by a claim of the author's (in the printed reading) and a ཁ་ཅིག's challenge to it.
- **Argumentative role:** p392 needed a strong pervasion, that every sentient being is of one continuum with Buddha superior, to show that no Buddha is "of a different continuum" from a bodhisattva. This paragraph protects that pervasion by fixing what "of one continuum" does *not* license. It does not license transferring a particular individual's history to the general term (the arhat and the Indra exchange). A time-indexed identity does not license an unindexed one (the Māndhātṛ exchange). Without these limits, the p390 definition of the result refuge as the Buddha "to be attained in one's own continuum" would collapse into absurdity.

## 4 · Philosophical Exposition & Commentary

**Core meaning.** The distinction behind the whole exchange is set out two paragraphs earlier, in this text's own explanation of the Uttaratantra verse: [ཐེག་ཆེན་ལམ་དུ་ཞུགས་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་རང་གི་ཐོབ་བྱའི་སངས་རྒྱས་འབྲས་བུའི་སྐྱབས་དང་། གཞན་རྒྱུད་ལ་གྲུབ་ཟིན་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་རྒྱུའི་སྐྱབས་དང་།](quote:p393-q01). For those who have entered the Great Vehicle, the Buddha they are themselves to attain is the result refuge, and the Buddha already accomplished in another's continuum is the cause refuge. So the result refuge is defined by continuum: it is *my* Buddhahood. p392 then generalises: whatever is a sentient being is of one continuum with Buddha superior. The claim is that every sentient being will one day be a Buddha, and in that sense "Buddha superior" stands in the same-continuum relation to every one of them.

In his own མཐའ་དཔྱོད, Chökyi Gyaltsen gives the ground for this relation in the same refutation that p392 gives here: [བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡིན་ན་སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་དེ་རང་ཉིད་དང་རྒྱུད་གཅིག་ཡིན་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། དེ་ཡིན་ན་སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་དེ་རང་ཉིད་ཀྱི་ཐོབ་བྱ་མཐར་ཐུག་ཡིན་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p393-q02). Buddha superior is of one continuum with every bodhisattva because it is each bodhisattva's final object of attainment. The subject of this relation is the *general* term "Buddha superior". The paragraph is about what that choice of subject commits one to.

**The first exchange.** The opponent's move is natural. The arhat is a sentient being, so Buddha superior is of one continuum with it. Whatever is of one continuum with the arhat and later than it has the arhat's realization in its past. So Buddha superior is "a person preceded by the realization of a śrāvaka arhat". That is false of Buddha superior as such. The arhat in question will indeed become a Buddha with such a past, but Buddha superior in general has no such history. The pervasion fails because the same-continuum relation holds of the general term only through *some* individual under it, and the history belongs to that individual.

The parallel text in the མཐའ་དཔྱོད makes the general subject explicit, where the སྤྱི་དོན only says དེ: [འདོད་ན། སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་ཆོས་ཅན། ཁྱོད་ཉན་ཐོས་དགྲ་བཅོམ་པའི་རྟོགས་པ་སྔོན་དུ་སོང་བའི་སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། དེ་དང་རྒྱུད་གཅིག་ཏུ་གྱུར་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ན། མ་ཁྱབ་སྟེ། ལྷའི་དབང་པོ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་ཆོས་ཅན། ཁྱོད་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པའི་གོ་འཕང་མངོན་དུ་བྱེད་ངེས་ཀྱི་གང་ཟག་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། ཁྱོད་དེ་དང་རྒྱུད་གཅིག་ཏུ་གྱུར་པའི་སེམས་ཅན་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p393-q03). It then supplies the step the སྤྱི་དོན leaves to the debater: [ཁྱབ་པ་འགྲིག་མ་གྲུབ་ན། དེར་ཐལ། ཁྱོད་སེམས་ཅན་གང་ཞིག་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་དེ་ཁྱོད་དང་རྒྱུད་གཅིག་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། དེ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་དུ་སྐྱེ་བ་ལེན་མྱོང་བའི་ཕྱིར། རྩ་བར་འདོད་ན། སེམས་ཅན་ཡིན་ན་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པའི་གོ་འཕང་མངོན་དུ་བྱེད་ངེས་ཀྱི་གང་ཟག་ཡིན་དགོས་པར་ཐལ། འདོད་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p393-q04). If Indra is certain to reach Śākyamuni's state merely because one Indra was the Bodhisattva, then by the same reasoning any sentient being would be, since the Bodhisattva was also a sentient being. The absurdity is the one in the arhat case, run forward in time instead of backward.

**Why Indra works.** "Indra" (ལྷའི་དབང་པོ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན) names an office, not an individual. The Vinaya's Māndhātṛ story, the same cycle the second half of the paragraph draws on, says so incidentally: [གཞོན་ནུ་ང་ལས་ནུ་གཞོན་ནུའི་རྩེད་མོས་རྩེ་བ་ལ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་དྲུག་ཤི་འཕོས་པ་ལོན་ནོ།](quote:p393-q11), "while the prince Māndhātṛ played at boys' games, six Indras died and passed on." One holder of that office was the Buddha in a past life. The Tathāgatācintyaguhyanirdeśa names him: [འདི་ལྟར་བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་འདི། དེའི་ཚེ་དེའི་དུས་ན་ལྷའི་དབང་པོ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན་མིག་བཟང་ཞེས་བྱ་བར་གྱུར་ཏེ།](quote:p393-q10), "this Blessed One Śākyamuni was, at that time, Indra, called Good Eye." The Maitreyaprasthāna contains a similar identification of the Buddha with an Indra of the past. So the paragraph's reason, that the Teacher once took Indra's birth, is well grounded in the canon. It makes "Indra" and the Teacher of one continuum in exactly the existential sense that makes "Buddha superior" and the arhat so, and it is equally unable to carry an individual's destiny over to the general term. The paragraph does not say that this is why Indra was chosen; that is my reading. It is the reading that makes the counter-instance work, and it fits the མཐའ་དཔྱོད's generalisation to all sentient beings.

**The second exchange.** The Māndhātṛ claim is a claim about tense. In his མཐའ་དཔྱོད, Chökyi Gyaltsen states the question and gives his own answer: [ཁ་ཅིག རྫ་མཁན་གྱི་ཁྱེའུ་སྣང་བྱེད་དང་ཁྱེའུ་བླ་མ་དང་། རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུས་སོགས་ད་ལྟར་གྱི་དུས་སུ་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་ཡིན་ནམ་མ་ཡིན། ཞེས་ཟེར། དེ་ལ་ཁ་ཅིག མ་ཡིན་ཟེར་བ་མི་འཐད་དེ། རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུས་དེ་ད་ལྟར་གྱི་དུས་སུ་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། དེ་ད་ལྟར་གྱི་དུས་སུ་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པར་གྱུར་པ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། དེ་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པར་གྱུར་པའི་དུས་ཡོད་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p393-q05). The potter's son Prabhākara, the youth Uttara and King Māndhātṛ are all past lives of the Buddha. Are they, at the present time, Śākyamuni? Some say no; he says yes, because there is a time at which Māndhātṛ has become Śākyamuni, and that time is now. What he refuses, in the paragraph, is the step from "Śākyamuni at the present time" to plain "Śākyamuni". A few folios later the མཐའ་དཔྱོད shows how little the time-indexed predicate carries: [ཁ་ཅིག རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུས་ཆོས་ཅན། ད་ལྟའི་དུས་སུ་ཡོད་པར་ཐལ། ད་ལྟའི་དུས་སུ་ད་ལྟའི་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ན་མ་ཁྱབ།](quote:p393-q07). Being the present Śākyamuni at the present time does not even entail that Māndhātṛ exists now. The king is past, and "at the present time" qualifies what he has become, not him.

**The scriptural ground for Māndhātṛ.** The མཐའ་དཔྱོད cites the identity only as "a sūtra": [རྟགས་གྲུབ་སྟེ། ། མདོ་ལས། ང་དེའི་ཚེ། དེའི་དུས་ན་རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུས་སུ་གྱུར་ཏོ། ། ཞེས་གསུངས་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p393-q08). A search of the Kangyur finds the formula at the close of the Māndhātṛ story in the Ratnakūṭa's *Pitāputrasamāgama*: [དེའི་ཚེ་དེའི་དུས་ན་རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུ་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་བྱུང་བ་དེ་གཞན་ཞིག་ཡིན་སྙམ་དུ་དོགས་ཤིང་ཐེ་ཚོམ་ཟའམ། ནེམ་ནུར་དུ་གྱུར་ན། ཁྱོད་ཀྱིས་དེ་ལྟར་མི་བལྟའོ། །དེ་ཅིའི་ཕྱིར་ཞེ་ན། རྒྱལ་པོ་ཆེན་པོ། ང་ཉིད་དེ་ཚེ་དེའི་དུས་ན་༄༅། །འཁོར་ལོས་བསྒྱུར་བའི་རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུ་ཞེས་བྱ་བར་གྱུར་ཏེ།](quote:p393-q09), "Great king, if you doubt or wonder whether the one who was King Māndhātṛ at that time was someone else, do not see it so. Why? I myself at that time was the wheel-turning king Māndhātṛ." The wording differs from the མཐའ་དཔྱོད's citation, which is shorter and spelled ང་ལས་ནུས་སུ. So this is the canonical *source* of the claim, not necessarily the exact text Chökyi Gyaltsen had in front of him. The Vinayavastu version of the story, also searched, identifies Māndhātṛ's own past life (a grain merchant) rather than the Buddha's, and is not the source.

**Je Tsongkhapa and Gyaltsab Je.** Neither the ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་ཕྲེང nor the རྣམ་བཤད་སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན runs this debate at the lemma (see §3). Searches for རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུས and for the Indra counter-instance return nothing in either. The yig cha is not leaning on them here.

**Panchen Sonam Drakpa.** His འགྲེལ་ཚིག་དོན་རབ་གསལ does not mention Māndhātṛ anywhere, so the Loseling side of this debate could not be checked. That is a limit of the evidence, not a finding about Loseling.

**Sera Jey specificity.** Every tier-1 passage used here is Chökyi Gyaltsen's own: the སྤྱི་དོན and its companion མཐའ་དཔྱོད. The pack found nothing at tiers 2–4. What this yig cha distinctively does is treat the cause refuge / result refuge distinction as a problem in the logic of general terms and time-indexed identity, and test it with jātaka material. The one comparable Gelug text located, Jamyang Zhepa's Gomang textbook (§5), uses the same device in a different place, which suggests the method was shared even if this application is Chökyi Gyaltsen's.

## 5 · Bones of Contention 🦴

**What is disputed?** Two things. (1) Whether "Buddha superior is of one continuum with every sentient being" commits one to Buddha superior's having each being's past, so that it is "preceded by" a Hearer arhat's realization. (2) Whether "Māndhātṛ is Śākyamuni at the present time" commits one to "Māndhātṛ is Śākyamuni".

**Who argues what?**

- **The ཁོ་ན་རེ (first exchange):** yes to (1). Same continuum carries the history with it, so p392's pervasion should be withdrawn.
- **The ཁ་ཅིག (second exchange):** yes to (2). Pressed further in p394, the claim leads by symmetry to Śākyamuni's being his own cause at Māndhātṛ's time.
- **Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen:** no to both. In the མཐའ་དཔྱོད he also answers the p394 counter-attack that the སྤྱི་དོན leaves standing. He accepts that Śākyamuni is, at Māndhātṛ's time, the cause of Śākyamuni, and denies only the final reason: [འདོད་ན། དེ་ཆོས་ཅན། ཁྱོད་རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུས་ཀྱི་དུས་སུ་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པའི་རྒྱུ་མ་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། དེའི་དུས་སུ་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་དེ་ཁྱོད་ཀྱི་རྒྱུ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ན། རྟགས་མ་གྲུབ་བོ། །](quote:p393-q06). A reader who has only the སྤྱི་དོན might take p394's ཞེས་ཟེར as the last word. The companion text shows that it is not.
- **Jamyang Zhepa (Drepung Gomang):** no direct engagement with this debate was located. His ཕར་ཕྱིན་མཐའ་དཔྱོད, in a different place (whether the author of the Ornament must be a Buddha), uses the identical device against an opponent who argues from an individual to the general term: [འོ་ན་ཁོ་རང་ལ་སངས་འཕགས་ཆོས་ཅན། བརྒྱད་སྟོང་པ་གསུང་པ་པོའི་སངས་རྒྱས་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། བརྒྱད་སྟོང་པ་གསུངས་པ་གང་ཞིགས་ངས་རྒྱས་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། ཁྱབ་པ་ཁས། འདོད་ན། བདག་ཅག་གི་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ་ལོ། །](quote:p393-q13). If what is true of one Buddha transferred to "Buddha superior", then Buddha superior would be the speaker of the Aṣṭasāhasrikā and so "our Teacher Śākyamuni". This is support by method from the other great textbook tradition, not agreement on this point. It is tier 5 and should be weighted accordingly.

**A textual complication.** The apparatus records witness ཀ reading ཟེར་ན where the printed text has བྱས་པ་ལ། ཁ་ཅིག (see §2). On the ཀ reading, "Māndhātṛ is Śākyamuni at the present time" belongs to an opponent, and the author would be the one pressing the consequence "then he is Śākyamuni". That would reverse who holds what in the second half of the paragraph. The two other witnesses and the edition's own choice agree with the printed text. The མཐའ་དཔྱོད settles the doctrinal question independently, because there Chökyi Gyaltsen [defends the claim in his own voice](quote:p393-q05). So the printed reading gives his view, and the ཀ reading should be treated as a variant, not as evidence that he rejected the claim.

**Why does it matter?** The definition of the result refuge in p390 ("the Buddha one is oneself to attain") needs a same-continuum relation between a sentient being and a Buddha who does not yet exist. p392 needs that relation to hold universally, so that no Buddha is "of a different continuum" from a bodhisattva. If same-continuum carried history with it, both claims would make every Buddha a former Hearer arhat and every Indra a future Teacher. The same-continuum relation and the time-indexed identity the paragraph protects are what allow the tradition to say both "my result refuge exists now" (p394's closing exchange) and "my Buddhahood is not yet accomplished". Without those limits the doctrine of going for refuge to one's own future Buddhahood is incoherent.

**Resolution.** Sera Jey follows Chökyi Gyaltsen. Same-continuum relations between general terms hold existentially and transfer no individual history. An identity indexed to the present time does not license the unindexed identity. Past-life identities from the jātakas are true at the time indexed and not simpliciter. The evidence for this is entirely tier 1 plus the Kangyur, and nothing located in the Indian commentaries or in Tsongkhapa and Gyaltsab bears on it either way.

## 6 · Key Terminology & Technical Concepts

- **རྒྱུད་གཅིག།** — being of one continuum; belonging to one stream of mind across lives.
  - **How understood here:** p392 and the མཐའ་དཔྱོད use it between *general* terms ("Buddha superior" and "bodhisattva", "sentient being"). There it holds as long as some instance of one term lies in the same stream as the subject, and it is symmetric. This paragraph fixes its limit: it carries no history or destiny from the individual to the general term.
  - **Debates:** whether the relation can be predicated of a general term at all is what the ཁོ་ན་རེ is testing. The Gomang parallel shows the same question arising for any predicate true of one Buddha.

- **འབྲས་བུའི་སྐྱབས། / རྒྱུའི་སྐྱབས།** — result refuge and cause refuge.
  - **How understood here:** defined by continuum in p390. The result refuge is the Three Jewels one is to attain in one's own continuum. The cause refuge is the Three Jewels already accomplished in another's. That is why the "one continuum" relation needs defending at all.
  - **Debates:** p391 disputes whether the omniscience "that will arise" in a last-moment bodhisattva's continuum is that bodhisattva's result refuge, and whether it exists at that moment. p394 closes by insisting that the result refuge of the respondent exists now. The own position requires both.

- **ཉན་ཐོས་དགྲ་བཅོམ་པའི་རྟོགས་པ་སྔོན་དུ་སོང་བ།** — "preceded by the realization of a Hearer arhat"; describes a Great Vehicle practitioner who entered from the Lesser Vehicle.
  - **How understood here:** a real class of persons in this system. The paragraph does not deny that Hearer arhats become Buddhas. It denies only that Buddha superior *as such* is in this class.

- **ད་ལྟར་གྱི་དུས་སུ།** — "at the present time"; a temporal index on a predicate.
  - **How understood here:** "X is Y at the present time" says what X has become at the present time. It is compatible with X's not existing now (the མཐའ་དཔྱོད's Māndhātṛ example) and does not yield "X is Y".
  - **Debates:** whether jātaka identities ("I at that time was Māndhātṛ") license present-tense identity is the question the མཐའ་དཔྱོད puts directly. Some said no; Chökyi Gyaltsen says yes, with the time index kept.

- **ལྷའི་དབང་པོ་བརྒྱ་བྱིན།** — Śakra, Indra, lord of the gods of the Thirty-three.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *Devendra Śakra*, *Śatakratu*.
  - **How understood here:** a title held in succession (the Vinaya's six Indras dying in one prince's boyhood). That is why the general term can be of one continuum with the Teacher through one past holder without being "certain to actualize the Teacher's state".
