# Is the omniscience still arising a resultant refuge? — the sprout in the process of arising

## 1 · Passage & Context

**Source Text:**

> ཁ་ཅིག ། རྒྱུན་མཐའ་བའི་རྒྱུད་ལ་འབྱུང་འགྱུར་གྱི་རྣམ་མཁྱེན་དེ་དེའི་འབྲས་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་ཟེར་ན། དེ་ལ་ཁ་ཅིག ། དེ་ཆོས་ཅན། དེའི་དུས་སུ་མེད་པར་ཐལ། དེའི་འབྲས་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། འདོད་མི་ནུས་ཏེ། ས་བོན་ལས་སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པའི་མྱུ་གུ་ས་བོན་གྱི་དུས་སུ་ཡོད་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། དེ་ཡོད། དེ་མྱུ་གུའི་དུས་སུ་མེད་པའི་ཕྱིར། དང་པོ་གྲུབ་སྟེ། ས་བོན་འགག་བཞིན་པ་དང་མྱུ་གུ་སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པ་དུས་མཉམ་པའི་ཕྱིར་ན་མ་ཁྱབ༑ གཉིས་པ་མ་གྲུབ་ན། མྱུ་གུ་དེ་རང་དུས་སུ་སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པར་ཐལ་ལོ། ། ཞེས་ཟེར་ན། ས་བོན་ལས་སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པའི་མྱུ་གུ་མེད་དེ། དེ་ལྟར་མ་ཡིན་ན། དེ་ཁོ་ཁོ་རང་དུ་ཐལ། དེ་ཡོད་པའི་ཕྱིར། འདོད་ན། མྱུ་གུ་ཡིན་ན་ས་བོན་ལས་སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པ་ཡིན་དགོས་པར་ཐལ་ལོ། ། དེས་ན་རྒྱུན་མཐའ་བའི་རྒྱུད་ལ་འབྱུང་འགྱུར་གྱི་རྣམ་མཁྱེན་དེ་དེའི་འབྲས་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་ཅིང་དེའི་རྒྱུད་ལ་སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པའི་རྣམ་མཁྱེན་དེ་དེ་མ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། དེ་མེད་པའི་ཕྱིར།

**Preceding Section:** p390 is the second topic of the general presentation of refuge (སྐྱབས་སྤྱིའི་རྣམ་གཞག), and it states the positive doctrine this paragraph turns into a debate. It quotes the Uttaratantra verse སྟོན་པ་བསྟན་པ་སློབ་དོན་གྱིས། ། ཐེག་པ་གསུམ་དང་བྱེད་གསུམ་ལ། ། … and explains why scripture sets out three refuges. For those who have entered the Mahāyāna path, the Buddha to be attained by themselves is the *resultant refuge* (སངས་རྒྱས་འབྲས་བུའི་སྐྱབས), and the Buddha already accomplished in another continuum is the *causal refuge* (རྒྱུའི་སྐྱབས). Pratyekabuddha-lineage practitioners take the Dharma jewel as their resultant refuge, and śrāvakas take the Saṅgha jewel. The paragraph closes by showing each jewel to be supreme among bipeds, among the free-from-desire and among assemblies. It gives no further account of what "the Buddha to be attained" is at any given moment.

**Following Section:** p392 opens a second ཁ་ཅིག on the *causal* side. Someone claims that the Three Jewels in a continuum different from a Mahāyāna path-of-accumulation bodhisattva are that bodhisattva's causal refuge. The reply is that there are no such Jewels. Every buddha-ārya is of one continuum with that bodhisattva, because every sentient being is. The chapter thus takes the causal/resultant pair from p390 and tests it at both ends: here at the very end of the path, there at its beginning.

**What this paragraph is doing.** It defends a thesis that a first ཁ་ཅིག states: the omniscience that *will arise* (འབྱུང་འགྱུར) in the continuum of a bodhisattva at the end of the continuum (རྒྱུན་མཐའ་བ, the last moment of the path of meditation) is that bodhisattva's resultant refuge. A second ཁ་ཅིག attacks it by an argument whose hinge is the sprout "in the process of arising from the seed" (ས་བོན་ལས་སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པའི་མྱུ་གུ). The paragraph answers by denying that any such sprout exists, and so denies that an omniscience "in the process of arising" exists in that continuum. The upshot is that the future omniscience is the resultant refuge and the "arising" omniscience is not, because there is no such thing.

## 2 · Translation & Breakdown

<!--line 0-86-->
> **ཁ་ཅིག ། རྒྱུན་མཐའ་བའི་རྒྱུད་ལ་འབྱུང་འགྱུར་གྱི་རྣམ་མཁྱེན་དེ་དེའི་འབྲས་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་ཟེར་ན།**
>
> Someone says: the omniscience (རྣམ་མཁྱེན, *sarvākārajñatā*) that will arise (འབྱུང་འགྱུར) in the continuum of one at the end of the continuum (རྒྱུན་མཐའ་བ) is that person's resultant refuge (འབྲས་སྐྱབས).

<!--line 86-163-->
> **དེ་ལ་ཁ་ཅིག ། དེ་ཆོས་ཅན། དེའི་དུས་སུ་མེད་པར་ཐལ། དེའི་འབྲས་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར།**
>
> To that, someone else [objects]: take that [future omniscience] as the subject. It follows that it does not exist at that person's time, because it is that person's resultant refuge.

<!--line 163-243-->
> **འདོད་མི་ནུས་ཏེ། ས་བོན་ལས་སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པའི་མྱུ་གུ་ས་བོན་གྱི་དུས་སུ་ཡོད་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ།**
>
> [And, the objector continues,] you cannot accept that, because a sprout in the process of arising (སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པ) from a seed exists at the time of the seed. [By parity, then, the omniscience in the process of arising in the continuum of the one at the end of the continuum exists at that person's time.]

<!--line 243-284-->
> **དེ་ཡོད། དེ་མྱུ་གུའི་དུས་སུ་མེད་པའི་ཕྱིར།**
>
> [That follows] because such a sprout exists, and it does not exist at the time of the sprout [so it must exist at the time of the seed].

<!--line 284-356-->
> **དང་པོ་གྲུབ་སྟེ། ས་བོན་འགག་བཞིན་པ་དང་མྱུ་གུ་སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པ་དུས་མཉམ་པའི་ཕྱིར་**
>
> The first [reason, that such a sprout exists] is established, because the seed's ceasing and the sprout's arising are simultaneous —

<!--line 356-365-->
> **ན་མ་ཁྱབ༑**
>
> — [if that is the argument,] there is no pervasion.

This short clause is ambiguous in its position. As printed, it sits inside the objector's speech, which does not close until ཞེས་ཟེར་ན at 424–435. It is best read as the author's interjected answer to the proof of the first reason: the simultaneity of ceasing and arising does not entail that a sprout-in-the-process-of-arising exists. That is exactly the answer given at 435 onward. The parallel in the Skal bzang mgul rgyan (§4) supports this reading, since it attaches མ་ཁྱབ་སྟེ to the same premise. The mtha' dpyod's version of the objection has no such clause.

<!--line 365-435-->
> **གཉིས་པ་མ་གྲུབ་ན། མྱུ་གུ་དེ་རང་དུས་སུ་སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པར་ཐལ་ལོ། ། ཞེས་ཟེར་ན།**
>
> If the second [reason, that it does not exist at the sprout's time] is not established, it would follow that the sprout is still in the process of arising at its own time. — If that is said:

<!--line 435-489-->
> **ས་བོན་ལས་སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པའི་མྱུ་གུ་མེད་དེ། དེ་ལྟར་མ་ཡིན་ན།**
>
> [Reply:] there is no sprout in the process of arising from the seed. For if that were not so,

<!--line 489-525-->
> **དེ་ཁོ་ཁོ་རང་དུ་ཐལ། དེ་ཡོད་པའི་ཕྱིར།**
>
> it would follow that that [sprout-in-the-process-of-arising] is itself [a sprout in the process of arising], because it exists.

The words དེ་ཁོ་ཁོ་རང་དུ་ཐལ are compressed. The sense, which the mtha' dpyod spells out, is that whatever exists as "the sprout arising from the seed" would have to be, at its own time, arising from the seed. Yet a sprout at its own time is already fully produced.

<!--line 525-588-->
> **འདོད་ན། མྱུ་གུ་ཡིན་ན་ས་བོན་ལས་སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པ་ཡིན་དགོས་པར་ཐལ་ལོ། །**
>
> If you accept that, it follows that whatever is a sprout must be in the process of arising from a seed. [That is absurd.]

<!--line 588-748-->
> **དེས་ན་རྒྱུན་མཐའ་བའི་རྒྱུད་ལ་འབྱུང་འགྱུར་གྱི་རྣམ་མཁྱེན་དེ་དེའི་འབྲས་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་ཅིང་དེའི་རྒྱུད་ལ་སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པའི་རྣམ་མཁྱེན་དེ་དེ་མ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། དེ་མེད་པའི་ཕྱིར།**
>
> Therefore the omniscience that will arise in the continuum of the one at the end of the continuum is that person's resultant refuge, while an omniscience in the process of arising in that person's continuum is not that [refuge], because no such thing exists.

The closing ཕྱིར་ཏེ reads as a conclusion rather than as a reason awaiting a thesis. Its force is that the first ཁ་ཅིག's thesis stands, and the objection fails because its middle term, an omniscience "in the process of arising", is empty.

## 3 · Structural Context (Dkar Chag)

- **Abhisamayālaṃkāra location:** the ten instructions (གདམས་ངག) of the first chapter, at the instruction on the Three Jewels: [སྒྲུབ་དང་བདེན་པ་རྣམས་དང་ནི། ། སངས་རྒྱས་ལ་སོགས་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ། །](quote:p391-q01). The verse names the Three Jewels as an object of instruction. It says nothing about refuge as cause or result, or about when a refuge exists.
- **Haribhadra's commentary location:** the aligned place in the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ glosses the Buddha-jewel instruction as teaching that omniscience, the dharma that makes a buddha, is not apprehended as object or subject: [སངས་རྒྱས་ལ་སོགས་སངས་རྒྱས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་དག་མཚན་ཉིད་ཀྱིས་སངས་རྒྱས་སུ་བྱེད་པའི་ཆོས་ཀྱི་མཚན་ཉིད་རྣམ་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པ་ཉིད་མི་དམིགས་པ་ལ་…](quote:p391-q02). The Saṅgha instruction follows as the twenty kinds of irreversible ārya bodhisattvas. Having read the passage, I can report that Haribhadra raises no distinction between causal and resultant refuge there, does not mention the end of the continuum, and says nothing about the temporal status of a result. The causal/resultant scheme enters from the Uttaratantra verse quoted in p390, by way of Tsongkhapa and Gyaltsab. The sprout-and-seed problem comes from the Tibetan analysis of the three times, not from any Indian commentary on the *Ornament*.
- **Text location:** in སྐབས་དང་པོའི་སྤྱི་དོན།, under སྐྱབས་གསུམ་ལ་གདམས་པ། (opening at p376), within གསུམ་པ་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་བཤད་པ (from p383), in the sub-section ༢༽ སྐྱབས་སྤྱིའི་རྣམ་གཞག་བཤད་པ (from p388). p390 is that sub-section's own exposition of the Uttaratantra verse. p391 and p392 are a pair of disputations appended to it, one testing the resultant refuge and one the causal refuge.
- **Section type:** a nested disputation. A thesis (ཁ་ཅིག … ཟེར་ན) meets an objection from a second party (དེ་ལ་ཁ་ཅིག … ཞེས་ཟེར་ན). The author then answers the objection, and in doing so upholds the original thesis.
- **Argumentative role:** it fixes the reference of "the Buddha to be attained" in p390's definition of the resultant refuge. The resultant refuge of someone on the last moment of the path is the omniscience that will arise. It is not something that already exists, even partially, as "arising". The author secures this by rejecting a category, the sprout-in-the-process-of-arising, rather than by adjusting the definition of refuge.

## 4 · Philosophical Exposition & Commentary

**Core meaning.** Everyone agrees that for a Mahāyāna practitioner the resultant refuge is the buddhahood to be attained in their own continuum. Gyaltsab Je, whom p390 follows almost verbatim, puts it as [ཐེག་པ་ཆེན་པོའི་རིགས་ཅན་ལམ་ལ་ཞུགས་པ་དེ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་རང་རྒྱུད་ལ་ཐོབ་བྱའི་སངས་རྒྱས་འབྲས་བུའི་སྐྱབས་དང་། གྲུབ་ཟིན་གྱི་སངས་རྒྱས་རྒྱུའི་སྐྱབས་སུ་ཤེས་པར་བྱ་བ་དང་།](quote:p391-q04). Tsongkhapa's gser phreng draws the same line: the three-vehicle assignment of the jewels is made [ཐུན་མོང་མ་ཡིན་པའི་ཐོབ་བྱ་འབྲས་བུའི་སྐྱབས་འགྲོའི་དབང་དུ་བྱས་པའི་ཕྱིར་རོ། །](quote:p391-q03). The hard case is the bodhisattva one moment short of buddhahood. The omniscience he will attain is the direct result of his present mind, and a direct result stands to its cause as sprout to seed. The objector's move is to say that if there is a sprout "in the process of arising" at the time of the seed, then there is an omniscience "in the process of arising" at the time of the end of the continuum. In that case the resultant refuge, or at least something indistinguishable from it, would exist now, which is awkward for a refuge defined as "to be attained". The paragraph cuts the argument at its root. There is no sprout in the process of arising. When the seed is ceasing, the sprout is not yet. When the sprout is, it is already produced and no longer arising.

**Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen's own treatment.** His སྐབས་དང་པོའི་མཐའ་དཔྱོད། runs the same debate at greater length, and three features of it change how this paragraph should be read.

First, the objector is a *respected* one. The first thesis belongs to a plain ཁ་ཅིག, but the objection is introduced as [འདི་ལ་མཁས་པ་ཁ་ཅིག](quote:p391-q05) and closed with ཞེས་གསུངས. The mtha' dpyod also states the step that the spyi don leaves implicit: if you accept, then the omniscience in the process of arising would not exist at that person's time either. That step is what makes the sprout analogy bite. The mtha' dpyod does not name this "learned one". The source was not located in the other commentaries available here.

Second, the mtha' dpyod gives the full form of the clause the spyi don compresses. If the sprout-arising were not absent at the sprout's own time, it would be arising at its own time. That cannot be accepted, [དེ་རང་དུས་སུ་ས་བོན་ལས་རྫོགས་པར་གྲུབ་ཟིན་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p391-q06), because at its own time it is already fully produced from the seed. The same passage reports two *other* replies to the learned objector. One holds that an omniscience in the process of arising does exist and exists at the end-of-continuum time. The other holds that it exists at the time of the first moment of omniscience. Chökyi Gyaltsen refutes both, and both concede the category the spyi don denies.

Third, his own answer is exactly the spyi don's, argued in full: [རང་ལན་ནི། ས་བོན་ལས་སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པའི་མྱུ་གུ་མ་གྲུབ་སྟེ། … དེ་ལས་སྐྱེས་ཟིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། མ་ཁྱབ་ན། ས་བོན་གྱི་འབྲས་བུ་ཡིན་ན་ས་བོན་ལས་སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པ་ཡིན་དགོས་པར་ཐལ།](quote:p391-q07). The spyi don's closing absurdity, "whatever is a sprout would have to be arising from a seed", is the compressed form of this.

**The positive thesis.** In the own-system section of the same mtha' dpyod, Chökyi Gyaltsen identifies the two refuges with this very person as his example: [བྱང་སེམས་རྒྱུན་མཐའ་བའི་ཐོབ་བྱར་གྱུར་པའི་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་བྱང་སེམས་རྒྱུན་མཐའ་བའི་འབྲས་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་ནོ། །](quote:p391-q08). That is why the spyi don ends by affirming the first ཁ་ཅིག's thesis rather than refuting it.

**A limit on what is conceded.** The paragraph lets stand the objector's consequence that the future omniscience does not exist at that person's time, since the only argument offered against accepting it has been dismantled. It should not be read as asserting the general rule that a resultant refuge never exists at the time of the one whose refuge it is. The mtha' dpyod rejects exactly that rule: [ཁ་ཅིག གང་ཟག་དེའི་འབྲས་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་ན་གང་ཟག་དེའི་དུས་ན་མེད་པས་ཁྱབ་ཟེར་ན། སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་ཆོས་ཅན། …](quote:p391-q09). A buddha-ārya is the final goal, and hence the resultant refuge, of the sentient beings who are its contemporaries. The non-existence at p391 is a fact about an omniscience still to arise in one's *own* continuum. It is not part of what "resultant refuge" means.

**Logical reasoning.** The argument depends on a strict reading of the Sautrāntika-derived analysis of production. Cause and effect are not simultaneous, and a result "arises" only in the sense that it is produced from a cause that has ceased. On the Sera Jey side, the ground is stated with a Pramāṇavārttika citation in the Skal bzang mgul rgyan, the dkon mchog gsum mtha' dpyod catalogued here under Lobsang Tenpay Nyima and ascribed in its title to Kedrup Jampa Tashi. That text runs this very debate. It answers the simultaneity argument with a denial of pervasion and grounds the answer in Dharmakīrti ([… ཟེར་ན། མ་ཁྱབ་སྟེ།](quote:p391-q12)): [རང་གི་རྒྱུ་ལས་གྲུབ་པའི་ཕྱིར། །འབྲས་བུ་རག་ལས་མེད་པར་ཡང། །རྟོག་པ་ཡིས་ནི་འབྲེལ་པར་བྱེད། །](quote:p391-q13). The result, being established from its own cause, is at its own time not dependent on it. Only conceptual thought connects the two. So "arising from the seed" is not a property the sprout has while it exists. The verse is Dharmakīrti's own and is cited here from the Pramāṇavārttika-kārikā in the Tengyur. Gyaltsab's commentary on the Sambandhaparīkṣā also quotes it.

**Sera Jey specificity.** This is a point on which the Sera Jey and Loseling yig cha divide; see §5.

## 5 · Bones of Contention 🦴

- **What is disputed?** There are two questions, nested. The first is whether a sprout "in the process of arising from the seed" exists at all, and so whether an omniscience "in the process of arising" exists in the continuum of the bodhisattva at the end of the continuum. The second follows from the first: is that the resultant refuge, or is only the omniscience "that will arise"?

- **Who argues what?**
  - *Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen (Sera Jey)*, in this spyi don and in his mtha' dpyod ([q07](quote:p391-q07)), holds that no sprout-in-the-process-of-arising exists. So no omniscience-in-the-process-of-arising exists, and the resultant refuge is the omniscience that will arise.
  - *The "learned one" (མཁས་པ་ཁ་ཅིག) of the mtha' dpyod* ([q05](quote:p391-q05)) and the unnamed respondents reported there ([q06](quote:p391-q06)) all take the sprout-in-the-process-of-arising to exist. They differ over when the arising omniscience exists: not at the end-of-continuum time, at that time, or at the first moment of omniscience. The mtha' dpyod does not identify them, and I have not identified them.
  - *Kedrup Tenpa Dargye (Drepung Loseling)*, in his ཕར་ཕྱིན་མཐའ་དཔྱོད་པད་མ་དཀར་པོའི་ཕྲེང་བ, takes the opposite line on both counts. He holds that the omniscience actually arising in the continuum of one on the final uninterrupted path *is* the final resultant refuge, because the omniscience that will arise there is: [ཁ་ཅིག །རྒྱུན་མཐའི་བར་ཆད་མེད་ལམ་པའི་རྒྱུད་ལ་དངོས་སུ་སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པའི་རྣམ་མཁྱེན་དེ། … འབྲས་སྐྱབས་མཐར་ཐུག་མ་ཡིན་ཟེར་ན། དེ་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ།](quote:p391-q10). He treats "arising" and "will arise" as picking out the same omniscience. He also explicitly refutes the very thesis on which Chökyi Gyaltsen's answer rests: [ཁ་ཅིག་ས་བོན་གྱི་དུས་སུ་སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པའི་མྱུ་གུ་མེད་ཟེར་ན། དེ་ཡོད་པར་ཐལ། ས་བོན་གྱི་སྐད་ཅིག་གཉིས་པའི་དུས་སུ་དངོས་སུ་སྐྱེས་མ་ཐག་པའི་མྱུ་གུ་ཡོད་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p391-q11). For Tenpa Dargye the sprout-in-the-process-of-arising exists: it is the sprout that has just been produced as the seed's direct result.
  - *The Skal bzang mgul rgyan* (Kedrup Jampa Tashi, as catalogued) sides with Chökyi Gyaltsen on the substance and adds the Pramāṇavārttika ground ([q12](quote:p391-q12), [q13](quote:p391-q13)). Its lineage affiliation is not stated in the file and I have not verified it.
  - *Jamyang Shepa (Gomang)*, in the ཕར་ཕྱིན་མཐའ་དཔྱོད་རིན་ཆེན་སྒྲོན་མེ, debates causal and resultant refuge in the same chapter. He discusses whether a buddha is both, and whether the resultant refuge must be a result. In the places I read and searched he does not take up the end-of-continuum omniscience or the arising sprout. His position on this question was not located.
  - *Indian sources*: silent. Haribhadra does not raise the question ([q02](quote:p391-q02)).

- **Why does it matter?** On the surface this is a question about refuge. Underneath, it is a question about the three times and about production, and the refuge case is where it becomes vivid. If an "arising" result exists while its cause is ceasing, then the not-yet-attained goal has a foothold in the present. Something that is the practitioner's resultant refuge would then already be in some sense present in his continuum before buddhahood. That strains the definition of the resultant refuge as what is *to be attained*, and blurs the line p390 draws between one's own future buddhahood and buddhas already accomplished. Chökyi Gyaltsen keeps the line sharp by paying a price in the theory of causation: "the sprout arising from the seed" names nothing. Tenpa Dargye keeps the ordinary use of "arising", identifies it with the just-produced direct result, and accepts the identification of the arising omniscience with the resultant refuge.

- **Resolution:** Sera Jey follows Chökyi Gyaltsen. The resultant refuge of the bodhisattva at the end of the continuum is the omniscience that will arise in his continuum. There is no sprout, and no omniscience, "in the process of arising", so the objection's middle term is empty. The simultaneity of the cause's ceasing and the result's arising does not establish that such an entity exists. The justification, spelled out in the mtha' dpyod and backed by Dharmakīrti in the Skal bzang mgul rgyan, is that a result at its own time is already fully produced and no longer depends on its cause. The Loseling position is a real alternative, not a misreading. Students debating across the two yig cha should expect the sprout-at-the-seed's-time question to be pressed from the other side.

## 6 · Key Terminology & Technical Concepts

- **འབྲས་སྐྱབས / འབྲས་བུའི་སྐྱབས (resultant refuge):** the refuge that is one's own goal
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *phala-śaraṇa* (reconstructed; not attested in the sources consulted here)
  - **How understood here:** for a Mahāyāna practitioner, the Three Jewels to be attained in one's own continuum ([q04](quote:p391-q04), [q08](quote:p391-q08)). It is contrasted with the causal refuge (རྒྱུའི་སྐྱབས), the Jewels already accomplished in another continuum.
  - **Debates:** whether being X's resultant refuge entails not existing at X's time. Chökyi Gyaltsen denies this in general ([q09](quote:p391-q09)) but accepts it for the omniscience still to arise in one's own continuum.

- **རྒྱུན་མཐའ་བ (one at the end of the continuum):** the bodhisattva on the final uninterrupted path (རྒྱུན་མཐའི་བར་ཆད་མེད་ལམ), the last moment before omniscience.
  - **How understood here:** chosen as the test case because the next moment is buddhahood, so the relation between him and his resultant refuge is exactly that of direct cause to direct result, like seed and sprout.

- **འབྱུང་འགྱུར vs འབྱུང་རུང vs སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པ:** "that will arise", "that can arise", and "in the process of arising"
  - **How understood here:** the spyi don phrases the thesis with འབྱུང་འགྱུར, while the mtha' dpyod ([q05](quote:p391-q05)) and the Skal bzang mgul rgyan use འབྱུང་རུང. Nothing in this paragraph turns on the difference. The contrast that matters is with སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པ, which the paragraph denies has any referent.
  - **Debates:** Tenpa Dargye treats the omniscience "actually arising" (དངོས་སུ་སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པ) and the omniscience "that will arise" as the same resultant refuge ([q10](quote:p391-q10)).

- **སྐྱེ་བཞིན་པ / འགག་བཞིན་པ (arising / ceasing, present-progressive):** the phases of production and destruction
  - **How understood here:** that a seed's ceasing and a sprout's arising are simultaneous is granted as a way of speaking. What is denied is that this licenses a *thing*, "the sprout arising from the seed", existing at the seed's time. A sprout at its own time is སྐྱེས་ཟིན, already produced, and "fully established from its own cause" (རང་གི་རྒྱུ་ལས་གྲུབ་པ, [q13](quote:p391-q13)).
  - **Debates:** Tenpa Dargye identifies the arising sprout with the sprout just produced (སྐྱེས་མ་ཐག་པ) as the seed's direct result, so for him it exists ([q11](quote:p391-q11)).
