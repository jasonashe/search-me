# Who is encircled? — ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ in Haribhadra's condensed homage

## 1 · Passage & Context

**Source Text:**

> གཉིས་པ་བསྡུས་ཏེ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་བ་ནི། སངས་རྒྱས་ཉན་ཐོས་ལ་སོགས་པས་ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ་དེ་སྒྲུབ་པར་བྱེད་པ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་དེ་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལོ། ། ཞེས་པས་འཆད༑ འདིའི་བསྐོར་ཅེས་པའི་དོན་ནི། སངས་རྒྱས་ཉན་ཐོས་རང་རྒྱལ་བྱང་སེམས་ཏེ་སྲས་བཞིས་ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ་དེ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་བཞེད་དོན་སྒྲུབ་པར་བྱེད་པའི་ཡུམ་མཁྱེན་པ་གསུམ་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ། ཞེས་པའི་དོན་ཡིན་ཏེ། ཤེས་རབ་སྒྲོན་མེར། ཉན་ཐོས་ཉིད་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཉིད་དང་སངས་རྒྱས་ཉིད་ཀྱིས་ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ་དེ་ལྟ་བུ་སྒྲུབ་པར་བྱེད་པ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་དེ་དག་གི་ཡུམ་ཤེར་ཕྱིན་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ། ཞེས་གསུངས་པ་དང་། ཉི་ཁྲི་རྣམ་འགྲེལ་ལས། སངས་རྒྱས་ཉན་ཐོས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་ཚོགས་ལྡན། ཞེས་གསུངས་པས་ན། རྩ་བའི་ལྡན་པའམ་བཅས་པའི་དོན་འགྲེལ་པར་བསྐོར་བ་ལ་བཤད་པའི་ཕྱིར༑ ཡུམ་སྲས་ལ་གཙོ་འཁོར་གྱི་ཐ་སྙད་བྱས་པས་ཆོག་སྟེ། མཁྱེན་པ་གསུམ་པོ་དེས་རང་སྲས་འཕགས་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་བཞེད་དོན་ཕུན་ཚོགས་སྒྲུབ་པར་བྱེད་པའི་ཕྱིར། སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན་ལས། སངས་རྒྱས་ཉན་ཐོས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ལ་སོགས་པས་ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ་སྟེ་བཞེད་དོན་སྒྲུབ་པར་བྱེད་པ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་དེ་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ། ཞེས་གསུངས་པ་མ་གཏོགས་གསལ་བར་མ་བཤད་དོ། །

**Preceding Section:** p98 closes the gloss on Haribhadra's description of the Buddha turning the wheel. It first takes up the charge that ཆོས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ་མ་ལུས་པ་རྣམ་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་ says the same thing twice with མ་ལུས་པ and ཐམས་ཅད, and gives two answers: the Golden Rosary's (ཐམས་ཅད can be used of a restricted, intended totality, so མ་ལུས་པ is added to rule that out) and the རྣམ་བཤད's (མ་ལུས་པ applies to the scriptures that express, ཐམས་ཅད to what they express). It then explains why Buddhas are called རྣལ་འབྱོར་ཅན་གྱི་དབང་ཕྱུག་གི་གཙོ་བོ: śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas and bodhisattvas are "lords of yogins" because their yoga exceeds that of Īśvara and the like, and the Buddhas are "chief" among these because their abandonment and realisation exceed all three.

**Following Section:** p100 turns back to the first of the three knowledges and opens a new excursus. Haribhadra said the knowledge of bases brings śrāvakas to "the two kinds of nirvāṇa"; so what is the difference between nirvāṇa with remainder and without? Chökyi Gyaltsen sets out four systems (Śrāvaka schools, Cittamātra, Prāsaṅgika, Svātantrika), and begins with the Śrāvaka account, citing the commentary on the Yuktiṣaṣṭikā for the view that the difference is whether the continuum of aggregates has ceased, and saying both Vaibhāṣika and Sautrāntika take nirvāṇa to be an analytical cessation.

**What this paragraph is doing.** It finishes the word-by-word gloss of Haribhadra's prose paraphrase of the Ornament's homage verse, by glossing its last clause. There is one real problem in that clause, and the paragraph deals only with that. The root verse says the Mother is the Mother "of the Buddhas together with (བཅས) the assemblies of śrāvakas and bodhisattvas". Haribhadra instead says the Mother is "encircled (ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ) by Buddhas, śrāvakas and so on". So who is doing the encircling, and around whom? Chökyi Gyaltsen says the four kinds of Ārya (the Mother's four "sons") encircle the Mother, meaning the three knowledges. He supports this with two Indian commentaries. He says it is legitimate because mother and sons can be spoken of as principal and retinue. He grounds that in the three knowledges' power to accomplish their sons' aims. Then he notes, with some pointedness, that Gyaltsab Je's commentary only repeats the clause.

## 2 · Translation & Breakdown

<!--line 0-30-->
> **གཉིས་པ་བསྡུས་ཏེ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་བ་ནི།**
>
> Second, the homage paid in condensed form (བསྡུས་ཏེ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་བ):

<!--line 30-134-->
> **སངས་རྒྱས་ཉན་ཐོས་ལ་སོགས་པས་ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ་དེ་སྒྲུབ་པར་བྱེད་པ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་དེ་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལོ། ། ཞེས་པས་འཆད༑**
>
> is explained by [Haribhadra's words] "Homage to that Mother who, encircled (ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ) by Buddhas, śrāvakas and the rest, is the very one that accomplishes that [which they seek]."

<!--line 134-162-->
> **འདིའི་བསྐོར་ཅེས་པའི་དོན་ནི།**
>
> The meaning of "encircled" (བསྐོར) here is:

<!--line 162-290-->
> **སངས་རྒྱས་ཉན་ཐོས་རང་རྒྱལ་བྱང་སེམས་ཏེ་སྲས་བཞིས་ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ་དེ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་བཞེད་དོན་སྒྲུབ་པར་བྱེད་པའི་ཡུམ་མཁྱེན་པ་གསུམ་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ།**
>
> "Homage to the Mother, the three knowledges (མཁྱེན་པ་གསུམ), which is encircled by the four sons (སྲས་བཞི), namely Buddhas, śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas and bodhisattvas, and which accomplishes the aims those sons intend (བཞེད་དོན)."

<!--line 290-310-->
> **ཞེས་པའི་དོན་ཡིན་ཏེ།**
>
> That is the meaning, because —

<!--line 310-483-->
> **ཤེས་རབ་སྒྲོན་མེར། ཉན་ཐོས་ཉིད་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཉིད་དང་སངས་རྒྱས་ཉིད་ཀྱིས་ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ་དེ་ལྟ་བུ་སྒྲུབ་པར་བྱེད་པ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་དེ་དག་གི་ཡུམ་ཤེར་ཕྱིན་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ། ཞེས་གསུངས་པ་དང་།**
>
> the *Garland of the Lamp of Wisdom* (ཤེས་རབ་སྒྲོན་མ, Prajñāpradīpāvalī) says: "Homage to their Mother, the Perfection of Wisdom, who, encircled in this way by śrāvakas, by bodhisattvas and by Buddhas, is the very one that accomplishes [their aims]";

<!--line 483-564-->
> **ཉི་ཁྲི་རྣམ་འགྲེལ་ལས། སངས་རྒྱས་ཉན་ཐོས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་ཚོགས་ལྡན། ཞེས་གསུངས་པས་ན།**
>
> and the *Commentary on the Twenty-Five Thousand* (ཉི་ཁྲི་རྣམ་འགྲེལ, the Vārttika) has "[the Mother] endowed with (ལྡན) the assembly of Buddhas, śrāvakas and bodhisattvas" —

<!--line 564-624-->
> **རྩ་བའི་ལྡན་པའམ་བཅས་པའི་དོན་འགྲེལ་པར་བསྐོར་བ་ལ་བཤད་པའི་ཕྱིར༑**
>
> so the commentary [Haribhadra's] has explained the root text's "endowed with" or "together with" (ལྡན་པའམ་བཅས་པ) as "encircled".

<!--line 624-669-->
> **ཡུམ་སྲས་ལ་གཙོ་འཁོར་གྱི་ཐ་སྙད་བྱས་པས་ཆོག་སྟེ།**
>
> It is fine to apply the terms "principal and retinue" (གཙོ་འཁོར) to Mother and sons,

<!--line 669-754-->
> **མཁྱེན་པ་གསུམ་པོ་དེས་རང་སྲས་འཕགས་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་བཞེད་དོན་ཕུན་ཚོགས་སྒྲུབ་པར་བྱེད་པའི་ཕྱིར།**
>
> because those three knowledges accomplish the excellent intended aims of their own sons, the Āryas.

<!--line 754-887-->
> **སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན་ལས། སངས་རྒྱས་ཉན་ཐོས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ལ་སོགས་པས་ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ་སྟེ་བཞེད་དོན་སྒྲུབ་པར་བྱེད་པ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་དེ་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ།**
>
> The *Ornament of the Essence* (སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན, Gyaltsab Je's རྣམ་བཤད་སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན) says: "Homage to that Mother who, encircled by Buddhas, śrāvakas, bodhisattvas and the rest, is the very one that accomplishes their intended aims" —

<!--line 887-925-->
> **ཞེས་གསུངས་པ་མ་གཏོགས་གསལ་བར་མ་བཤད་དོ། །**
>
> but beyond saying that, it does not explain [the point] clearly.

**On the ཡུམ / ཡུལ reading.** In Haribhadra's line, both editions of the Sphuṭārthā in this archive read [སྒྲུབ་པར་བྱེད་པ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་ཡུལ་དེ་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལོ།](quote:p99-q01): the Derge Tengyur and the Commentaries text. Chökyi Gyaltsen, Gyaltsab Je, Khedrup Je and Panchen Sonam Drakpa all quote it with ཡུམ. This is recorded as a variant only. The paragraph's argument does not depend on it, because the two Indian parallels it cites both name the ཡུམ outright.

**On the quotation from Gyaltsab.** The yig cha quotes the སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན as ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ་སྟེ་བཞེད་དོན་སྒྲུབ. The edition of Gyaltsab in this archive reads [ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བའི་བཞེད་དོན་དེ་སྒྲུབ](quote:p99-q07). A search found the yig cha's form only in the yig cha itself. The two readings differ in who is encircled. With བ་སྟེ, "encircled" is a predicate of the Mother. With བའི, it modifies the aims (the aims of the encircling assembly), which leaves open who is at the centre. That openness is exactly what Chökyi Gyaltsen goes on to complain of.

## 3 · Structural Context (Dkar Chag)

- **Abhisamayālaṃkāra location:** the homage verse, specifically its fourth line, [ཉན་ཐོས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་ཚོགས་བཅས་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་ནི་ཡུམ་དེ་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ།](quote:p99-q02) (Tengyur Toh. 3786). The pack names no lemma for this outline path; I located this line by reading. The paragraph is not commenting on the root directly. It comments on Haribhadra's prose paraphrase of the root, and the root line is present in the argument as the thing Haribhadra paraphrased.
- **Haribhadra's commentary location:** the closing clause of the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ's paraphrase of the homage verse, [སངས་རྒྱས་ཉན་ཐོས་ལ་སོགས་པས་ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ་དེ་སྒྲུབ་པར་བྱེད་པ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་ཡུལ་དེ་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལོ།](quote:p99-q01), which follows directly on the ཆོས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ་མ་ལུས་པ་རྣམ་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་བསྐོར་བར་མཛད་པ glossed in p98. I read the passage. Haribhadra makes the substitution without comment. He does not mention the root's བཅས. He does not say whom the Buddhas, śrāvakas and others encircle. He does not explain why Buddhas, who in the root are the head noun ("Mother *of the Buddhas*"), now stand at the front of a list of encirclers. The next sentence moves straight on to whether the Ornament summarises bases, antidotes or aspects. So the question in this paragraph is not one Haribhadra raises. It exists only for a reader who sets his paraphrase beside the root line. Tsongkhapa was the first in this lineage (as far as the sources here show) to do that explicitly.
- **Text location:** སྐབས་དང་པོའི་སྤྱི་དོན།, in the section explaining the words of Haribhadra's commentary on the homage. The pack places ༢༽ བསྡུས་ཏེ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་བ inside "གསུམ་པ་རྣམ་མཁྱེན་གྱིས་ཆོས་འཁོར་སྐོར་བའི་བདག་རྐྱེན་བྱེད་ཚུལ". Reading the text shows that nesting is flattened. The division that gives this paragraph its heading comes a few paragraphs earlier, at the start of the gloss: [འདི་ལ་སོ་སོའི་ཡོན་ཏན་བརྗོད་པ་དང་། བསྡུས་ཏེ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་བ་གཉིས་ལས།](quote:p99-q14). The first member, praising each of the three knowledges' qualities, has three sub-parts (knowledge of bases, of paths, and of all aspects), and the third of those ran through p97–p98. So this paragraph is the *second of two*, a sibling of the whole three-knowledges block, not a fourth item under the knowledge of all aspects. The pack's path should be read with that correction.
- **Section type:** word commentary (ཚིག་དོན) on an Indian commentary, with a short justification and an implicit criticism of a Gelug predecessor. There is no ཁ་ཅིག and no formal debate.
- **Argumentative role:** it settles how to construe the Mother's relation to the four Āryas before the text turns (p100 onward) to the doctrinal questions Haribhadra's paraphrase raises. The construal matters later. The reason given here, that the three knowledges accomplish their sons' aims, is the same property Chökyi Gyaltsen uses elsewhere to *define* the Mother that the Ornament venerates.

## 4 · Philosophical Exposition & Commentary

**Core meaning.** The root line can be parsed two ways, and so can its Sanskrit behind it. On one parse, "together with the assemblies of śrāvakas and bodhisattvas" goes with "the Buddhas": the Mother is mother *of the Buddhas-with-their-following*. On the other, the assembly (Buddhas included) goes with the Mother: the Mother is the one *accompanied by* Buddhas, śrāvakas and bodhisattvas. Haribhadra's ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ ("encircled") only fits the second parse, because on the first the Buddhas would be the centre of the circle, not part of it. Chökyi Gyaltsen commits to the second parse. The Mother, which he identifies as the three knowledges, sits at the centre, and all four kinds of Ārya surround her as her sons. The paragraph makes three moves: it cites two Indian witnesses, draws a conclusion about what Haribhadra has done with the root, and justifies that conclusion.

**The Indian witnesses.** Both are cited correctly and both are in the Tengyur. Buddhaśrījñāna's Prajñāpradīpāvalī says, in full, [ཉན་ཐོས་ཉིད་དང། བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཉིད་དང། སངས་རྒྱས་ཉིད་ཀྱིས་ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ་དེ་ལྟ་བུ་སྒྲུབ་པར་བྱེད་པ་ཉིད་ཀྱིས་དེ་དག་གི་ཡུམ་ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་དེ་ལ་གུས་པར་ཕྱག་འཚལ་བའོ།](quote:p99-q03): "homage with devotion to their Mother, the Perfection of Wisdom, who, encircled in this way by śrāvakas, bodhisattvas and Buddhas, is the one that accomplishes…". This is the least ambiguous statement of the reading. The Buddhas are the third of three encirclers, and the Mother is "their" (དེ་དག་གི) Mother, of all three. The yig cha abbreviates ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ to ཤེར་ཕྱིན and drops དེ་ལ་གུས་པར. Those are the abbreviations of a quotation made from memory or through an intermediary, not a different text. The Vārttika (Toh. 3788) gives the verse itself in a different translation: [སངས་རྒྱས་ཉན་ཐོས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་ཚོགས་དང་ལྡན་ཡུམ་དེ་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལོ།](quote:p99-q04). Here the Buddhas head the list of the assembly and ལྡན stands immediately before ཡུམ, so on the surface of the Tibetan it is the Mother who is "endowed with" the assembly. The yig cha's ཚོགས་ལྡན is a clipping of this line. The paragraph calls this work ཉི་ཁྲི་རྣམ་འགྲེལ. The wording it quotes matches Toh. 3788 (catalogued in this archive under Bhadanta Vimuktisena), not Ārya Vimuktisena's ཉི་ཁྲི་སྣང་བ, whose root text keeps the Ornament's ཚོགས་བཅས་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་ནི་ཡུམ.

**Where the paragraph comes from.** Almost every step is Tsongkhapa's, from the ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་ཕྲེང. Having cited the same passage of the Prajñāpradīpāvalī, he writes [འགྲེལ་པ་འདི་ནི་རྣམ་འགྲེལ་ལས། སངས་རྒྱས་ཉན་ཐོས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་ཚོགས་དང་ལྡན་ཡུམ་ཞེས་པ་དང་མཐུན་ཞིང་། རྩ་བའི་བཅས་པའམ་ལྡན་པའི་དོན་འགྲེལ་པས་བསྐོར་བ་ལ་བཤད་དེ། རྩ་བར་བཤད་པའི་ཤཱནྟི་པ་སོགས་ཀྱི་བཤད་པ་དང་མི་མཐུན་ནོ། ། དེས་ན་བཅས་ས་དང་ལྡན་ས་བསྐོར་ས་ནི་འདིར་ཡུམ་ལ་གོ་དགོས་སོ། །](quote:p99-q05). In translation: "This commentary [Haribhadra's] agrees with the Vārttika's 'the Mother endowed with the assembly of Buddhas, śrāvakas and bodhisattvas'. The commentary explains the root's 'together with' or 'endowed with' as 'encircled', which does not agree with the explanation of Śāntipa and others, who explain it according to the root. So here the thing that is 'with', 'endowed', 'encircled' must be understood to be the Mother." He continues: [བསྐོར་བའི་དོན་ཡང་ཡུམ་སྲས་ལ་གཙོ་འཁོར་གྱི་ཐ་སྙད་ཡོད་པས་དེ་ཙམ་ལ་བསམས་སོ།](quote:p99-q06). "The sense of 'encircled' is meant only in that Mother and sons can be spoken of as principal and retinue." Chökyi Gyaltsen's རྩ་བའི་ལྡན་པའམ་བཅས་པའི་དོན་འགྲེལ་པར་བསྐོར་བ་ལ་བཤད་པ and ཡུམ་སྲས་ལ་གཙོ་འཁོར་གྱི་ཐ་སྙད are Tsongkhapa's sentences with the verb changed. So are the four sons in his gloss: Tsongkhapa's own paraphrase, just before the passage quoted, already reads སངས་རྒྱས་ཉན་ཐོས་སོགས་པས་རང་རྒྱལ་བྱང་སེམས་ཏེ་སྲས་བཞིས་ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ. The paragraph's first-line gloss is Tsongkhapa's too, lightly reworded. One thing the yig cha leaves out is Tsongkhapa's naming of the alternative, the Śāntipa reading. That silence is worth noticing, and §5 supplies what it leaves unsaid.

**What Chökyi Gyaltsen adds.** Tsongkhapa's justification is a bare convention: mother and sons *can be* called principal and retinue. Chökyi Gyaltsen gives a reason why it applies here: མཁྱེན་པ་གསུམ་པོ་དེས་རང་སྲས་འཕགས་པ་རྣམས་ཀྱི་བཞེད་དོན་ཕུན་ཚོགས་སྒྲུབ་པར་བྱེད་པའི་ཕྱིར. The phrasing is not incidental. It is word for word the definition he gives in his own མཐའ་དཔྱོད on the first chapter of the Mother that is the object of the Ornament's homage: [རང་ལུགས་ནི། རང་སྲས་འཕགས་པའི་བཞེད་དོན་ཕུན་ཚོགས་སྒྲུབ་ནུས་ཀྱི་མཁྱེན་པ་དེ། རྒྱན་གྱི་མཆོད་བརྗོད་ཀྱི་ཡུལ་དུ་གྱུར་པའི་ཡུམ་གྱི་མཚན་ཉིད་ཡིན།](quote:p99-q12). "Own system: a knowledge able to accomplish the excellent intended aims of its own sons, the Āryas, is the definition of the Mother that is the object of the Ornament's homage." So the paragraph is doing more than tidying grammar. It ties Haribhadra's "encircled" to the Sera Jey definition of the Mother. The Mother is the centre of the circle *because* it is what accomplishes the aims of those around it. The principal/retinue relation is read off the definition, not merely allowed by convention. The same text, earlier and at the level of the root verse, had already read the line this way: [འཕགས་བཞིའི་ཡུམ་བྱེད་ཚུལ་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་བསྟོད་པ་ནི། ། ཉན་ཐོས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་ཚོགས་བཅས་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་ནི་ཡུམ་དེ་ལ། ཞེས་པས་འཆད།](quote:p99-q13). The fourth line is "praise by way of how she acts as Mother of the *four* Āryas", which counts the Buddhas among the sons. That is the reading p99 now shows Haribhadra to share.

**Gyaltsab Je's commentary.** The paragraph's last sentence is a mild criticism: the སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན "says this, and does not explain it clearly". The criticism is accurate. Gyaltsab's entire comment on the clause is [སངས་རྒྱས་ཉན་ཐོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ལ་སོགས་པས་ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བའི་བཞེད་དོན་དེ་སྒྲུབ་པར་བྱེད་པ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་དེ་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ་ལོ།](quote:p99-q07), and then he turns to the next topic. His insertion of བཞེད་དོན is useful, and Chökyi Gyaltsen keeps it. But Gyaltsab never says whom the assembly encircles, and his བའི reading leaves the construal open.

**Panchen Sonam Drakpa.** Loseling's textbook shows what that openness allowed. Panchen builds his gloss on Gyaltsab's sentence and fills the gap: [གཙོ་བོ་སངས་རྒྱས་འཁོར་ཉན་ཐོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ལ་སོགས་པས་ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བའི་བཞེད་དོན་དེ་སྒྲུབ་པར་བྱེད་པ་ཉིད་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་དེ་ལ་ཕྱག་འཚལ](quote:p99-q08). "The principal, the Buddha, encircled by the retinue of śrāvakas, bodhisattvas and the rest." In other words, the Buddha is at the centre and the Mother accomplishes the aims of that assembly. A search of the commentaries finds the phrase གཙོ་བོ་སངས་རྒྱས་འཁོར་ཉན་ཐོས in exactly two places: here, and in the sentence of the Golden Rosary where Tsongkhapa rejects it.

**Sera Jey specificity.** On this point the two Gelug yig cha that descend from Gyaltsab's commentary part ways, and Chökyi Gyaltsen is the one who follows Tsongkhapa. The paragraph does not name Panchen, and nothing in it proves Panchen is its target. Its closing complaint that Gyaltsab "does not explain clearly" is about Gyaltsab. The contrast is still there in the texts, and a student at Sera Jey who has read Loseling's gloss would recognise it. For Drepung Gomang I checked Jamyang Zhepa's ཕར་ཕྱིན་མཐའ་དཔྱོད། for ཚོགས་བཅས, ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ and གཙོ་འཁོར. It does not take up the question at this place, so no Gomang position can be reported.

## 5 · Bones of Contention 🦴

**What is disputed?** How to construe the Ornament's ཉན་ཐོས་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་ཚོགས་བཅས་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་ནི་ཡུམ, and whether Haribhadra's ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ reads it the same way the Indian commentators who stay close to the root do. The paragraph contains no ཁ་ཅིག, but the dispute is real and has two layers, one Indian and one Tibetan.

**Who argues what?**

- **Ratnākaraśānti (Śāntipa) and Abhayākaragupta — the Buddha is the centre.** This is the reading Tsongkhapa calls རྩ་བར་བཤད་པའི་ཤཱནྟི་པ་སོགས་ཀྱི་བཤད་པ, and it is in the Tengyur as he says. The Śuddhamatī asks whose Mother she is, and answers: [གང་གི་ཡུམ་ཡིན་ཞེ་ན། ཉན་ཐོས་ལ་སོགས་པའི་ཚོགས་དང་བཅས་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱིའོ།](quote:p99-q09), "Of the Buddha together with the assembly of śrāvakas and the rest." Ratnākaraśānti then raises the obvious difficulty with his own construal and answers it: [གལ་ཏེ་དེ་ལྟར་ན་བུ་དང་ལྡན་པའི་ཆུང་མ་དེ་ནི་བུའི་ཡང་ཆུང་མར་འགྱུར་རོ་ཞེ་ན། བརྗོད་པར་འདོད་པའི་བྱེ་བྲག་གིས་དེའི་སྐྱོན་མེད་དེ། འདིར་ནི་སངས་རྒྱས་ཚོགས་དང་ལྡན་ཞེས་བྱ་བའི་ལྡན་པའི་དོན་ནི་འདིར་ལྷན་ཅིག་པ་ལ་བརྗོད་པར་འདོད་དེ།](quote:p99-q10). The objection runs: if so, a man's wife would also be the wife of the son he is "with". The reply is that by the intended sense ལྡན here means only "together with". Abhayākaragupta's Marmakaumudī construes the line the same way: [ཉན་ཐོས་དང་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་ཚོགས་དང་བཅས་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་རྣམས་ཀྱི་ཡུམ་སྟེ། ཇི་སྐད་དུ་བཤད་པའི་དོན་ཆེན་པོ་ཐོབ་པར་བྱེད་པས་སོ།](quote:p99-q11). He is presumably one of the སོགས in Tsongkhapa's phrase. On this reading the Mother is still the Mother of the whole assembly, but only by extension from the Buddha, and the Buddha is not one of the Mother's encirclers.
- **Buddhaśrījñāna, the Vārttika, and Haribhadra as Tsongkhapa reads him — the Mother is the centre.** The assembly, Buddhas included, surrounds the Mother, and the Mother is equally the Mother of all of them.
- **Tsongkhapa:** Haribhadra sides with the Vārttika against Śāntipa. The "encircled" is the Mother. Glossing it as "Buddha principal, others retinue" and then debating whether pratyekabuddhas belong to a Buddha's retinue is [དམ་བཅའ་ལྟར་སྣང་ལ་རྩོད་པ་ལྟར་སྣང་འགོད་པ](quote:p99-q06), "a pseudo-debate set against a pseudo-thesis".
- **Gyaltsab Je:** restates Haribhadra without deciding.
- **Panchen Sonam Drakpa:** "Buddha principal, others retinue", the gloss Tsongkhapa rejected.
- **Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen:** follows Tsongkhapa, and grounds the principal/retinue relation in his definition of the Mother.

**A point the wording does not settle.** The Tibetan translation of Ratnākaraśānti's own work gives the verse in the Vārttika's form, with ཚོགས་དང་ལྡན་ཡུམ, placing ལྡན right before "Mother". Yet his prose construes it the other way. So the Tibetan word order of the Vārttika line, which the yig cha cites as support, does not by itself decide the construal. The translation of Ratnākaraśānti shows that the same wording could carry the Buddha-centred reading. What decides it for Haribhadra is the verb he chose. ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ cannot be said of a Buddha who is himself one of the encirclers, and Buddhaśrījñāna says explicitly that the Buddhas are among them. The yig cha's argument is sound, but its real weight rests on the ཤེས་རབ་སྒྲོན་མ citation more than on the ཉི་ཁྲི་རྣམ་འགྲེལ one.

**Why does it matter?** For two reasons. First, whether the Buddhas are among the Mother's *sons* decides whether the knowledge of all aspects is a "mother" in the same sense as the other two. On the Śāntipa reading the Buddha is the father-figure at the centre and the Mother's relation to him differs from her relation to the others. That is exactly the asymmetry the wife-and-son objection exposes. On the reading adopted here, all four Āryas stand in one relation to the three knowledges, and that is what lets the Sera Jey definition quoted above cover the whole class with a single formula. Second, it shows the method of this yig cha. When a Tibetan predecessor has glossed a line of Haribhadra without deciding it, Chökyi Gyaltsen goes back to the Indian parallels and to Tsongkhapa, and says in so many words that the predecessor was unclear.

**Resolution.** Sera Jey takes the encircled one to be the Mother, the three knowledges, and the encirclers to be the four sons. Buddhas are included, and pratyekabuddhas are read into Haribhadra's ལ་སོགས་པ. This is justified by the Prajñāpradīpāvalī and the Vārttika, and by the three knowledges' power to accomplish their sons' aims. The Śāntipa reading is not refuted. The paragraph does not mention it. It is set aside as a reading of the root that Haribhadra did not adopt, and Tsongkhapa had already made that point.

**A note on the weight of the evidence.** The case for the reading rests on tier-4 Indian material (Buddhaśrījñāna, Vimuktisena) and on Tsongkhapa (tier 2), supported by Chökyi Gyaltsen's own definition (tier 1). The opposing reading is also tier-4 Indian (Ratnākaraśānti, Abhayākaragupta). The Gelug disagreement rests on one line of Panchen's. It is visible only by comparing glosses; neither yig cha argues the point against the other.

## 6 · Key Terminology & Technical Concepts

- **ཡོངས་སུ་བསྐོར་བ།** — encircled, surrounded as by a retinue.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *parivṛta*. Stated tentatively: the Sanskrit of the Sphuṭārthā was not checked here.
  - **How understood here:** as a relation between a principal and those around it, with the Mother as principal. The word is Haribhadra's replacement for the root's བཅས, and everything in the paragraph follows from taking it seriously as a spatial image with one centre.
  - **Debates:** Ratnākaraśānti's ལྷན་ཅིག་པ ("together with") is the alternative gloss of the underlying ལྡན/བཅས. It yields no centre-and-circle picture and makes the Buddha, not the Mother, the head of the group.

- **ཡུམ་སྲས་ / གཙོ་འཁོར།** — Mother and sons; principal and retinue.
  - **How understood here:** two pairs of terms mapped onto each other. The Mother (the three knowledges) is the principal, and the Ārya "sons" are the retinue. Tsongkhapa allows the mapping as a convention (ཐ་སྙད). Chökyi Gyaltsen justifies it by what the knowledges do for the sons.
  - **Debates:** the "sons" relation itself is contested in Chökyi Gyaltsen's མཐའ་དཔྱོད, for example whether a Mahāyāna path of accumulation not preceded by the lesser path can be a Mother when it has no Ārya son yet. The definition he defends there is the one this paragraph's reason clause presupposes.

- **བཞེད་དོན་ཕུན་ཚོགས་སྒྲུབ་པ།** — accomplishing the excellent intended aims (of one's own Ārya sons).
  - **How understood here:** as the defining function of the Mother that the Ornament venerates, and therefore as what makes her the centre of the circle. For each son, the aim is the result that son's vehicle seeks: the two nirvāṇas for śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, the welfare of the world for bodhisattvas, and the turning of the wheel for Buddhas. These map onto the three lines of the verse glossed in the paragraphs before this one.
  - **Debates:** the word བཞེད་དོན is not in Haribhadra's clause, which has only དེ་སྒྲུབ་པར་བྱེད་པ. It enters with Gyaltsab and is kept by Panchen and Chökyi Gyaltsen.

- **མཁྱེན་པ་གསུམ།** as ཡུམ — the three knowledges identified as "the Mother" of this homage.
  - **How understood here:** the paragraph identifies the Mother as མཁྱེན་པ་གསུམ, not as the Perfection of Wisdom in general. This is consistent with the Sera Jey view that the Mother directly praised in the homage is Ārya knowledge able to bring about its sons' aims, not the textual or resultant Perfection of Wisdom as such. The Prajñāpradīpāvalī's ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ is silently narrowed in the gloss.
