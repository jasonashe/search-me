# Can one ārya be both conventional and ultimate sangha? The ultimate bodhisattva argument

## 1 · Passage & Context

**Source Text:**

> ཁ་ཅིག ། ཀུན་རྫོབ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་དང་། དོན་དམ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་འགལ་ཟེར་ན་མི་འཐད་དེ། བྱང་འཕགས་ཡིན་ན། དོན་དམ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་ཡིན་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། དེ་ཡིན་ན་དོན་དམ་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡིན་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། དོན་དམ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད་ཐོབ་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ལ་དེ་ལྟར་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། འཇུག་པ་ལས། དེ་ནས་བཟུང་སྟེ་དེ་ནི་ཐོབ་པར་གྱུར་པ་ཡི། ། བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཞེས་བྱའི་སྒྲ་ཉིད་ཀྱིས་བསྙད་དོ། ། ཞེས་གསུངས་པའི་ཕྱིར།

**Preceding Section:** p388 answers someone who says nothing is all three refuges at once (སྐྱབས་གསུམ་ཡིན་པ་མེད). Chökyi Gyaltsen offers a single common locus: the mental consciousness of an ārya Buddha. It is the Buddha jewel because it is a Buddha. It is the Dharma jewel because it is a path truth. And it is the sangha jewel because it is an ārya person. That last step rests on the claim that the mental consciousness *is* the person, for which he cites Bhāviveka's རྟོག་གེ་འབར་བ (ཁོ་བོ་ཅག་ཡིད་ཀྱི་རྣམ་པར་ཤེས་པ་ལ་བདག་གི་སྒྲ་དངོས་སུ་འདོགས), the ཤེས་རབ་སྒྲོན་མེ, and Haribhadra's རྒྱན་སྣང. The two paragraphs are one move made twice. p388 shows that the three jewels can share a basis, and p389 shows that the conventional and ultimate halves of one jewel can.

**Following Section:** p390 opens the next heading with གཉིས་པ་ནི and moves from identifying the jewels to why there are three refuges at all. It cites the Uttaratantra's སྟོན་པ་བསྟན་པ་སློབ་དོན་གྱིས། ། ཐེག་པ་གསུམ་དང་བྱེད་གསུམ་ལ། ། and explains the three as posited for three kinds of trainee: Buddha for those of the Mahāyāna lineage, Dharma for pratyekabuddhas, sangha for śrāvakas. So p389 is the last debate of the identification before the text changes subject.

**What this paragraph is doing.** Five paragraphs earlier the yig cha divided the sangha jewel into conventional and ultimate. Here it answers someone who takes that division as a dichotomy, as though nothing could fall on both sides. The reply is a chain of three reasons ending in a verse of Candrakīrti. Every ārya bodhisattva is an ultimate sangha jewel, because every ārya bodhisattva is an ultimate bodhisattva, because every bodhisattva who has attained ultimate mind generation is one. The second half of the proof is left unstated. An ārya bodhisattva is an ārya person, and by the yig cha's own definition the conventional sangha jewel is synonymous with ārya person. So the ārya bodhisattva is the common locus, and the two are not contradictory.

>

<!--line 396-415-->
> **ཞེས་གསུངས་པའི་ཕྱིར།**
>
> — so it is said.

**On the unstated half.** The paragraph proves only that the ārya bodhisattva is an ultimate sangha jewel. It never says the same person is a conventional sangha jewel, and that is what makes the two compatible. The missing premise is the yig cha's own definition, [དང་པོ་དང་། འཕགས་པའི་གང་ཟག་དོན་གཅིག](quote:p389-q04): the conventional sangha jewel is synonymous with ārya person. Chökyi Gyaltsen's mtha' dpyod states the counter-instance outright and names the subject, as shown in §4.

**On the verse.** The yig cha reads ཐོབ་པར་གྱུར་པ་ཡི (genitive). Candrakīrti's root text in the Tengyur reads [ཐོབ་པར་གྱུར་པ་ཡིས](quote:p389-q01) (instrumental), and so does Chökyi Gyaltsen's own quotation of the line in his དབུ་མའི་སྤྱི་དོན. Nothing in the argument turns on it. The verse also does not contain the word དོན་དམ. It says only that from the first ground one who has attained "that" is called a bodhisattva. The pervasion goes through only if "that" (དེ) is read as the ultimate mind generation. Candrakīrti's autocommentary invites that reading, as §4 shows.

## 3 · Structural Context (Dkar Chag)

- **Abhisamayālaṃkāra location:** chapter one, the ten instructions (གདམས་ངག). The lemma is the second of them, [སྒྲུབ་དང་བདེན་པ་རྣམས་དང་ནི། ། སངས་རྒྱས་ལ་སོགས་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ། ། མ་ཞེན་ཡོངས་སུ་མི་ངལ་དང་། །](quote:p389-q15), the instruction on the three jewels, which Gyaltsab Je classes as instruction on the support of practice (སྒྲུབ་པའི་རྟེན).
- **Haribhadra's commentary location:** in the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ Haribhadra quotes the lemma verse and then gives each instruction a one-line gloss. The sangha gloss reads [བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་འཕགས་པ་སློབ་པ་ཕྱིར་མི་ལྡོག་པ་རྣམས་ལ་སྐྱེ་བ་མེད་པ་ཉིད་དུ་འཇུག་པར་བྱའོ་ཞེས་ལུང་འབོགས་པ་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་ལ་འདོམས་པ་དང་།](quote:p389-q14), after listing the twenty sangha. That is all he says. He identifies the sangha of this context as *ārya* bodhisattva learners, which is precisely the class this paragraph is about. But he draws no conventional/ultimate division of the sangha jewel, and the question of whether the two halves exclude each other does not arise in his commentary. The division comes into this yig cha from the Uttaratantra (ཀུན་རྫོབ་/དོན་དམ applied to each of the seven vajra points) and from the Tibetan treatments of that text, not from Haribhadra. The yig cha itself marks this by heading the excursus རྒྱུད་བླ་མ་ལས་གསུངས་ཚུལ.
- **Text location:** the pack puts this under སྐྱབས་གསུམ་ལ་གདམས་པ་ → གསུམ་པ་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་བཤད་པ → ༢༽ སྐྱབས་སྤྱིའི་རྣམ་གཞག་བཤད་པ, which it opens at p388. Reading the yig cha itself suggests that last marker is two paragraphs early. The excursus (སྐབས་འདིར་མཐའ་དཔྱད་པ) divides its Uttaratantra part into four: ཐུན་མོང་མ་ཡིན་པའི་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་ངོས་གཟུང་བ, སྐྱབས་སྤྱིའི་རྣམ་གཞག་བཤད་པ, ཀུན་རྫོབ་པའི་སྐྱབས་དང་དོན་དམ་པའི་སྐྱབས་ཀྱི་ཁྱད་པར, and དཀོན་མཆོག་གི་སྒྲ་དོན. The second is introduced by གཉིས་པ་ནི། རྒྱུད་བླ་མ་ལས། སྟོན་པ་བསྟན་པ… at the start of p390. On that reading p388 and p389 are the closing debates of the *first* subtopic, the identification of the uncommon three jewels, not the opening of the second. I mark this as a discrepancy between the pack's outline and the text rather than a settled correction.
- **Section type:** objection and refutation (ཁ་ཅིག … ཟེར་ན་མི་འཐད་དེ), with the refutation in the spyi don's compressed form. It gives no ཆོས་ཅན and no ཐལ, just the thesis and a stacked chain of reasons ending in scripture.
- **Argumentative role:** it protects the division the yig cha has just set up. It shows that the division is made "by way of how the terms are applied" (སྒྲས་བརྗོད་རིགས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས) and is not a partition of the sangha into two exclusive kinds.

## 4 · Philosophical Exposition & Commentary

**Core meaning.** The opponent's inference is natural. If a jewel divides into conventional and ultimate, surely these are two kinds, and whatever is one is not the other. The reply turns on what kind of division it is. The yig cha introduced it with a qualifier the opponent ignores: [དབྱེ་ན། སྒྲས་བརྗོད་རིགས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས་ཀུན་རྫོབ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་དང་། དོན་དམ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་གཉིས་ལས། དང་པོ་དང་། འཕགས་པའི་གང་ཟག་དོན་གཅིག གཉིས་པ་ནི། འགོག་བདེན་དང་། ལམ་བདེན་ལྟ་བུའོ། །](quote:p389-q04). The ultimate sangha jewel is *illustrated* (ལྟ་བུ) by cessation and path truths, not exhausted by them. This paragraph adds a second kind of illustration, the ārya bodhisattva himself. The ārya bodhisattva is also, as an ārya person, a conventional sangha jewel, so he falls under both headings, and the dichotomy fails.

**The chain of reasons, and where each link comes from.** The first link, ārya bodhisattva ⇒ ultimate sangha jewel, is asserted and supported only by the second. The second, ārya bodhisattva ⇒ ultimate bodhisattva, depends on a category the Indian Prajñāpāramitā commentaries already use. Daṃṣṭrasena's Bṛhaṭṭīkā divides bodhisattvas into conventional and ultimate, and defines the second as [དོན་དམ་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་ནི་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་ས་རབ་ཏུ་དགའ་བ་ལ་སོགས་པ་ས་བཅུའི་སེམས་བསྐྱེད་པ་དང་ལྡན་པ་རྣམས་ནི་དོན་དམ་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཞེས་བྱའོ། །](quote:p389-q03): those who have the mind generations of the ten grounds, beginning with Very Joyful. That is exactly the paragraph's third reason, the bodhisattva who has attained ultimate mind generation. The same division stands under Vasubandhu's name in the ཡུམ་གསུམ་གནོད་འཇོམས and in Jagaddalanivāsin, so it is standard Indian furniture. None of these texts connects it to the sangha jewel. That connection is the yig cha's.

**Why the Madhyamakāvatāra, and what it proves.** The verse is Candrakīrti's, from the first-ground chapter of the Madhyamakāvatāra: [དེ་ནས་བཟུང་སྟེ་དེ་ནི་ཐོབ་པར་གྱུར་པ་ཡིས། །བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཞེས་བྱའི་སྒྲ་ཉིད་ཀྱིས་བསྙད་དོ། །](quote:p389-q01). The pack's search found it only in Tibetan works that quote it (Khedrup Tenpa Dargye, Jamyang Zhepa's Madhyamakāvatāra mtha' dpyod, Chökyi Gyaltsen's own ཀླུ་དབང་གི་རོལ་མཚོ). The Tengyur text was located by direct search, and that is the text it should be cited from. Taken alone, the verse does not say "ultimate". What makes it bear on this paragraph is Candrakīrti's own gloss in the Bhāṣya, which explains why the name belongs from the first ground: [སེམས་དེ་ཐོབ་པ་ནི་རྣམ་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་དུ་སོ་སོའི་སྐྱེ་བོའི་ས་ལས་འདས་པའི་གནས་སྐབས་སུ་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་སྒྲ་ཁོ་ནས་བརྗོད་པར་བྱ་བ་ཡིན་གྱི། རྣམ་པ་གཞན་གྱིས་ནི་མ་ཡིན་ཏེ། དེའི་ཚེ་དེ་འཕགས་པ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་རོ།](quote:p389-q02). "The attainment of that mind" is to be spoken of only by the word bodhisattva, once one has passed the ground of ordinary beings, "because at that time he is an ārya." A few lines earlier, glossing the preceding verse, the Bhāṣya describes the bodhisattva's mind at this ground as the non-dual wisdom that bears the name Very Joyful, which is the natural referent of "that mind" here, though the Bhāṣya does not say so in one sentence. So the Bhāṣya supplies both of the things the paragraph needs. It gives the identity of "that" as the ultimate mind, and the link between the name and āryahood. The paragraph's opening condition, བྱང་འཕགས་ཡིན་ན, is Candrakīrti's དེའི་ཚེ་དེ་འཕགས་པ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར read backwards.

**Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen's fuller statement.** The same debate appears in his སྐབས་དང་པོའི་མཐའ་དཔྱོད, in the form the spyi don compresses: [ཁ་ཅིག དོན་དམ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་དང་། ཀུན་རྫོབ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་འགལ་ཟེར་ན། བྱང་སེམས་མཐོང་ལམ་པ་ཆོས་ཅན། ཀུན་རྫོབ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་མ་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། དོན་དམ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ།](quote:p389-q05). The reason chain and the Madhyamakāvatāra citation follow word for word. Two things are added there that the spyi don omits. First, the subject is named: the bodhisattva on the path of seeing, the lowest ārya bodhisattva, and so the hardest case. Second, a second counter-instance follows at once: [བྱང་སེམས་མཐོང་ལམ་པའི་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་ཡིད་ཀྱི་རྣམ་པར་ཤེས་པ་ཆོས་ཅན། ཀུན་རྫོབ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་མ་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། དོན་དམ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། ཁྱབ་པ་ཁས། མ་གྲུབ་ན། དེར་ཐལ། ལམ་བདེན་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ཏེ། འཕགས་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་མཁྱེན་པ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། འདོད་མི་ནུས་ཏེ། འཕགས་པ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p389-q06). That mental consciousness is an ultimate sangha jewel as a path truth, and a conventional one as an ārya person. This is p388's Bhāviveka thesis put to work on p389's question, and it shows the two paragraphs were built as a pair. The second counter-instance also does not depend on the "ultimate bodhisattva" reasoning at all, only on the yig cha's own illustrations. That makes it the more robust of the two.

**What "ultimate" means here.** Two further passages in the same mtha' dpyod make clear how loosely the qualifier is meant to bind. On ultimate mind generation, he argues from a cessation: [དོན་དམ་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡིན་ན། བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཡིན་དགོས་པར་ཐལ། དོན་དམ་པའི་སེམས་བསྐྱེད་ཡིན་ན། ཐེག་ཆེན་སེམས་བསྐྱེད་ཡིན་དགོས་པའི་ཕྱིར། རྟགས་ཁས། འདོད་ན། བྱང་སེམས་མཐོང་ལམ་པའི་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་འགོག་བདེན་ཆོས་ཅན། དེར་ཐལ། དེའི་ཕྱིར། རྟགས་གྲུབ་སྟེ། དེ་དོན་དམ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་གང་ཞིག རྒྱུ་མཚན་མཚུངས་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p389-q08). A cessation in such a bodhisattva's continuum is an "ultimate bodhisattva", because it is an ultimate sangha jewel, and it is plainly not a bodhisattva. So for Chökyi Gyaltsen "ultimate bodhisattva" and "ultimate sangha jewel" imply each other in the Mahāyāna case, and neither "ultimate X" need be an X. That is the logic of a division by how terms are applied, and it is why the opponent's inference from "two halves" to "exclusive" has no force. Second, he separates this whole vocabulary from the ultimate *refuge*: [ཐེག་ཆེན་གྱི་མཐོང་ལམ་ཆོས་ཅན། སངས་རྒྱས་དཀོན་མཆོག་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། དོན་དམ་པའི་སྐྱབས་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། རྟགས་ཁས། ཁྱབ་སྟེ། རྒྱུད་བླ་མ་ལས། དམ་པའི་དོན་དུ་འགྲོ་བ་ཡི། ། སྐྱབས་ནི་སངས་རྒྱས་ཉག་གཅིག་ཡིན། ། ཞེས་བཤད་པའི་ཕྱིར། རང་ལུགས་ལ། དོན་དམ་པའི་སྐྱབས་དང་མཐར་ཐུག་གི་སྐྱབས་དོན་གཅིག་ཀུན་རྫོབ་པའི་སྐྱབས་དང་གནས་སྐབས་ཀྱི་སྐྱབས་དོན་གཅིག](quote:p389-q09). Ultimate refuge means final refuge, and the Uttaratantra is explicit that the ārya assembly is not that: [སྤང་ཕྱིར་བསླུ་བའི་ཆོས་ཅན་ཕྱིར། །མེད་ཕྱིར་འཇིགས་དང་བཅས་པའི་ཕྱིར། །ཆོས་རྣམས་གཉིས་དང་འཕགས་པའི་ཚོགས། །གཏན་གྱི་སྐྱབས་མཆོག་མ་ཡིན་ནོ། །དམ་པའི་དོན་དུ་འགྲོ་བ་ཡི། །སྐྱབས་ནི་སངས་རྒྱས་ཉག་གཅིག་ཡིན། །](quote:p389-q10). An ārya bodhisattva can therefore be an "ultimate sangha jewel" without being an ultimate refuge. The third subtopic of this very excursus, ཀུན་རྫོབ་པའི་སྐྱབས་དང་དོན་དམ་པའི་སྐྱབས་ཀྱི་ཁྱད་པར, takes up exactly that distinction.

**Gyaltsab Je.** His རྣམ་བཤད་སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན, at the lemma, treats the three jewels as the support of practice and does not divide the sangha. The phrase དོན་དམ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན does not occur in it. Where he does draw the division, in his Uttaratantra commentary, he allocates differently: [དོན་དམ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན་ནི་ཐེག་ཆེན་འཕགས་པའི་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་རིག་པ་དང་རྣམ་པར་གྲོལ་བ་དང། ཀུན་རྫོབ་པ་ནི། ཐེག་པ་ཆེན་པོའི་འཕགས་པའི་ཚོགས་སོ། །](quote:p389-q11). The ultimate sangha is the knowledge and liberation *in the continua* of Mahāyāna āryas, and the conventional sangha is the assembly of those āryas. On this allocation the persons stand on the conventional side and their qualities on the ultimate. Gyaltsab does not say the two are exclusive. But nothing in his wording puts an ārya bodhisattva under "ultimate sangha", which is what p389 asserts.

**Je Tsongkhapa.** The ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་ཕྲེང does not draw the conventional/ultimate division of the sangha jewel. What it does give is the boundary: [འཕགས་པ་དང་སློབ་དཔོན། རིགས་བྱིན་ཤེར་འབྱུང་བུད་དྷ་ཤྲཱི། ཨ་བྷྱ་ཀ་ར་རྣམས་ནི་འཕགས་པའི་དགེ་འདུན་ལ་འབྱེད་པར་བཞེད་དེ། འདི་རྒྱུད་བླ་མར། དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་བཤད་པ་ས་དང་པོ་ཡན་ཆད་ལ་ཐོགས་མེད་ཀྱིས་བཤད་པས་དེ་དང་མཐུན་ནོ། །](quote:p389-q16). Ārya Vimuktisena, Haribhadra and the others read the twenty sangha as the ārya sangha, and this agrees with Asaṅga's placing of the Uttaratantra's sangha jewel from the first ground up. That is the line Candrakīrti's དེ་ནས་བཟུང་སྟེ draws, and it is why a Madhyamakāvatāra verse about the first ground can be pressed into service in a discussion of the sangha jewel.

**Panchen Sonam Drakpa.** His འགྲེལ་ཚིག་དོན་རབ་གསལ is a word gloss and contains no conventional/ultimate division of the sangha; a search for ཀུན་རྫོབ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན in it finds nothing. The Loseling position on this question was not located among the sources available here.

**Sera Jey specificity.** Two things are characteristic. The first is the method. The yig cha lets a division stand only once it has been shown to be non-exclusive, and it proves that by producing a common locus, as p388 did for the three jewels. The second is the substantive step of putting an ārya bodhisattva *person* on the ultimate side. That step is not in Gyaltsab's allocation, Gomang's, or Ngok's (§5). As far as the sources checked here go, it is Chökyi Gyaltsen's own, and he makes it identically in the spyi don and the mtha' dpyod.

## 5 · Bones of Contention 🦴

**What is disputed?** Whether the conventional and ultimate sangha jewels are mutually exclusive. Underneath that is the real question: whether an ārya person can be an ultimate sangha jewel at all, or whether "ultimate sangha" names only the qualities (cessation, path, knowledge and liberation) in such a person's continuum.

**Who argues what?**

- **The ཁ་ཅིག:** they are exclusive. The opponent's best support is given in the mtha' dpyod, which is an argument from parallel: [ཁོ་ན་རེ། ཀུན་རྫོབ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་དང་དོན་དམ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་འགལ་བར་ཐལ། ཀུན་རྫོབ་པའི་ཆོས་དཀོན་མཆོག་དང་དོན་དམ་པའི་ཆོས་དཀོན་མཆོག་འགལ་བའི་ཕྱིར་ཟེར་ན། མ་ཁྱབ།](quote:p389-q07). The conventional Dharma jewel is scripture and the ultimate is cessation and path, and no scripture is a path truth. Why should the sangha be different? Chökyi Gyaltsen answers only "no pervasion". He grants that the Dharma pair excludes each other and denies that the sangha pair must follow. The reason is visible in his own definitions. The conventional Dharma jewel is scripture, a verbal thing, while the conventional sangha jewel is the ārya *person*, and on p388's Bhāviveka thesis the person can be a mind that is itself a path truth. The opponent is not identified by name in any source checked. Whether he is a real predecessor or a stock debating position was not established.
- **Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen:** not exclusive. Every ārya bodhisattva is both, as ārya person and as ultimate bodhisattva, and so is the mental consciousness in his continuum.
- **Gyaltsab Je (Uttaratantra commentary):** divides the Mahāyāna sangha into ultimate (the knowledge and liberation in āryas' continua) and conventional (the ārya assembly). He does not rule on exclusivity. But he assigns persons only to the conventional side, and so offers no support for the paragraph's first reason.
- **Jamyang Zhepa (Gomang):** also calls the division "by way of how the terms are applied", and allocates as Gyaltsab does: [ཡང་སྒྲས་བརྗོད་ཀྱིས་དབྱེ་ན་དོན་དམ་པ་སློབ་པའི་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་འགོག་ལམ་རྣམས་དང་། ཀུན་རྫོབ་པ་འཕགས་པ་སློབ་པ་རྣམས་དང་། ཐ་སྙད་པ་སྔར་བཤད་པའི་སྐུ་སོགས་ཡོད་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p389-q13). The ultimate is the cessations and paths in learners' continua, and the conventional is ārya learners. I did not find him addressing whether the two exclude each other, so the Drepung comparison is one of allocation, not of an explicit counter-thesis.
- **Ngok Loden Sherab:** the sharpest contrast, and the furthest from this curriculum: [ཀུན་རྫོབ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན་དང་། དོན་དམ་པའི་དགེ་འདུན་གཉིས་ལས། དང་པོ་ནི་བྱང་སེམས་འཕགས་པ་མཐའ་དག་ལ་བྱ་ལ། གཉིས་པ་ནི། འཕགས་པ་དེའི་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་ངོ་སྐལ་གྱི་སྤང་བྱ་ལས་གྲོལ་བའི་སེམས་ཀྱི་རང་བཞིན་འོད་གསལ་བ་དེ་ཉིད་དོ། །](quote:p389-q12). *All* ārya bodhisattvas are the conventional sangha, and the ultimate is the luminous nature of their minds freed from their share of what is to be abandoned. He goes on to say that the Uttaratantra's sangha, as one of three, must be the latter alone. On Ngok's division the very class p389 calls "ultimate sangha jewel" is named as the conventional one.

**A note on the weight of the evidence.** The paragraph's own position is attested only in Chökyi Gyaltsen, in two of his own works (tier 1). Every other text that draws the division puts ārya bodhisattva persons on the conventional side or leaves them off the ultimate side. That includes Gyaltsab Je (Sungbum, Gelug), Jamyang Zhepa (tier 5, Gomang) and Ngok (tier 5). None of them states that the two sides exclude each other, so none is a direct opponent. But none supports the first reason either. The Indian support is real but indirect: Daṃṣṭrasena for the category "ultimate bodhisattva", Candrakīrti for tying the name to the first ground and to āryahood. Neither speaks of the sangha jewel. Haribhadra is silent on the division.

**Why does it matter?** The refuge excursus uses "ultimate" in at least three senses: the ultimate *jewel* (a division by how terms apply), the ultimate *refuge* (final refuge, the Buddha alone), and the ultimate *bodhisattva* (from the first ground). Collapsing any two produces absurdities. If ultimate sangha jewel entailed ultimate refuge, every ārya bodhisattva would be a Buddha jewel. If ultimate bodhisattva entailed bodhisattva, a cessation would be a bodhisattva. The paragraph is one of the places where the yig cha keeps these apart.

**Resolution.** Sera Jey holds, with Chökyi Gyaltsen, that the two sangha jewels are not exclusive. The ārya bodhisattva is a common locus: conventional as an ārya person, ultimate as an ultimate bodhisattva. The mind in his continuum is a second common locus: ultimate as a path truth, conventional as a person. The justification is that the division is made by how the terms are applied, so its halves need not be subsets of the sangha jewel, or exclusive of each other. The reader should know that the first counter-instance goes further than Gyaltsab Je's allocation. The second, resting only on the yig cha's definitions and on p388, is the one that would survive a Gyaltsab-style reading.

## 6 · Key Terminology & Technical Concepts

- **སྒྲས་བརྗོད་རིགས་ཀྱི་སྒོ་ནས་དབྱེ་བ།** — a division "by way of how the terms are applied", as against a division of a genus into its kinds.
  - **How understood here:** this is the qualifier that decides the debate. The members of such a division need not be instances of the thing divided. An ultimate sangha jewel can be a cessation, which is not an ārya person, even though "ārya person" defines the sangha jewel. Nor need the members exclude each other. The opponent reads the division as a partition, and the paragraph shows that it is not.
  - **Debates:** Jamyang Zhepa uses the same qualifier (སྒྲས་བརྗོད་ཀྱིས་དབྱེ་ན) for the same division, so the logical type of the division is shared across Sera Jey and Gomang. Where they differ is in what is allowed on the ultimate side.

- **དོན་དམ་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ།** — ultimate bodhisattva.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *pāramārthika-bodhisattva*.
  - **How understood here:** in Daṃṣṭrasena, one who has the mind generations of the ten grounds. In this paragraph it is the hinge term, convertible (in the Mahāyāna case) with "ultimate sangha jewel". In the mtha' dpyod it is not a subset of bodhisattva, since a cessation qualifies.
  - **Debates:** the Indian commentaries use it only to sort bodhisattvas. Carrying it over into the sangha jewel, and letting it include non-persons, is Chökyi Gyaltsen's step.

- **དོན་དམ་སེམས་བསྐྱེད།** — ultimate mind generation.
  - **How understood here:** the non-dual wisdom of the first ground onward, which is what Candrakīrti's Bhāṣya calls "that mind" in the verse. The paragraph reads the verse's དེ as this, and that reading is what lets a verse without the word དོན་དམ support a claim about the ultimate bodhisattva.
  - **Debates:** its definition is argued at length in the mtha' dpyod's section on mind generation, including whether it must be a Mahāyāna mind generation at all. A ཁ་ཅིག there cites the སྒོམ་རིམ to argue that it must, and Chökyi Gyaltsen answers that the pervasion does not hold.

- **དོན་དམ་པའི་སྐྱབས།** — ultimate refuge.
  - **How understood here:** the term the paragraph does *not* use, and must not be confused with. In the own system of the mtha' dpyod it is synonymous with the final refuge (མཐར་ཐུག་གི་སྐྱབས), which the Uttaratantra restricts to the Buddha alone. An ārya bodhisattva is an ultimate sangha jewel and not an ultimate refuge.
  - **Debates:** the distinction between conventional/ultimate refuge and temporary/final refuge is the subject of the excursus's third subtopic, and the mtha' dpyod reports a ཁ་ཅིག who separates them differently.
