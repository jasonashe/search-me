# How the six abhijñās are brought about — the "accomplishing cause" and the Sūtrālaṃkāra verse

## 1 · Passage & Context

**Source Text:**

> གསུམ་པ་ཡོད་དེ། བསམ་གཏན་བཞི་པའི་དངོས་གཞིའི་སེམས་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས། མི་རྟོག་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱིས་བཟུང་སྟེ་རང་རིག་གི་རྣམ་གནས་ཡིད་ལ་བྱས་པས། མངོན་ཤེས་དྲུག་མྱུར་དུ་འགྲུབ་པར་འགྱུར་ཏེ། མདོ་སྡེའི་རྒྱན་ལས། བསམ་གཏན་བཞི་པ་ཤིན་ཏུ་དག་ཐོབ་ནས། ། མི་རྟོག་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཡོངས་སུ་བཟུང་བ་དང་། ། ཇི་ལྟར་རྣམ་གནས་ཡིད་ལ་བྱེད་པ་ལས། ། མཐུ་འགྲུབ་པ་ཡི་དམ་པ་ཐོབ་པར་འགྱུར། ། ཞེས་གསུངས་པ་ལྟར། བསམ་གཏན་གྱི་དངོས་གཞིའི་སེམས་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས། སྣང་བའི་གཟུགས་དང་། སྒྲ་སྣང་སྣ་ཚོགས་པ་དང་། སེམས་ཀྱི་རྣམ་གྲངས་མཐའ་ཡས་པ་དང་། སྔོན་གྱི་ཚེ་རབས་མཐའ་ཡས་པ་དང་། ལུས་ཡང་ཞིང་ཐོགས་པ་མེད་པ་དང་། ཆོས་ཐམས་ཅད་བདེན་པར་མེད་པ་ཡིད་ལ་བྱས་ཤིང་སྒོམ་པས་མངོན་ཤེས་མྱུར་དུ་ཐོབ་པར་འགྱུར་བའི་ཕྱིར།

**Preceding Section:** p419 does two things. Its first and longer part finishes the definitions of the six abhijñās: it rejects a ཁ་ཅིག who would add "together with its concomitant samādhi and prajñā" to the definition of the miracle-abhijñā (there is no prajñā concomitant with a prajñā), defines the other-minds, past-lives, divine-eye and exhaustion abhijñās with their "abiding in the type" (རིགས་སུ་གནས་པ) clauses, argues that the divine eye and ear abhijñās are mental and not sense consciousnesses, distinguishes the divine-eye *abhijñā* (from meditative cultivation in this life) from the divine *eye* among the five eyes (from ripened karma), and places the abhijñās by person and ground. Its closing lines open the fourth heading of the excursus, cause, with a three-fold division — [the common cause, the individual projecting causes, the accomplishing cause](quote:p420-q16) — state the common cause in one line (the actual dhyāna), and give the projecting causes as six verses of the Ratnāvalī: lamps offered at stūpas project the divine eye, music and bells the divine ear, guarding others' minds the other-minds abhijñā, shoes and mounts and honouring the old the miracle abhijñā, Dharma-giving the recollection of lives, and knowing that things lack own-nature the sixth.

**Following Section:** p421 is the fifth heading, the purpose (དགོས་པ). It quotes Gyaltsab Je's རྣམ་བཤད that with the six abhijñās one hears the Dharma taught across endless worlds, knows one's past links with disciples, draws them with miracles and reads their minds, and so completes one's own practice and quickly omniscience; then it argues from the verses of the Sūtrālaṃkāra that follow the one quoted here — the three abidings, the offerings to buddhas, the purification of beings, the light rays, the pure fields — that the abhijñās have special results and special functions.

**What this paragraph is doing:** It gives the third of the three causes, the one that actually brings the abhijñās about in practice: on the basis of a fourth-dhyāna mind, held by non-conceptual gnosis, one attends to the "laid-down aspect" (རྣམ་གནས) proper to each abhijñā. It proves this from the Sūtrālaṃkāra, then restates the method as a list of six objects of attention — one per abhijñā — whose repeated cultivation brings the abhijñās quickly. It is expository, not polemical: there is no ཁ་ཅིག, and nothing is refuted.

**On རང་རིག་གི་རྣམ་གནས.** As printed the phrase reads "the rnam gnas of self-awareness", which has no sense here; the second half of the paragraph, which assigns one object of attention to each abhijñā, shows that what is meant is the rnam gnas *proper to each* abhijñā. The translation follows that sense and marks it as the reading the context requires, not as the words on the page.

**On the verse.** The yig cha's wording of the verse agrees with the text as it stands inside Vasubandhu's bhāṣya (མཐུ་འགྲུབ, ཡོངས་སུ་གཟུང/བཟུང) rather than with the separate Derge kārikā, which reads [བསམ་གཏན་བཞི་བ་… མཐུ་གྲུབ་པ་ཡི་དམ་པ་ཐོབ་པར་འགྱུར།](quote:p420-q01); Tsongkhapa and Ngok both have གྲུབ. The difference is one of spelling and does not affect the argument.

**On the two "dhyānas".** The paragraph names its support twice and differently: the *fourth* dhyāna in its thesis, echoing the verse, and simply "an actual dhyāna" when it restates the method. Section 5 takes this up.

## 3 · Structural Context (Dkar Chag)

- **Abhisamayālaṃkāra location:** the pack announces no lemma for this outline path, so the position has to be argued. The excursus opens at p415 under the heading མངོན་ཤེས་དྲུག་ལ་གདམས་པ, and that is the eighth of the ten instructions of the first chapter, the Ornament's [སྤྱན་ལྔ་དང་ནི་མངོན་ཤེས་ཀྱི། ། ཡོན་ཏན་དྲུག་དང་](quote:p420-q12) — "the six qualities of clairvoyance". Taking the paragraph to comment ultimately on that line is secure; the root verse says nothing about how abhijñās are produced, and nothing in it corresponds to this paragraph in particular.
- **Haribhadra's commentary location:** at the aligned place in the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ, Haribhadra's whole treatment is [one sentence](quote:p420-q13): the six are listed with their objects — miracles such as shaking the earth, sounds great and small in all worlds, others' minds with or without desire, many former lives, all forms, abandonment of the two obscurations — and the bodhisattva is instructed to realize them as primordially peaceful (གཟོད་མ་ནས་ཞི་བ་ཉིད). He raises no question of the mental support, of causes, or of what one attends to in order to generate them. The instruction the sūtra and Haribhadra give is about the abhijñās' *emptiness*; the whole apparatus of this paragraph — support, gnosis, manaskāra — is imported into the Prajñāpāramitā curriculum from the Yogācāra śāstras, the Sūtrālaṃkāra and the Abhidharmasamuccaya. That is the first thing to notice about the passage.
- **Text location:** སྐབས་དང་པོའི་སྤྱི་དོན།, within the excursus on the six abhijñās. Chökyi Gyaltsen lays that excursus out in [six parts](quote:p420-q14): the support, the nature of what is supported, the boundaries (ས་མཚམས), the cause, the purpose and the distinctions. The pack's outline puts this paragraph at མངོན་ཤེས་དྲུག་ལ་གདམས་པ། → བཞི་པ་རྒྱུ་ལ། → ༣༽ འགྲུབ་བྱེད་ཀྱི་རྒྱུ, the third of the [three causes](quote:p420-q16) announced at the end of p419. It is the whole of that sub-heading; p421 moves on to the fifth part.
- **Section type:** own-system exposition (རང་ལུགས), stated as a thesis with a scriptural proof and an elaboration; no opponent.
- **Argumentative role:** it completes the account of cause by supplying what the other two lack. The common cause (the dhyāna) says what the abhijñās need as a basis; the projecting causes (Ratnāvalī) say what past merit disposes one toward each; only this one says what the meditator actually *does*. It also prepares p421, which takes up the next verses of the same Sūtrālaṃkāra chapter.

## 4 · Philosophical Exposition & Commentary

**Core meaning.** The paragraph makes three claims, in the order of the verse. (1) The *support*: an actual dhyāna — the verse says the fourth, "thoroughly pure". (2) The *gnosis* that holds the practice: non-conceptual gnosis, which is what makes this a bodhisattva's power and not a ṛṣi's. (3) The *attention*: one attends to the aspect laid down for each abhijñā, again and again. The six objects of attention make the third claim concrete, and the adverb མྱུར་དུ, used twice, is the paragraph's own contribution: this is the *swift* way.

**The verse in its own text.** In the Sūtrālaṃkāra the verse opens the treatment of the *cause* of the bodhisattva's power (མཐུ), after a verse on its nature and before verses on its results and functions. Vasubandhu's bhāṣya introduces it [as the verse "on the topic of cause" and reduces it to three things: which support, which gnosis, which attention](quote:p420-q02). Chökyi Gyaltsen's heading "accomplishing cause" and his three-limbed thesis reproduce that frame exactly — this is the Sūtrālaṃkāra's own analysis, not a Tibetan gloss laid over it. The next verses in the same chapter, on the results and functions, are what p421 quotes; the yig cha is walking through one Sūtrālaṃkāra section in order.

**What རྣམ་གནས means.** The verse's ཇི་ལྟར་རྣམ་གནས is obscure on its own, and the key to it is in Sthiramati. He glosses it as [the causes by which each abhijñā is obtained, *as laid down in the sūtras*](quote:p420-q04), and gives two examples: whoever wants the divine eye should imagine all directions pervaded by light; whoever wants the miracle abhijñā should imagine the body as light. Asaṅga's Abhidharmasamuccaya uses the same formula in prose: after assigning the supports, it explains [why they are accomplished — "because, in dependence on dhyāna, one attends many times, just as it has been laid down"](quote:p420-q05) (ཇི་ལྟར་རྣམ་པར་གཞག་པ་བཞིན་དུ་ལན་མང་དུ་ཡིད་ལ་བྱེད་པ). The paragraph's སྒོམ་པས — "by meditating on" — is that ལན་མང་དུ.

**Where the list comes from.** The six-item list in the second half of the paragraph is Tibetan, and older than Gelug. Tsongkhapa gives it in the ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་ཕྲེང immediately after the verse: [the first rnam gnas is a light body, the second pervasion by sound, the third …](quote:p420-q07), [the fourth attending backward from the moment of mind just ceased to the linking of rebirth, the fifth the directions pervaded by light, the sixth "phenomena are selfless"](quote:p420-q08). Ngok Loden Sherab has [the same gloss and the same six in almost the same words](quote:p420-q10) in the ལུང་རིགས་ཀྱི་རོལ་མཚོ, so Tsongkhapa is transmitting an eleventh-century Kadam reading rather than composing one. Its first and fifth items are Sthiramati's two examples; the others are filled in on the same pattern.

**What Chökyi Gyaltsen changes.** Set side by side with Tsongkhapa's list, the paragraph does two things silently.

- *It reorders the list.* Tsongkhapa and Ngok run miracle, ear, other minds, past lives, eye, exhaustion — Haribhadra's order. The paragraph runs eye (forms of light), ear, mind, past lives, miracle (light body), exhaustion. That is precisely the order of the Sūtrālaṃkāra's own preceding verse as Vasubandhu glosses it — [knowledge of death and birth, the divine ear, "the varieties of mind" (སེམས་ཀྱི་རྣམ་གྲངས), past abodes, miracle, exhaustion](quote:p420-q18) — and the paragraph's phrase སེམས་ཀྱི་རྣམ་གྲངས is Vasubandhu's word for the third. Chökyi Gyaltsen has aligned the list with the Sūtrālaṃkāra he is quoting, not with the Prajñāpāramitā sūtra's order. Whether this was deliberate or came from a source not in this archive cannot be settled from the texts checked here; the list in exactly his words was not found anywhere else in the commentaries.
- *It restates the sixth object.* Tsongkhapa and Ngok have "all phenomena are selfless" (བདག་མེད). The paragraph has "all phenomena are not truly existent" (བདེན་པར་མེད་པ) — the Madhyamaka formulation this curriculum uses throughout for the ultimate. In a verse drawn from a Cittamātra śāstra and a list inherited from Ngok, that one word places the exhaustion abhijñā's object firmly in the Svātantrika reading of the Ornament. It also answers the Prajñāpāramitā instruction Haribhadra gives — realize the six as primordially peaceful — from the side of cause.
- *It drops Tsongkhapa's inference-based third.* Tsongkhapa's third rnam gnas is the reasoning "this body of mine has this kind of mind, so others with such a body have such a mind"; the paragraph has only "the limitless varieties of mind". The inferential step disappears.

**The meaning of "in dependence on".** The paragraph's བརྟེན་ནས is technical. Tsongkhapa, in the same passage, says that ["depending" means being born as of the nature of that level](quote:p420-q09) — not a bowl resting on a stand, not a sprout from a seed — and that this is an Abhidharma convention to be applied to every mental support of the path. The abhijñā *is* a fourth-dhyāna (or a dhyāna-level) mind; it does not lean on one.

**Gyaltsab Je and Panchen Sonam Drakpa.** Gyaltsab's རྣམ་བཤད་སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན, at the ten instructions, defines the six abhijñās and passes straight to their benefits — the passage p421 quotes. It gives no account of cause and does not cite this verse. Panchen Sonam Drakpa's འགྲེལ་ཚིག་དོན་རབ་གསལ, a word-gloss that follows Gyaltsab, contains neither this verse nor the term རྣམ་གནས anywhere; there is no Loseling statement on this point in the texts checked. The three-cause heading, and with it this paragraph, therefore stands on Tsongkhapa's gser phreng and on the Indian Yogācāra sources, not on Gyaltsab.

**Sera Jey specificity.** Nothing in the paragraph is peculiar to Sera Jey in content: the verse is standard, and the method is Tsongkhapa's (and Ngok's). What is characteristic is the arrangement. Tsongkhapa's treatment has [five parts — support, nature, distinctions, boundaries, purpose](quote:p420-q06) — and quotes the Sūtrālaṃkāra verse under *support*, as evidence that the abhijñās rest on the fourth dhyāna. Chökyi Gyaltsen adds a sixth heading, cause, splits it three ways, and moves the verse there. The effect is to read the verse as Vasubandhu and Sthiramati introduce it — as a verse about cause — rather than as a proof-text for the support.

## 5 · Bones of Contention 🦴

The paragraph states no opponent, but it takes sides on questions that are disputed, and on one of them Gomang says the opposite.

**What is disputed?** (a) What mental support the abhijñās require; (b) whether the verse's བསམ་གཏན་བཞི་པ means "the fourth dhyāna" or "the four dhyānas"; (c) whether the manaskāra account is *the* way abhijñās are produced or one way among others.

**Who argues what?**

- **Asaṅga (Abhidharmasamuccaya):** [the first five on the four pure dhyānas, the rest on the perfected fourth](quote:p420-q05). Only the exhaustion abhijñā is tied to the fourth.
- **Sthiramati:** reads the verse's first line as [the four form-realm dhyānas — first, second, third and fourth](quote:p420-q03). On his reading the verse does not single out the fourth at all.
- **Tsongkhapa:** the first five on any of the four pure actual dhyānas, the sixth on the perfected fourth; nevertheless he glosses the verse's support as "the fourth dhyāna" ([རྟེན་བསམ་གཏན་བཞི་པ](quote:p420-q07)).
- **Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen:** earlier in this same excursus he rules that [the first five arise from whichever actual dhyāna is appropriate](quote:p420-q15) and the sixth from the perfected dhyāna. In this paragraph he states the thesis in terms of the fourth dhyāna and then, in the restatement, of "an actual dhyāna". Read together, the two passages say: any actual dhyāna is sufficient; the fourth, thoroughly pure, is what makes the accomplishment *swift*. The double མྱུར་དུ carries that point, and the paragraph should not be read as retracting the earlier ruling. That this is how the two passages fit is my reading; the yig cha does not say so in words.
- **Jamyang Zhepa (Gomang):** takes exactly Chökyi Gyaltsen's position as a ཁ་ཅིག and [refutes both halves of it](quote:p420-q17): the first five can arise on the first preparatory stage, མི་ལྕོགས་མེད, though with difficulty, and the Kośa's "the paths are easy on the dhyānas, difficult on other levels" implies that the difficult ones still occur, slowly. (His Kośa line was not located in the Tengyur's Abhidharma section under that wording; it is reported here only as his citation.)
- **Ngok Loden Sherab:** after giving the manaskāra list, says it [appears to be *a* method of producing abhijñā](quote:p420-q11), and that the Abhidharma describes another: after attaining the actual dhyāna, one abandons the absorption-obscurations that impede abhijñā by an uninterrupted path and a liberated path.

**Why does it matter?** On (a), the Sera Jey–Gomang difference decides whether a bodhisattva can have the first five abhijñās before gaining any actual dhyāna — a question with consequences for how the path of accumulation is described. The paragraph's word "swiftly" is the point where the two positions meet: Jamyang Zhepa's argument turns on the difference between slow and swift, and Chökyi Gyaltsen's paragraph presents the fourth-dhyāna method as the swift one. On (b), Sthiramati's reading would make the fourth-dhyāna thesis of the paragraph's opening clause unsupported by the verse it cites. On (c), Ngok's remark shows that the Tibetan tradition knew the manaskāra account as one strand among two; the yig cha gives only that strand.

**Resolution.** Sera Jey follows Chökyi Gyaltsen: an actual dhyāna is the necessary support (so the Gomang allowance for the preparatory stage is not accepted), the fourth is the best one, and the manaskāras are the practical cause. The justification is the Abhidharmasamuccaya on the support and the Sūtrālaṃkāra, as Vasubandhu frames it, on the cause. The weakest point is (b): the reading of བསམ་གཏན་བཞི་པ as "the fourth" is Tsongkhapa's and Ngok's, and the one Indian commentator in this archive who glosses the line reads it the other way. The yig cha does not address that.

## 6 · Key Terminology & Technical Concepts

- **རྣམ་གནས།** — the "laid-down aspect": the object of attention prescribed for generating a given abhijñā.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** probably *(yathā)vyavasthāna*; the Sanskrit of the verse was not checked here. Sthiramati and Asaṅga render the idea as རྣམ་པར་གཞག་པ.
  - **How understood here:** each abhijñā has its own — light for the eye, sound for the ear, and so on — which is why the paragraph needs a qualifier (the troubled རང་རིག་གི) to say "proper to each". Tsongkhapa pairs it with ཡིད་བྱེད ("rnam gnas or manaskāra") and treats the two as the same thing.
  - **Debates:** Sthiramati takes the prescriptions to be those found "in the sūtras"; the six-item list itself is attested first here in Ngok.

- **འགྲུབ་བྱེད་ཀྱི་རྒྱུ།** — the accomplishing cause, as against the common cause (ཐུན་མོང་བའི་རྒྱུ) and the projecting causes (འཕེན་བྱེད་ཀྱི་རྒྱུ).
  - **How understood here:** the cause that operates in the practice session itself; the other two are a precondition (the dhyāna) and a karmic disposition (Ratnāvalī's merit). The three-way division is the yig cha's own; it was not found in Tsongkhapa or Gyaltsab.

- **མི་རྟོག་ཡེ་ཤེས་ཀྱིས་བཟུང་བ།** — being held by non-conceptual gnosis.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *nirvikalpajñāna-parigraha*.
  - **How understood here:** Sthiramati glosses it as gnosis that does not conceive of apprehended and apprehender; it is what distinguishes a bodhisattva's abhijñās, which he calls "supreme" (དམ་པ), from those of śrāvakas, pratyekabuddhas and worldly ṛṣis. The paragraph keeps the phrase but does not say whether it requires direct realization of emptiness or can be satisfied at the level of the path of accumulation; that the abhijñās are "held" by it on a Svātantrika reading is consistent with the sixth object, བདེན་མེད.

- **བསམ་གཏན་བཞི་པ་ཤིན་ཏུ་དག་པ། / རབ་མཐའི་བསམ་གཏན།** — the thoroughly pure fourth dhyāna; the "perfected" or "extremity" dhyāna.
  - **How understood here:** the verse's "thoroughly pure" is taken as the fourth dhyāna that serves as the support for special qualities; Tsongkhapa notes that on the Abhidharmasamuccaya's system any dhyāna or formless absorption fit to support special qualities counts as "perfected".
  - **Debates:** Sthiramati reads བཞི་པ as "the four"; he glosses "thoroughly pure" as free of absorptions dominated by craving, pride or doubt, or free of the eight faults (breathing in and out, examination, analysis, pleasure, pain, mental pleasure and mental displeasure).

- **བརྟེན་ནས།** — "in dependence on", said of a mind and its level.
  - **How understood here:** in Tsongkhapa's sense, arising *as* a mind of that level; hence a fourth-dhyāna abhijñā is itself a fourth-dhyāna mind.
