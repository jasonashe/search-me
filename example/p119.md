# Is the Heart Sūtra definitive? — the two-truths definition of drang nges refuted

## 1 · Passage & Context

**Source Text:**

> ཁ་ཅིག ། དོན་དམ་བདེན་པ་དངོས་བསྟན་བསྟན་བྱའི་གཙོ་བོར་བྱས་ནས་སྟོན་པའི་མདོ། ངེས་དོན་གྱི་མདོའི་མཚན་ཉིད་དང་། ཀུན་རྫོབ་བདེན་པ་དངོས་བསྟན་བསྟན་བྱའི་གཙོ་བོར་བྱས་ནས་སྟོན་པའི་མདོ། དྲང་དོན་གྱི་མདོའི་མཚན་ཉིད་ཟེར་ན། ཤེས་རབ་སྙིང་པོ་ཆོས་ཅན། ངེས་དོན་གྱི་མདོ་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། མཚན་ཉིད་དེའི་ཕྱིར། རྟགས་གྲུབ་སྟེ། འཁོར་ལོ་བར་པ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། ཁྱབ་སྟེ། དགོངས་འགྲེལ་ལས། སྟོང་པ་ཉིད་སྨོས་པའི་རྣམ་པས། ཞེས་གསུངས་པའི་ཕྱིར།

**Preceding Section:** p118, which ends by refuting a *different* definition of the
same pair. There the opponent proposed that a sūtra whose taught meaning can be
accepted literally (སྒྲ་ཇི་བཞིན་དུ་ཁས་བླང་དུ་རུང་བ) is a definitive-meaning
sūtra; Chökyi Gyaltsen answered with a sūtra teaching that conditioned things
are impermanent — literally acceptable, yet the closing clause there,
འདུས་བྱས་མི་རྟག་པ་དྲང་དོན་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར།, states a claim nobody in this system
will surrender. The first half of p118 is about something else
again: whether the Buddha turned the three wheels simultaneously.

**Following Section:** p120, where the opponent counter-attacks the *structure*
of the refutation rather than the definition — arguing from the existence of a
provisional sūtra whose principal expressed content is definitive to the
existence of a definitive sūtra whose principal content is provisional — and
then the debate moves from the Heart Sūtra to the Aṣṭasāhasrikā.

**What this paragraph is doing.** It is the second of at least three attempts
in a row to define དྲང་དོན and ངེས་དོན, each stated by a ཁ་ཅིག and each knocked
down by a single counter-instance. The candidate here is a definition by
*subject matter*: definitive if it principally, explicitly teaches ultimate
truth; provisional if it principally, explicitly teaches conventional truth.
The counter-instance is the Heart Sūtra.

>
> The reason is established, because it is [a sūtra of] the middle wheel

<!--line 311-320-->

<!--line 320-383-->
> **དགོངས་འགྲེལ་ལས། སྟོང་པ་ཉིད་སྨོས་པའི་རྣམ་པས། ཞེས་གསུངས་པའི་ཕྱིར།**
>
> the *Saṃdhinirmocana* (དགོངས་འགྲེལ) says "by way of stating emptiness
> (སྟོང་པ་ཉིད་སྨོས་པའི་རྣམ་པས)."

**On the unstated step.** The paragraph gives the ཐལ, the རྟགས་གྲུབ and the
ཁྱབ་པ, and stops. There is no འདོད་མི་ནུས clause — unlike the parallel move at
the end of p118, which spells its absurdity out. This is not an omission: in
this system the Heart Sūtra's provisional status is a settled point, and
supplying it is left to the debater. Section 5 below gives the two witnesses
that supply it.

**On the abbreviation.** སྟོང་པ་ཉིད་སྨོས་པའི་རྣམ་པས། is five syllables cut out
of a long sentence; the full form is given under §4.

## 3 · Structural Context (Dkar Chag)

- **Abhisamayālaṃkāra location:** the maṅgala verses, not the body of the
  treatise. The excursus this paragraph belongs to is attached to the third of
  the three knowledges in Maitreya's opening homage —
  [གང་དང་ཡང་དག་ལྡན་པས་ཐུབ་རྣམས་རྣམ་པ་ཀུན་ལྡན་སྣ་ཚོགས་འདི་གསུངས་པ། །](quote:p119-q08),
  "endowed with which the Sages taught these various [teachings] in all their
  aspects." Sarvākārajñatā is what enables the turning of the wheel; hence a
  general presentation of ཆོས་འཁོར hangs here.
- **Haribhadra's commentary location:** correspondingly, at
  [རྣམ་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་སྐྱེ་བ་མེད་པར་ཐུགས་སུ་ཆུད་པ་གང་དང་ལྡན་ན།](quote:p119-q07) in
  the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ, where Haribhadra glosses that same line and continues
  ཆོས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ་མ་ལུས་པ་རྣམ་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་བསྐོར་བར་མཛད་པ. Haribhadra names the
  turning of the wheel and passes straight on. He raises no question of
  provisional and definitive there, and no Indian commentary on the *Ornament*
  needs to: the drang nges apparatus enters the Prajñāpāramitā curriculum from
  the Saṃdhinirmocana and from Tsongkhapa's ལེགས་བཤད་སྙིང་པོ, not from
  Haribhadra. That the yig cha spends fifteen paragraphs here on a question its
  Indian commentary does not raise is itself worth noticing.
- **Text location:** སྐབས་དང་པོའི་སྤྱི་དོན།, the ཆོས་འཁོར excursus that opens
  at p108 under the lemma རྣམ་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་སྐྱེ་བ་མེད་པ་ཐུགས་སུ་ཆུད་པ་ཞེས་སོགས་ཀྱི་སྐབས་སུ།.
  That excursus divides into ༡༽ མཐའ་ཅུང་ཟད་དཔྱད་པ and ༢༽ རང་ལུགས་བཞག་པ; this
  paragraph is well inside the first. The own-system presentation begins at
  p123, and its third sub-topic is precisely དྲང་ངེས་ཀྱི་དོན — so the positive
  account of what this paragraph is demolishing is deferred by four paragraphs.
- **Section type:** objection and refutation (གཞན་ལུགས་དགག་པ), in the compressed
  form of a single ཐལ་འགྱུར with its reason and pervasion both established
  against the opponent.
- **Argumentative role:** to close off the most natural way of defining drang
  nges — by which of the two truths a sūtra is *about*. The paragraph does not
  argue that the two truths are the wrong topic; it shows that the criterion
  cross-cuts the classification it is meant to generate.

## 4 · Philosophical Exposition & Commentary

**Core meaning.** The opponent's definition is attractive because it lines two
familiar pairs up with each other: ultimate/conventional against
definitive/provisional. The refutation works by finding a text where the two
pairs come apart. The Heart Sūtra is, on anyone's account, a sūtra whose
principal explicit content is ultimate truth — Dharmamitra says so in as many
words in the *Prasphuṭapadā*:
[དེ་ཡང་ཁ་ཅིག་ནི་དོན་དམ་པའི་བདེན་པ་སྙིང་པོའི་དོན་གཙོ་བོར་བྱས་པ་འཕགས་པ་ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པའི་སྙིང་པོ་ལ་སོགས་པའོ། །](quote:p119-q09)
— "some [scriptures] make ultimate truth, the essential meaning, primary: the
noble *Heart of the Perfection of Wisdom* and so forth." Tsongkhapa takes over
that very division in the ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་ཕྲེང:
[སློབ་དཔོན་ཆོས་ཀྱི་བཤེས་གཉེན་བཞེད་པ་ལྟར་ཡུམ་གྱི་བརྗོད་བྱ་ལ་སྙིང་པོའི་དོན་དོན་དམ་པའི་བདེན་པ་དང་། རྒྱ་ཆེ་བ་མངོན་རྟོགས་ཀྱི་རིམ་པ་གཉིས་ལས།](quote:p119-q06)
— the Mother's subject matter is of two kinds, the essential meaning which is
ultimate truth, and the extensive meaning which is the stages of realization;
the first is what the Collections of Reasoning expound, the second what the
*Ornament* expounds. So the reason is not merely established, it is established
by the tradition's own doxography of the sūtras. And yet the Heart Sūtra is
provisional. Hence the definition fails.

**Why the reason is established the way it is.** Note the route the paragraph
actually takes. It does not argue directly that the Heart Sūtra principally
teaches ultimate truth; it argues that the Heart Sūtra is a middle-wheel sūtra
and that middle-wheel sūtras principally teach ultimate truth, with the
Saṃdhinirmocana supplying the second step. The five syllables quoted are cut
from the sentence Gyaltsab Je gives in full at exactly this place in the
*Ornament*'s opening:
[ཐེག་པ་ཆེན་པོ་ལ་ཡང་དག་པར་ཞུགས་པ་རྣམས་ལ་སྟོང་པ་ཉིད་སྨོས་པའི་རྣམ་པས། ཆེས་ངོ་མཚར་རྨད་དུ་བྱུང་བའི་ཆོས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ་གཉིས་པ་བསྐོར་ཏེ།](quote:p119-q03)
— "for those who had entered the Great Vehicle he turned a second, still more
marvellous wheel of Dharma, by way of stating emptiness." Khedrup Je quotes the
same sentence in the same commentarial place in the ཕར་ཕྱིན་རྟོགས་དཀའི་སྣང་བ།,
though to different purpose —
[དགོངས་པ་ངེས་འགྲེལ་ལས་ཀྱང་། སྟོང་པ་ཉིད་སྨོས་པའི་རྣམ་པས་ཆེས་ངོ་མཚར་རྨད་དུ་བྱུང་བའི་ཆོས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ་གཉིས་པ་བསྐོར་ཞེས་དང་།](quote:p119-q05)
— he is fixing which of the three wheels is the "wheel of no characteristics"
against people who assign that name to the third. The line is standard
furniture in this part of the curriculum; what varies is what is loaded onto
it.

**Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen's own view.** The decisive point for reading this
paragraph is that Chökyi Gyaltsen *accepts* the pervasion the opponent is using.
In his own མཐའ་དཔྱོད on this same first chapter, arguing about the three
Mothers, he writes
[འདོད་མི་ནུས་ཏེ། དོན་དམ་བདེན་པ་དངོས་ཀྱི་བསྟན་བྱའི་གཙོ་བོར་བྱེད་པའི་ཕྱིར། དེར་ཐལ། འཁོར་ལོ་བར་པ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p119-q01)
— "that cannot be accepted, because it principally teaches ultimate truth as its
explicit object; because it is a middle-wheel [sūtra]." The inference *middle
wheel, therefore principally teaches ultimate truth* is his own working premise
elsewhere. So the refutation here is not aimed at the Saṃdhinirmocana or at the
classification of the Heart Sūtra by subject matter. It is aimed narrowly at the
identification of that classification with drang nges. Read carelessly, the
paragraph looks like a rejection of the Saṃdhinirmocana pervasion; it is the
opposite — the pervasion is granted, and that is exactly what sinks the
definition.

The same distinction, put positively, is in his general-meaning commentary on
Tsongkhapa's ལེགས་བཤད་སྙིང་པོ:
[འཁོར་ལོ་བར་པ་འདིར་ངོ་བོ་ཉིད་ཡོད་མེད་ཀྱི་དོན་སྒྲས་ཟིན་ལ་མ་བསྟན་ཏེ། འདིར་སྒྲས་ཟིན་ལ་ཆོས་ཐམས་ཅད་རང་གི་མཚན་ཉིད་ཀྱིས་མ་གྲུབ་སྙམ་དུ་བསྟན་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p119-q02)
— in the middle wheel the question of whether things have own-nature is not
taught in what the words literally state (སྒྲས་ཟིན); what the words literally
state is that all dharmas are unestablished by their own characteristic. A
sūtra's topic and a sūtra's literal purport are two different things, and drang
nges is a classification of the second. That sentence is the philosophical
content of this paragraph's refutation, stated by the same author in the text
where it belongs.

**Je Tsongkhapa's perspective.** The ལེགས་བཤད་སྙིང་པོ (དྲང་ངེས་རྣམ་འབྱེད) is not
among the commentaries available here, so its wording reaches us at one remove,
through Jamyang Zhepa's citation — see §5. The ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་ཕྲེང, which is
available, does not run this debate: it treats the Heart Sūtra only in its list
of the eleven "son" sūtras (ཤེར་སྙིང་སྟེ་བཅུ་གཅིག་ནི་སྲས་སོ།) and in the
division of the Mother's subject matter quoted above. That is worth saying
plainly: on this particular question the gser phreng is not the authority the
yig cha is leaning on.

**Gyaltsab Je's commentary.** The རྣམ་བཤད་སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན། gives the whole
Saṃdhinirmocana passage on the three wheels and then immediately observes that
the great masters read it differently, Asaṅga literally:
[འཁོར་ལོ་གཉིས་པའི་མཚན་གཞི་ཤེར་ཕྱིན་གྱི་མདོ་རྒྱས་འབྲིང་བསྡུས་པ་གསུམ་དྲང་བའི་དོན་དུ་འཆད་ཚུལ་ནི།](quote:p119-q04)
— "as for how the three Prajñāpāramitā sūtras, extensive, medium and brief, which
are the instances of the second wheel, are explained as provisional in meaning."
This is the sharpest possible reminder of what is at stake in getting the
definition right. If "principally teaches ultimate truth" meant "definitive,"
then the Saṃdhinirmocana, read literally, would be contradicting itself in
consecutive sentences: it characterises the second wheel by emptiness and calls
it དྲང་བའི་དོན in the same breath. The opponent's definition makes the sūtra
incoherent; Chökyi Gyaltsen's refutation is what saves it.

**Panchen Sonam Drakpa.** The rubric asks for his reading, and here it cannot be
supplied. His འགྲེལ་ཚིག་དོན་རབ་གསལ། is by design a word-gloss on the root text
and Haribhadra's commentary following Gyaltsab Je's རྣམ་བཤད་སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན།; it
carries no ཆོས་འཁོར excursus. The phrase འཁོར་ལོ་བར་པ does not occur in it at
all, and neither does ཤེར་སྙིང. The Drepung comparison below therefore comes
from Gomang, not from Loseling, and that is a limitation of the evidence rather
than a finding about Loseling's position.

**Sera Jey specificity.** There is nothing sectarian in the conclusion — no
Gelug author defines drang nges by the two truths. What is characteristic of
this yig cha is the *form*: a definition is not discussed, it is given one
counter-instance and dropped, and the reader is expected to supply the
unacceptability himself. Four such demolitions run back to back (p118 twice,
p119, p121) before any positive account is offered at p123. A student working
through this section is being trained to test definitions, not to learn a
doctrine.

## 5 · Bones of Contention 🦴

**What is disputed?** Whether the drang nges division of the sūtras can be
defined by which of the two truths a sūtra principally and explicitly teaches.

**Who argues what?**

- **The ཁ་ཅིག position:** yes. Definitive = principally, explicitly teaches
  ultimate truth; provisional = principally, explicitly teaches conventional
  truth. The position has real support behind it — Dharmamitra sorts the
  Prajñāpāramitā corpus by exactly this criterion, and Tsongkhapa adopts his
  sorting.
- **Jetsun Chökyi Gyaltsen:** no. Both limbs fail, and the Heart Sūtra breaks the
  first. Subject matter and literal purport are independent.

**The suppressed premise, and who supplies it.** The paragraph never says why
"the Heart Sūtra is definitive" is unacceptable. Two texts say it. Jamyang
Zhepa, in the ཕར་ཕྱིན་མཐའ་དཔྱོད་རིན་ཆེན་སྒྲོན་མེ།, quotes Tsongkhapa's
ལེགས་བཤད་སྙིང་པོ directly:
[དྲང་ངེས་རྣམ་འབྱེད་ལས། དེ་ཡང་ཤེས་རབ་སྙིང་པོ་ལྟ་བུ་མདོ་དེའི་ནང་དུ་དོན་དམ་པ་དང་བདེན་པའི་ཁྱད་པར་གསལ་བར་མ་སྦྱར་བར་གཟུགས་མེད་ཅེས་པ་ལ་སོགས་པ་བསྟན་པ་ནི་ཇི་ལྟར་བསྟན་པ་དེ་ཙམ་གྱིས་སྒྲ་ཇི་བཞིན་པར་བཟུང་དུ་མི་རུང་བས་གཞན་དུ་དྲང་དགོས་པས་དྲང་དོན་ནོ། །](quote:p119-q10)
— "in a sūtra like the Heart Sūtra, where 'there is no form' and the rest is
taught without clearly attaching the qualifier 'ultimately' or 'truly,' it
cannot be taken literally just as taught; since it must be led elsewhere, it is
provisional in meaning." And Panchen Delek Nyima states the criterion in general
form:
[མདོ་ལ་ཐེག་ཆེན་གྱི་མདོ་དང་ཐེག་དམན་གྱི་མདོ་གཉིས་དང་། དྲང་ངེས་ཀྱི་ཆ་གཉིས་སུ་འདོད་པ་སེམས་ཙམ་པ་དང་མཚུངས་ལ། མཚན་གཞི་མི་འདྲ་བ་ཡིན་ཏེ། སེམས་ཙམ་པས་འཁོར་ལོ་དང་པོ་གཉིས་དྲང་དོན་གྱི་མདོ་དང་། ཕྱི་མ་ངེས་དོན་གྱི་མདོར་འདོད། སྐབས་འདིར་འཁོར་ལོ་ཐོག་མཐའ་གཉིས་དྲང་དོན་གྱི་མདོ་དང་། བར་པ་ལ་དྲང་ངེས་ཀྱི་ཆ་གཉིས་ཡོད་དེ། དགག་བྱ་ལ་དོན་དམ་གྱི་ཁྱད་པར་སྦྱར་བ་རྣམས་ངེས་དོན་གྱི་མདོ་དང་། དེ་ལྟར་མ་སྦྱར་བའི་འཁོར་ལོ་བར་པ་རྣམས་དྲང་དོན་གྱི་མདོར་འདོད་པ་ཡིན་པའི](quote:p119-q11)
— on the Svātantrika account the first and last wheels are provisional, and the
middle wheel has both parts: those that attach the ultimate qualifier to the
object of negation are definitive, those that do not are provisional. The Heart
Sūtra does not attach it. So it is provisional despite being about nothing but
emptiness — which is precisely the case the opponent's definition cannot
accommodate.

**A note on the weight of this evidence.** The two texts that supply the missing
step are the weakest in the pool, both tier 5, and one of them is a Drepung
textbook. That is not a defect in the argument — the Gomang and Sera Jey
positions coincide here, and the words Jamyang Zhepa is quoting are Tsongkhapa's
— but a reader should know that the ལེགས་བཤད་སྙིང་པོ itself is not among the
sources checked here, and that its wording is being taken on Jamyang Zhepa's
report.

**Why does it matter?** Because the whole Prajñāpāramitā curriculum turns on the
claim that the sūtras it studies are the definitive teaching, and that claim
cannot be secured by pointing at their subject matter. If it could, the
Saṃdhinirmocana's own characterisation of the second wheel would settle the
question in one line, and there would be nothing for the Madhyamaka reading to
do. The reason there is a debate at all is that emptiness can be *stated*
without being stated in the way that makes a passage bear its literal reading.
That is the distinction this paragraph is protecting, and it is why the drang
nges question is not decided by counting what a sūtra talks about.

**Resolution.** Sera Jey rejects the definition. The paragraph itself supplies
only the counter-instance; the positive account waits for p123's outline, where
དྲང་ངེས་ཀྱི་དོན is the third heading of the own-system presentation. Until then
the reader has a negative result: whatever drang nges is, it is not a
classification by which truth is principally taught. The reason it is not is
that a sūtra may make ultimate truth its whole subject and still fail to say so
in words that can be taken as they stand.

## 6 · Key Terminology & Technical Concepts

- **དངོས་བསྟན་བསྟན་བྱ།** — the explicitly taught object of exposition, as against
  what a text establishes only implicitly (ཤུགས་བསྟན).
  - **How understood here:** the opponent's definition is carefully worded with
    both དངོས་བསྟན and གཙོ་བོ, so it is already guarding against the obvious
    counter-examples — a text that mentions ultimate truth in passing, or one
    that implies it. The refutation gets past those guards because the Heart
    Sūtra satisfies the definition on its strictest reading. That is what makes
    it a good counter-instance rather than a quibble.
  - **Debates:** the pairing of དངོས་བསྟན with ཤུགས་བསྟན is doing heavy work
    across this whole section; Chökyi Gyaltsen's own ལེགས་བཤད་སྙིང་པོ commentary
    uses ཤུགས་ལ་བསྟན་པ for exactly what the middle wheel does establish about
    own-nature.

- **སྒྲས་ཟིན།** — what the words literally state; the literal purport of a passage
  as against its topic.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** roughly *yathāruta*, the sense carried by
    སྒྲ་ཇི་བཞིན་པ.
  - **How understood here:** this is the term the opponent's definition has no
    room for, and it is the hinge of the whole dispute. Drang nges is a
    classification of སྒྲས་ཟིན; the two truths classify བསྟན་བྱ. The paragraph is
    the demonstration that these two do not coincide.

- **འཁོར་ལོ་བར་པ།** — the middle wheel.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *madhyama-dharmacakra*.
  - **How understood here:** as a *chronological and doctrinal* category fixed by
    the Saṃdhinirmocana, not as a value judgement. Membership in it settles what
    a sūtra is about and settles nothing about whether it is definitive — on the
    Svātantrika account it contains both kinds.
  - **Debates:** whether the middle wheel is definitive at all is the great
    Cittamātra–Madhyamaka fault line; Gyaltsab Je's note that Asaṅga reads the
    three Mothers as provisional is the near edge of it. This paragraph stays
    scrupulously off that ground — it takes only what the Saṃdhinirmocana says
    about how the second wheel teaches, not what it says about the second
    wheel's status.

- **དོན་དམ་གྱི་ཁྱད་པར་སྦྱར་བ།** — attaching the qualifier "ultimately" to the
  object of negation.
  - **How understood here:** the actual Svātantrika criterion of definitiveness
    within the middle wheel, and the thing the Heart Sūtra conspicuously lacks
    when it says གཟུགས་མེད rather than "ultimately there is no form." It is worth
    seeing that this criterion is about *how a sentence is formed*, not about
    what it is about — which is exactly the point the paragraph is making
    negatively.
  - **Debates:** the Prāsaṅgika does not accept this criterion, which is why
    Panchen Delek Nyima gives it under the Svātantrika heading and then turns to
    the Prāsaṅgika system separately. Since the *Ornament* is expounded in this
    curriculum on Svātantrika lines, the Svātantrika criterion is the operative
    one here.
