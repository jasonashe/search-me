# What causes the five eyes? — the six perfections and the legs of miraculous power

## 1 · Passage & Context

**Source Text:**

> བཞི་པ་ནི། མདོ་ལས། བྱང་ཆེན་མིག་ལྔ་ཡོངས་སུ་སྦྱོང་བར་འདོད་པ་དང་། རབ་ཏུ་ཐོབ་པར་འདོད་པས་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་དྲུག་ལ་བསླབ་པར་བྱའོ། ། ཞེས་གསུངས་པ་ལྟར། ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་དྲུག་ལ་སློབ་པ་དང་། རྫུ་འཕྲུལ་གྱི་རྐང་པ་སྒོམ་པ་ལ་སོགས་པ་རྣམས་ཡིན་ནོ། །

**Preceding Section:** p412 (page 305) is the third of the five sub-topics, the boundaries (ས་མཚམས) of the five eyes. It quotes Gyaltsab Je's རྣམ་བཤད་སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན་ — flesh eye from the accumulation path, divine eye from the preparation path, wisdom eye from the meditative equipoise of the path of seeing, dharma eye from its subsequent attainment, the Buddha eye proper at buddhahood and a concordant one from the eighth ground — and glosses it for a Mahāyānist who has not first entered a lesser path. It then brings in the གསེར་ཕྲེང་ to allow the divine eye already on the accumulation path, and closes with a qualification that matters here: these boundaries are stated གཙོ་ཆེ་བའི་དབང་དུ་བྱས — by what is principal — and are not strict limits, since there are persons who have not entered any path yet have the divine eye (anyone with a fourth-dhyāna basis), and śrāvakas who have the dharma eye.

**Following Section:** p414 (page 306) is the fifth sub-topic, the purpose of generating the five eyes. It again quotes the རྣམ་བཤད: with the flesh eye one knows where the spiritual friends and one's disciples are throughout a trichiliocosm; with the divine eye, where disciples die and are reborn; with the wisdom eye, the ultimate nature that is the means of taming them; with the dharma eye, the sharp and dull faculties of disciples; and with even a concordant Buddha eye one knows nearly all objects and so is moved from within to practise "without depending on others" (གཞན་གྱི་དྲིང་མི་འཇོག་པར). It then states this as a syllogism.

**What this paragraph is doing.** It fills the fourth slot of a fivefold enquiry that Chökyi Gyaltsen set up when he opened the investigation of the five eyes — [definition, divisions, boundaries, cause, and the purpose of arising from the cause](quote:p413-q04). The cause is given in two parts: the training in the six perfections, supported by a sūtra quotation, and the cultivation of the legs of miraculous power (རྫུ་འཕྲུལ་གྱི་རྐང་པ), left open with ལ་སོགས་པ. The paragraph is short and argues nothing; its interest lies in where its two causes come from, and in what it quietly drops from its source.

## 3 · Structural Context (Dkar Chag)

- **Abhisamayālaṃkāra location:** the pack announces no lemma for this outline path, so the placement has to be argued. It can be, because the yig cha itself opens this excursus on the words སྤྱན་ལྔ་དང་ནི — [the five eyes as the cause of not depending on others in practice, to be explained by how the sūtra says it, how the treatise comments on it, and an investigation](quote:p413-q05). Those words belong to the first chapter's verse on the ten instructions (གདམས་ངག), [སྤྱན་ལྔ་དང་ནི་མངོན་ཤེས་ཀྱི། ། ཡོན་ཏན་དྲུག་དང་མཐོང་ལམ་དང་། ། བསྒོམ་ཞེས་བྱ་ལ་གདམས་ངག་ནི། ། བཅུ་ཡི་བདག་ཉིད་ཤེས་པར་བྱ། །](quote:p413-q06); the five eyes are the seventh instruction. Maitreya's verse names the five eyes and says nothing about their causes.
- **Haribhadra's commentary location:** the aligned place in the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ is the gloss of the ten instructions, where the five eyes get one clause — [ཤའི་དང་། རྣམ་པར་སྨིན་པ་ལས་བྱུང་བའི་ལྷའི་དང་། ཤེས་རབ་དང་ཆོས་དང་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་གྲངས་ཇི་ལྟ་བ་བཞིན་དུ་དངོས་པོ་སོ་སོར་ངེས་པ་དང་།](quote:p413-q07) — which runs straight into the list of their objects and ends with the instruction to accomplish them as one in suchness (དེ་བཞིན་ཉིད་དུ་གཅིག་པ་ཉིད་དུ་བསྒྲུབ). Having read that passage, I can report that Haribhadra gives the five eyes' objects and nothing on their causes. He does not mention the six perfections, and he does not mention the legs of miraculous power. The one causal word in his gloss, རྣམ་པར་སྨིན་པ, "born of ripening", is attached to the *divine* eye, not the flesh eye. Neither of p413's causes therefore comes from the Indian commentary the Ornament is read through. The first comes straight from the sūtra, and the second, as section 4 shows, reaches the yig cha through Tsongkhapa.
- **Text location:** སྐབས་དང་པོའི་སྤྱི་དོན, under སྤྱན་ལྔ་ལ་གདམས་པ (opens at p404, page 298) → གསུམ་པ་མཐའ་དཔྱད་པ (opens at p410, page 301). The pack labels the heading at p413 "༥༽ རྒྱུ་ལས་སྐྱེས་པའི་དགོས་པ་དང་ལྔ", but that is the tail of the enumeration line. The enumeration itself makes p413 the fourth item, the cause, and p414 the fifth. Read that way the sequence is regular: the definition and divisions come first, then the boundaries (p412), the cause (p413), and the purpose (p414).
- **Section type:** exposition within an investigation (མཐའ་དཔྱད) — a plain statement of the author's own position with a scriptural source, no opponent and no consequence.
- **Argumentative role:** it links the boundaries to the purpose. Having said where on the path each eye is gained, the yig cha says what practice produces them, so that p414 can say what they are for. The sūtra sentence quoted here is, in the Kangyur, directly followed by the sentence that p414's purpose draws on (section 4). Cause and purpose are thus read off two adjacent lines of the same sūtra passage.

## 4 · Philosophical Exposition & Commentary

**Core meaning.** The five eyes are not given by birth or by a single meditation. The bodhisattva gains them by practising the whole bodhisattva path, the six perfections, and, for the eyes that see form at a distance, by developing the concentrations called the legs of miraculous power.

**The sūtra and its wording.** The Kangyur Pañcaviṃśati closes its long five-eyes passage with [ཤཱ་རདྭ་ཏིའི་བུ། དེ་ལྟར་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཆེན་པོ་མིག་ལྔ་ཡོངས་སུ་སྤྱང་བར་འདོད་པ་དང། ཐོབ་པར་འདོད་པས། ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་དྲུག་ལ་བརྩོན་པར་བྱའོ།](quote:p413-q01). The yig cha's quotation differs in two small ways. It has བསླབ་པར ("should train") for the Kangyur's བརྩོན་པར ("should strive"), and it adds རབ་ཏུ before ཐོབ་པར. Chökyi Gyaltsen is consistent about both: when he first lays out the sūtra a few pages earlier, [he gives the sentence in the same form, as the summary (དོན་བསྡུས) of the whole five-eyes passage](quote:p413-q03). Tsongkhapa's གསེར་ཕྲེང has བསླབ་པར without རབ་ཏུ (below), and Haribhadra's eight-chapter recension (ལེའུ་བརྒྱད་མ, in this archive under Seng ge bzang po) agrees with the Kangyur's བརྩོན་པར. The variant changes no doctrine. It does mean the yig cha is quoting the sentence as it circulated in the Tibetan commentaries, not from a Kangyur print. The sūtra then gives its own reason: [ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་ནི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་ཐམས་ཅད་དང། མིག་ལྔ་པོ་འདི་དག་སྐྱེད་པའོ། །མིག་ལྔ་པོ་དེ་དག་ལ་བསླབས་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའ་སེམས་དཔའ་ཆེན་པོ་རྣམས་ནི། བླ་ན་མེད་པ་ཡང་དག་པར་རྫོགས་པའི་བྱང་ཆུབ་ཏུ་མངོན་པར་འཚང་རྒྱའོ།](quote:p413-q02) — "the perfection of wisdom generates all the perfections and these five eyes". The yig cha does not quote this, but it is the sūtra's warrant for reading the preceding sentence causally, not merely as exhortation. An Indian commentary on the Śatasāhasrikā (attributed to Daṃṣṭrasena, tier 4) reads it the same way: [བྱང་ཆུབ་སེམས་དཔའི་སྤྱན་ལྔ་པོ་དེ་དག་ཐོབ་པ་ཡང་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་དྲུག་སྤྱད་པས་ཐོབ་པོ་ཞེས་བསྟན་པ་སྟེ།](quote:p413-q11).

**Where the paragraph comes from.** p412 and p414 are both built on quotations from Gyaltsab Je, and p413 is not, because Gyaltsab gives no cause. His རྣམ་བཤད goes straight from the boundaries to the purpose — [སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་ས་དང་། རྗེས་མཐུན་པ་ས་བརྒྱད་པ་ནས་ཡོད་དོ། ། སྤྱན་ལྔ་པོ་འདི་དང་ལྡན་ན།](quote:p413-q10) — in one breath. For the cause Chökyi Gyaltsen turns to Tsongkhapa, and p413 is a near-verbatim reduction of the གསེར་ཕྲེང (tier 2): [གསུམ་པ་ནི་བྱང་ཆེན་མིག་ལྔ་ཡོངས་སུ་སྦྱོང་བར་འདོད་པ་དང་ཐོབ་པར་འདོད་པས་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་དྲུག་ལ་བསླབ་པར་བྱའོ་ཞེས་གསུངས་པ་ལྟར་ཕྱིན་དྲུག་ལ་སློབ་པ་དང་། ངོ་བོ་ཉིད་མེད་པ་དང་། འགྲེལ་ཆེན་ལས་ཤའི་མིག་ལ་བཤད་པ་ལྟར་རྫུན་འཕྲུལ་གྱི་རྐང་པ་སྒོམ་པ་ཡང་ཡིན་ནོ། །](quote:p413-q08). Set side by side, the two texts differ in three ways:

- Tsongkhapa lists **three** causes: training in the six perfections, ངོ་བོ་ཉིད་མེད་པ ("absence of intrinsic nature", presumably its realization), and cultivation of the legs of miraculous power. Chökyi Gyaltsen keeps the first and the third and covers the middle one with ལ་སོགས་པ.
- Tsongkhapa says the legs of miraculous power are a cause **as the Great Commentary explains for the flesh eye**, so the second cause is tied to one eye only. Chökyi Gyaltsen drops the qualification, and as written p413 lists the legs as a cause of the five eyes in general.
- The "Great Commentary" gloss itself, which Tsongkhapa quotes when he defines the eyes, is [འགྲེལ་ཆེན་ལས་རྫུ་འཕྲུལ་གྱི་རྐང་པ་བསྒོམས་པའི་མཐུས་སྟོང་གསུམ་གྱི་གཟུགས་ཆེ་ཆུང་ཐམས་ཅད་མཐོང་བའི་རྣམ་སྨིན་གྱི་མིག་གྲུབ་པ་ནི་ཤའི་སྤྱན་དང་།](quote:p413-q09) — the flesh eye is the *ripened* eye established by the power of cultivating the legs of miraculous power. In an earlier paragraph Chökyi Gyaltsen attributes the dharma-eye line of the same gloss to the རྒྱན་སྣང, i.e. Haribhadra's Āloka. However, I searched the Āloka as held in this archive (Commentaries and Tengyur TD03791), the Vivṛti (TD03793), and the Śatasāhasrikā commentaries TD03807 and TD03808, and the flesh-eye sentence was **not located** in any of them. The Āloka here has only the same one-clause list as the don gsal. So the Indian source of the second cause is, on the evidence available, unverified. It reaches the yig cha through Tsongkhapa's citation, and I cannot confirm it independently.

**Logical reasoning.** Why should the six perfections cause the eyes? The sūtra's answer (q02) is that the perfection of wisdom generates both the perfections and the eyes. The six perfections are the whole of the path, so every realization on the path, the five eyes included, is their fruit. The legs of miraculous power are a different kind of cause. In Chökyi Gyaltsen's own definition, [the flesh eye is an awareness relying on the flesh eye-organ as its uncommon dominant condition](quote:p413-q16), and the legs of miraculous power are the samādhis whose power transforms that organ. The Great Commentary gloss (q09) says the ripened eye-organ is "established by the power of" cultivating them. The two causes therefore work at different levels: the perfections for the whole set of eyes, and the legs for the special organ underlying the first.

**Historical debate.** Jamyang Shepa (tier 5, Gomang), who sets out the five eyes under the same three heads of definition, boundary and cause, cites the same sūtra and then quotes the གསེར་ཕྲེང explicitly for the cause. Chökyi Gyaltsen takes Tsongkhapa's wording without naming him; Jamyang Shepa names his source. Jamyang Shepa also presses the question Tsongkhapa's "ripened eye" raises. Ārya Vimuktisena's ཉི་ཁྲི་སྣང་བ (Tengyur) had explained the flesh eye's fixed range as [born of ripening, or as the object-possessor of a dominant-condition result](quote:p413-q12). Jamyang Shepa turns that against anyone who takes it literally: [བྱང་སེམས་ཚོགས་ལམ་པའི་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་ཤའི་སྤྱན་ཆོས་ཅན། ལུང་མ་བསྟན་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། རྣམ་སྨིན་གྱི་འབྲས་བུ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p413-q13). A ripened effect is ethically neutral, but the flesh eye of a bodhisattva on the accumulation path is a path and therefore virtuous. He rejects outright the view that the flesh eye is the physical organ ripened from the legs of miraculous power — [ལ་ལས་རྫུ་འཕྲུལ་གྱི་རྐང་པ་བསྒོམ་པའི་རྣམ་སྨིན་གྱི་དབང་པོ་གཟུགས་ཅན་ཟེར་ན། དེ་གཉིས་ཀྱང་མི་འཐད་](quote:p413-q14). His own definition splits the difference: [རང་རྒྱུ་ཕྱིན་དྲུག་དང་། ཐེག་ཆེན་གྱི་རྫུ་འཕྲུལ་གྱི་རྐང་ང་བསྒོམ་པའི་རྣམ་སྨིན་གྱི་རང་ཡུལ་གཟུགས་ཆེ་ཆུང་ཐམས་ཅད་མཐོང་བའི་མིག་དབང་ཁྱད་པར་ཅན་གྱིས་བདག་རྐྱེན་བྱས་པའི་](quote:p413-q15) — the six perfections and the Mahāyāna legs ripen into a special eye-*organ*, and the flesh eye is the awareness for which that organ is dominant condition. That is exactly p413's pair of causes, written into a definition that places each one.

**Sera Jey specificity.** The paragraph itself takes no side in that later argument, but Chökyi Gyaltsen's own definition of the flesh eye (q16) already has the structure Jamyang Shepa makes explicit: an awareness (རིག་པ) that relies on a flesh eye-organ as its uncommon dominant condition. On the yig cha's own terms, then, the natural reading of p413 is Jamyang Shepa's. The legs of miraculous power cause the flesh eye by way of its organ, and the six perfections cause the eyes as path-awarenesses. Unlike Jamyang Shepa, Chökyi Gyaltsen does not spell this out, and I found no passage in which he does. That the two monastic textbooks agree here, with Gomang simply more explicit, is my reading and not something either text states.

## 5 · Bones of Contention 🦴

**What is disputed?** Nobody in these sources denies that the six perfections cause the five eyes; the sūtra says so and the Indian and Tibetan commentators repeat it. The live questions concern the second cause and the list itself:

1. **What the legs of miraculous power cause.** Tsongkhapa ties them to the flesh eye, citing a Great Commentary gloss (q08, q09). Chökyi Gyaltsen's wording drops that restriction. Read strictly, p413 lists them among the causes of all five eyes. Read charitably, the ལ་སོགས་པ and the paragraph's dependence on the གསེར་ཕྲེང imply Tsongkhapa's restriction. The yig cha does not say which, and I mark this as unclear.
2. **How a ripened eye can be a path.** If the flesh eye is the ripening of cultivating the legs (q09), it seems to be ethically neutral like any ripened effect, yet it is counted a path-realization. Jamyang Shepa states the difficulty (q13), rejects a naive answer (q14) and resolves it by putting the ripening in the organ and the eye in the awareness (q15). Chökyi Gyaltsen does not raise it here.
3. **Timing.** p412 puts the flesh eye on the Mahāyāna accumulation path. Chökyi Gyaltsen's own Abhidharma presentation, following the Kośa, [places the legs of miraculous power only from the peak stage of the preparation path](quote:p413-q18), which would make the effect come before its cause. His Mahāyāna presentation in the fourth-chapter spyi don instead has the legs [cultivated principally on the great accumulation path](quote:p413-q17), which is compatible with p412. So the Ornament's path structure, not the Kośa's, must be what p413 assumes. Neither paragraph raises this; it is my inference from the two texts. p412's closing remark that its boundaries are only "by what is principal" leaves room for it in any case.
4. **What ལ་སོགས་པ covers.** Tsongkhapa's third cause, ངོ་བོ་ཉིད་མེད་པ, disappears. One possible reason is that it overlaps with the perfection of wisdom already contained in the six perfections. Another is that it belongs rather with the next sub-point of the same instruction, which Haribhadra gives as accomplishing the five eyes as one in suchness, and which Tsongkhapa glosses directly after his list of causes as realizing them to be one in the suchness free of elaboration. This is speculation, and I found no text that states either reason.

**Who argues what?**
- **Tsongkhapa** (གསེར་ཕྲེང, tier 2): three causes, with the legs tied to the flesh eye on the authority of the Great Commentary.
- **Gyaltsab Je** (རྣམ་བཤད, same Gelug transmission): silent on the cause; he goes from boundaries to purpose.
- **Chökyi Gyaltsen** (this yig cha, Sera Je): two named causes plus ལ་སོགས་པ, with the flesh-eye restriction dropped.
- **Jamyang Shepa** (Gomang, tier 5): the same two causes, credited explicitly to the གསེར་ཕྲེང and built into a definition that sorts organ from awareness.
- **Haribhadra**: silent on causes. **Vimuktisena**: calls the flesh eye ripening-born, which sets up the problem. **Daṃṣṭrasena** (attributed): six perfections as cause, nothing on the legs.

**Why does it matter?** The five eyes are taught in the Ornament as the ground for practising "without depending on others" (p414). If they come from the whole path of the perfections, that independence is the fruit of ordinary Mahāyāna practice. If one of them needs specific concentrations, the independence also depends on samādhi training. The question of ripening matters because a debater who says "the flesh eye is a ripened effect" without Jamyang Shepa's distinction commits himself to a neutral path.

**Resolution.** As far as this yig cha states it, the Sera Je position is the six perfections and the legs of miraculous power, following Tsongkhapa. The most coherent reading of it is: the legs as the cause of the flesh eye's special organ, the perfections as the cause of the five eyes as realizations, and the Ornament's path-mapping of the legs rather than the Kośa's. The yig cha supports that reading through its own definition of the flesh eye (q16) and its fourth-chapter mapping (q17), but it does not state the reading in so many words.

## 6 · Key Terminology & Technical Concepts

- **སྤྱན་ལྔ / མིག་ལྔ:** the five eyes — flesh, divine, wisdom, dharma, Buddha.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *pañca cakṣūṃṣi*
  - **How understood here:** the sūtra uses the ordinary word མིག; the yig cha's definitions use སྤྱན for the awareness and reserve ཤའི་མིག for the physical organ it depends on (q16). This distinction is what lets the paragraph's two causes be placed.
  - **Debates:** whether the flesh eye is the organ or the awareness; Jamyang Shepa rejects the organ reading (q14).
- **ཡོངས་སུ་སྦྱོང་བ / རབ་ཏུ་ཐོབ་པ:** "purifying" and "fully attaining" the five eyes — the two aspirations the sūtra names.
  - **How understood here:** the pair suggests two phases: bringing an eye to its purified form, as in the sūtra's recurring formula of each eye ཡོངས་སུ་དག་པ, and attaining it. The yig cha's reading has རབ་ཏུ, which the Kangyur lacks.
- **ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་དྲུག་ལ་བསླབ་པ:** training in the six perfections.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *ṣaṭpāramitāśikṣā*
  - **How understood here:** a general cause. The sūtra grounds it in the perfection of wisdom's generating all the perfections and the eyes (q02).
  - **Debates:** none among these sources; the Kangyur's བརྩོན་པ versus the commentarial བསླབ་པ is a textual variant, not a doctrinal one.
- **རྫུ་འཕྲུལ་གྱི་རྐང་པ:** the legs (bases) of miraculous power, four samādhis (of aspiration, effort, intention and analysis), the third group of the thirty-seven factors of awakening.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *ṛddhipāda*
  - **How understood here:** a specific cause, which in Tsongkhapa's source is tied to the flesh eye through the ripening of a special eye-organ (q08, q09).
  - **Debates:** where on the path they begin (Kośa: peak of preparation, q18; Ornament as read by Chökyi Gyaltsen: principally the great accumulation path, q17); and whether their ripened effect can be a virtuous path (Jamyang Shepa, q13–q15).
- **རྣམ་སྨིན་གྱི་མིག:** the "ripened eye" of the Great Commentary gloss.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *vipāka-cakṣus* (reconstructed)
  - **How understood here:** the special organ, produced as the ripening of prior cultivation, on which the flesh eye depends.
  - **Debates:** Haribhadra attaches ripening to the divine eye (q07), and Vimuktisena attaches it to the flesh eye (q12). Jamyang Shepa's position is that ripening belongs to the organ, not to the path-awareness (q13–q15).
