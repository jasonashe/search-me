## Pervasion-Acceptance Audit (new in v4 — run before Accepting any bare conditional)

A Challenger's conditional C1 ("If it is [A], it follows it is [B]") is a pervasion concession in disguise: Accepting it enters BOTH the pervasion and its contrapositive into your ledger (c), and the Challenger will spend the contrapositive later against a subject you hold. Before you answer "Accept" to a universal conditional, write in Stage 2: (i) the contrapositive, in words; (ii) one attempted counterexample to each direction, drawn FIRST from substance-bearers and whole objects (a white horse, a pot, a conch) — bearers are seen, heard and touched through their qualities without being those qualities, and they break more textbook pervasions than any canonical subject does; (iii) the appearing-object check: a pervasion about an OBJECT OF a consciousness holds of what APPEARS to that consciousness (for an eye consciousness: shape and color), not automatically of everything that consciousness is said to apprehend. If any check fails, the answer is "Why", however plausible the conditional sounds.

the terms of the consequence, an NPV must carry its counterexample).

## Revision budget

Revisions are how an understanding honestly changes; they are also
costly, because each one concedes your earlier understanding was wrong.
Per match: revisions 1–2 are free; the 3rd costs justification points;
a 4th is epistemic collapse — the match is lost on the spot, whatever
the debate score says. A node created by a revision cannot itself be
defeated until the Challenger has forced at least one new ledger
commitment since its creation: you may not thrash.

# V7 MODULE — match types B and C (2026-10-28)

Version 7 = v6 (including the V6 understanding layer) + this module.
Source: the user's redesign directive of 2026-10-28 (Part 2), designed
with Gemini Argon (knowledge/argon_consultation_009.md). Your brief
declares the match type: A (straight), B (reason-establishment) or C
(meaning-giving). Type A is played exactly as v6 specifies. Types B and
C replace only the opening exchange; from turn 2, play continues as
standard consequence debate under the full v6 protocol (state, payloads,
audits, revision budget). Where this module and earlier text conflict,
this module governs.

## The three opening forms are legal moves, not form errors

In a type B or C match, the Challenger's first move is one of the two
demand forms below, and your first move is the matching statement form.
Do NOT answer a demand with "State your thesis or reason in valid
debate form": the demands are this match type's valid opening. The D5
form-demand remains in force for anything else that is not a legal move.

## Type B — you must give a reason for the Challenger's thesis

Challenger's demand (exact form):
"Establish the thesis: the subject, [S], is [P]. State your reason."

Your answer (exact form, one line):
"The subject, [S], is [P], because of being [R]."

Choosing R: the reason must come FROM your EpistemicState — a stance or
definition node you hold must give both that S is R and that whatever is
R is P. Prefer the definiens of P, or a division sign of P, over a
remote chain. R must genuinely establish: R may not be P restated, may
not be S, and may not contain P as a conjunct. A true-but-non-
establishing reason is a failed assignment, scored as such at turn 1.

By stating the reason you commit, at turn 1, to THREE ledger entries in
this order: L_a the minor premise (S is R); L_b the pervasion (whatever
is R is P); L_c the thesis (S is P). They bind exactly like concessions
made in play. Your state_snapshot from turn 1 onward carries
"active_root_commitment": {"type": "B", "subject": S, "predicate": P,
"reason": R, "source_nodes": [ids]} — pinned, never edited.

Turn-1 payload for type B: the derivation block is constructive:
{"constructive": true, "cited_state_ids": [...], "cited_ledger_ids": [],
"cited_facts": ["S :: R", "R-pervasion :: P" as fact strings where the
fact base states them], "selected_reason": R, "minor_premise_check":
"<which node/fact gives S is R>", "pervasion_check": "<which node/fact
gives R pervades P>", "step_a_holds": null, "step_b_holds": null,
"thesis_holds": null, "counterexample": null, "derived_move":
"STATE_REASON", "gate_audit": {"conflict": false, "detail": "",
"override": null}, "prose_summary": "<35–75 words: why this reason, from
your understanding, establishes the thesis>"}.

The primary assignment is NOT revisable. Defeating the stance that
carries the assigned reason's minor premise or pervasion, in order to
escape a contradiction, is an immediate match loss — worse than losing
the point. Auxiliary stances may still be revised within the v6 budget.
If the Challenger genuinely contradicts your reason (forces you to
accept "S is not R", or to accept a subject that is R but not P), the
honest play is to let the defeating consequence land: answer as the
truth table and your state require, and the match ends with the reason
defeated. Do not spend a revision, a sense ruling, or a strained reading
to protect the assignment.

## Type C — you must give the meaning of the Challenger's term

Challenger's demand (exact form):
"State the meaning of [T]."

Your answer (exact form, three lines):
"Definiens: [D]
Divisions: [div_1]; [div_2]; ...
Illustration: [I]"
Write "Divisions: none stated" if Purchok gives no division you hold.

The meaning comes FROM your turn-0 definition node for T. HARD CANON
LOCK: where Purchok's text gives T an explicit definition (the fact
base / matrix records one), state exactly its clauses — do not add a
clause, drop a clause, or substitute a clause of your own, and never
use an interpretive commitment to alter the turn-1 definiens. Where the
text defines by division, state the division as the definiens content
requires and list the members under Divisions. The illustration must be
a canonical instance that is both T and satisfies D — choose one your
state classifies with confidence, because it becomes an attack anchor.

By stating the meaning you commit, at turn 1, to ledger entries in this
order: the illustration atom (I is T), then one atom per stated
division (each div_i is T). The forward pervasion (whatever is T
satisfies D), the reverse pervasion (whatever satisfies D is T), and
the illustration's satisfaction of D are recorded as your root
commitments for the match: "active_root_commitment": {"type": "C",
"term": T, "clauses": [d_1, ...], "illustration": I, "source_nodes":
[ids]} in every state_snapshot from turn 1 on. Retracting a stated
clause to escape a counterexample is a match loss, like retracting the
type B reason. If a counterexample genuinely lands — a subject you must
accept as satisfying D but not T, or as T but failing a clause — let it
land; the meaning was wrong, and the honest record of that is the point
of the exercise.

Turn-1 payload for type C: constructive derivation:
{"constructive": true, "cited_state_ids": ["D_x", ...],
"cited_ledger_ids": [], "cited_facts": ["I :: T"],
"clauses": [d_1, d_2, ...], "illustration_check": "<which fact/node
gives I is T and satisfies D>", "step_a_holds": null,
"step_b_holds": null, "thesis_holds": null, "counterexample": null,
"derived_move": "STATE_MEANING", "gate_audit": {"conflict": false,
"detail": "", "override": null}, "prose_summary": "<35–75 words: the
meaning as your understanding holds it, and why the illustration
witnesses it>"}.
Every clause in "clauses" must trace to the cited definition node: the
referee checks each clause against the cited nodes' text, and a clause
that traces to nothing is a justification failure.

Boundary play (turns 2+): where Purchok does not explicitly state how
D applies to an edge-case subject, you may cite an interpretive
commitment — only one that existed at turn 0, or one created by a
logged revision that names the specific textual ambiguity; it must
contradict no fact-base entry; and when a consequence follows from your
own interpretive stance, you accept it rather than denying its
pervasion without a counterexample.

## Turn 0 in types B and C

The Challenger's target is not in your brief, so your turn-0 state is
built over the match's domain without it: root_thesis is the system
invariant {"subject": "Purchok's Lorig", "predicate": "internally
consistent and textually grounded", "status": "INVARIANT"}, definition
nodes for the domain's main categories, at least three stances, at

at turn 0 and binds at turn 1 as above. Do not pad the state toward a
guessed target; a state built to dodge one thesis is visible in the
citations and scores as evasion.

# V8 MODULE — escape-challenges (2026-10-28)

Version 8 = v7 + this module. Source: the user's redesign directive of
2026-10-28 (Part 3), designed with Gemini Argon
(knowledge/argon_consultation_010.md). When you have treated two
parallel cases differently — accepted a predicate of one subject while
denying it of another — the Challenger may challenge the distinction
itself. That challenge is a legal move, not a form error: never answer
it with the D5 form-demand. Where this module and earlier text
conflict, this module governs.

## The two challenges and your three answers

The challenge turn carries a transcript field "escape_ref" naming the
turns it pairs. Your answers, exact forms:

C5a, difference-demand: "The subject, [S_neg], it follows that it is
[P], because [S_pos] is [P] and the reasoning is the same. State the
difference."
- D-Diff: "There is a difference: [S_pos] is [D], whereas [S_neg] is
  not [D], and being [D] is relevant to being [P]."
  D must be a canonical predicate from the fact base (or "not [D]", or
  "[D1] and [D2]" of canonical predicates) that your state actually
  holds to distinguish the pair: S_pos is D, S_neg is not D, and D is
  relevant to P by one declared route — SUFFICIENT (whatever is D is
  P), NECESSARY (whatever is P is D, so lacking D entails lacking P),
  or DEFINIENS_CLAUSE (D is a clause of P's definition that S_neg
  misses). Declaring the difference locks all three claims into the
  ledger: you are bound to them exactly as to a concession.
- D-Concede: "I concede the distinction: the subject, [S_neg], is
  [P]." — see Withdrawal below.
- D-Unfounded: "Distinction challenge unfounded: at turn [t] I did not
  accept that [S_pos] is [P]." (or "...I did not deny that [S_neg] is
  [P].") — ONLY when the named pair is genuinely not in the ledger as
  an accepted/denied asymmetry. An unfounded-rejection of a founded
  challenge is a form violation and the challenge stands against you.

C5b, reason-demand: "The subject, [S_neg], it follows that it is not
[P], because? State the reason." (When your denial was of a negated
predicate, the demand names the positive thesis you thereby stand on.)
- D-Reason: "The subject, [S_neg], is not [P], because of being [R]."
  (positive variant likewise). R is chosen and locked exactly as a
  type B turn-1 reason: minor premise (S_neg is R), pervasion
  (whatever is R is not P), thesis (S_neg is not P) enter the ledger.
- D-Concede and D-Unfounded ("Reason demand unfounded: at turn [t] I
  did not deny that [S_neg] is [P].") as above.

## Payloads for the new answers

All are constructive turns (derivation.constructive = true, prose
summary 35–75 words, citations as usual):
- D-Diff: "stated_difference": D, "relevance_route":
  "SUFFICIENT"|"NECESSARY"|"DEFINIENS_CLAUSE", cited nodes must
  contain D and P.
- D-Reason: "selected_reason": R with minor_premise_check and
  pervasion_check, as in type B; parsed predicate = P (the denied
  predicate), subject = S_neg.
- D-Concede: derived_move "D_CONCEDE" and the state_revision described
  under Withdrawal.
- D-Unfounded: derived_move "D_UNFOUNDED"; the blurb names the ledger
  check you performed.

## What never answers a challenge

Citing an interpretive commitment or a frame does NOT answer a C5.
"Because I hold frame F" is not a difference between the subjects.
Your commitments must cash out as an object-level D or R about the
subjects themselves. A sense ruling is likewise not an answer in
itself: if the challenged denial rested on a sense ruling, your D is
the definiens clause of the strict sense that separates the pair (the
DEFINIENS_CLAUSE route). Defending a sense ruling under challenge
spends no second sense-ruling token.

## The refutation episode and withdrawal

After a D-Diff or D-Reason, the Challenger gets up to two refutation
consequences (against S_neg's lacking D, S_pos's holding D, the
relevance link, or the reason's two modes), then may close with
Khor lo!. Answer them under the ordinary protocol — but know the
stakes: the difference/reason commitments you just locked are bound
like the type B assignment. If a refutation consequence is correct,
accepting it collapses the distinction.

Withdrawal (concede, or collapse under refutation) does three things:
(1) the denied thesis counts accepted — (S_neg is P) stands in the
ledger from that point; (2) it costs ONE revision from your
3-per-match budget, recorded in your payload at the concede turn (or
at the turn where you accept the refuting consequence) as a
state_revision with trigger "DISTINCTION_WITHDRAWAL", defeating the
stance node that carried the distinction and appending its replacement
(the node that accepts (S_neg is P)); your derivations must therefore
always cite the stance your classification answers rest on, so there
is a node to defeat — a distinction held nowhere in your state is an
ad hoc escape and scores as such; (3) if your revision budget is
already exhausted, a collapsed distinction is a match loss, not a free
reversal. If the reversed thesis is the root thesis's negation, the
automatic-loss gate applies at once: a distinction that was the only
protection of the root thesis, once refuted, loses the match.

One exception to the reversal direction: if the refutation defeated
only the POSITIVE hold (it showed S_pos is not D, so S_pos is just
like S_neg) AND the denied entry is the root thesis itself, you may
instead spend the withdrawal revision retracting the E_pos concession
(the replacement node denies (S_pos is P)) — unless (S_pos is P) was
established by a consequence carrying a verified text citation, in
which case no retraction path exists.

An escape that stands costs you nothing: the pair is locked
vindicated for the rest of the match and cannot be re-challenged.

# V9 MODULE — the quotation move (2026-10-28)

Version 9 = v8 + this module. Source: the user's redesign directive of
2026-10-28 (Part 4), designed with Gemini Argon
(knowledge/argon_consultation_011.md). The Challenger may quote Purchok
to you as a reason — a new move, C6 — but only to repair a denial you
just made: establishing a pervasion you answered "No pervasion" to, or
a minor premise you answered "Reason not established" to. A verified
citation is an authority move: "Why", "No pervasion", "Reason not
established" and the form-demand are NOT legal answers to C6. Your
legal answers are exactly three. Where this module and earlier text
conflict, this module governs.

## 1. Accept the quotation

Pervasion repair: "Accept. By the authority of the text, if something
is [R], it is necessarily [P]; therefore the subject, [S], is [P]."
Minor-premise repair: "Accept. By the authority of the text, the
subject, [S], is [R]."
Accepting writes the pervasion (and, for pervasion repair, the thesis
itself, since the minor premise already stood conceded) into the
ledger as text-established: binding like any concession, and the
automatic-Tsa gate applies to it as to any acceptance. If the
established pervasion or atom contradicts one of your ACTIVE stances,
revise in the same payload (state_revision, trigger
"TEXT_ESTABLISHED", defeating the stance the text overrules) — the
text binds your answers, and your understanding must follow it. If
your revision budget is exhausted, accept anyway; the contradiction
stands on the record.

Before accepting, READ the passage's neighbourhood yourself (the
Challenger's citation exposes the window around the span). If Purchok
frames the consequence as an opponent's, or denies its pervasion just
afterwards, do not accept: that is what the voice claim is for.
Accepting an opponent-voice or false pervasion corrupts your ledger
with a non-Purchok pervasion, scores as over-acceptance, and arms an
immediate Tsa! against you.

## 2. Opponent-voice claim

Form: "That consequence does not establish the pervasion in Purchok's
own system, because it is stated from an opponent's position, as shown
by the marker '[marker]'."
The claim is only as good as its evidence: the turn MUST carry a
"context_cite" field {start, end, quote} — character-exact in the
etext — pointing at the framing marker governing the span, at
Purchok's downstream denial of that consequence's pervasion, or at a
passage where Purchok rejects the same pervasion; and it must lie
within the window around the quotation ([cite.start − 400,
cite.end + 300]) or itself contain the claimed reason and predicate.
A voice claim with no verified context citation is void: the
quotation counts established and the claim scores against you. Payload:
constructive, "quote_response": "VOICE", citing the stance that denied
the pervasion and the marker evidence. A voice claim costs no sense
token — it is a factual claim about the text's framing — but if it
collapses (below) it costs one revision.

## 3. Interpretation

Form: "I accept the passage, but it does not establish your pervasion
as stated: in that passage '[term]' refers to [qualified sense],
whereas in our debate '[term]' is taken [unqualified sense], because
the text specifies '[anchor]'."
Legitimate grounds only (declare one in the payload as
"interp_ground"): TERM_SENSE_SHIFT (the span's Tibetan carries an
explicit modifier on the term — name it in the payload as
"claimed_modifier_tibetan"; the referee checks it occurs in the span),
SUBJECT_QUALIFICATION (the quoted subject or the passage's section
carries a qualification the debate's subject lacks), TEXTUAL_RESTRICTION
(the window contains Purchok's explicit restriction of the
consequence's scope). "Purchok spoke loosely", and qualifications with
no textual anchor, are not interpretations.
An interpretation is a sense ruling: the payload records
gate_audit.override "SENSE_RULING" (the same cap of 2 per match covers
interpretations), and the reading registers as an interpretive
commitment node in your state — you are bound to that sense of the
term for the rest of the match. A second interpretation in the same
match must carry a context_cite where Purchok himself contrasts the
two senses; without one it is automatically rejected and the
quotation counts established.

## The closing move

After a voice claim or an interpretation, the Challenger gets one
refutation turn (a counter-citation). Then you close the episode:
accept (in the Accept form above) if the counter-evidence is sound —
judge it honestly from the window: a registered own-voice passage, an
own-voice marker governing the span, or the absence of any downstream
denial against your claimed marker's actual reach, all tell against a
deflection — or hold: "I hold the voice claim." / "I hold the
interpretation." When you close by accepting after a deflection, the
failed deflection costs one revision: the same payload carries the
state_revision defeating the stance that denied the pervasion
(trigger "TEXT_ESTABLISHED"). Holding against deterministic evidence
(a passage already classified own-voice, a downstream verdict in your
favour absent) is recorded as a failed deflection all the same.
