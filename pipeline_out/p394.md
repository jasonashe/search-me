# Was Śākyamuni Mandhātṛ? Identity across lives, and whether the respondent's Buddha exists now

## 1 · Passage & Context

**Source Text:**

> གཞན་ཡང་། སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་དེ་རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུས་ཀྱི་དུས་སུ་རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུས་སུ་ཐལ། རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུས་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པའི་དུས་སུ་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། འདོད་ན། དེ་ཆོས་ཅན། དེའི་དུས་སུ་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པའི་རྒྱུ་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། དེའི་དུས་སུ་རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུས་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། འདོད་མི་ནུས་ཏེ། དེའི་དུས་སུ་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་ཁྱོད་ཀྱི་རྒྱུ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར། ཞེས་ཟེར། ཁ་ཅིག ། མདུན་གྱི་སྐྱེས་བུ་ལན་འདེབས་པ་པོ་དང་རྒྱུད་གཅིག་ཏུ་གྱུར་པའི་སངས་རྒྱས་འཕགས་པ་དེ་ད་ལྟར་མེད༑ ཟེར་ན། དེ་ད་ལྟར་ཡོད་པར་ཐལ། དེའི་འབྲས་སྐྱབས་མཐར་ཐུག་ཏུ་གྱུར་པའི་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ་པོ་དེ་ད་ལྟར་ཡོད་པའི་ཕྱིར།

**Preceding Section:** p393 (page 282) has two parts. The first ends the defence of the thesis that governs this whole stretch: no Buddha is of a different continuum (རྒྱུད་ཐ་དད) from a Mahāyāna accumulation-path bodhisattva, because every sentient being is "of one continuum" with every Buddha Ārya. Someone objects that on this view a śrāvaka arhat would have to be a person preceded by a śrāvaka arhat's realisation. Chökyi Gyaltsen answers that the pervasion fails, using a parallel: Indra is of one continuum with Śākyamuni, since Śākyamuni once took birth as Indra, yet that does not make Indra someone certain to attain Śākyamuni's state. The second part of p393 states a positive claim: King Mandhātṛ (རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུས) is, at the present time, the Teacher Śākyamuni. A ཁ་ཅིག argues that Mandhātṛ would then simply *be* Śākyamuni, and gets "no pervasion". The thesis is then proved: at Śākyamuni's time Mandhātṛ is Śākyamuni, because at that time he has become him (དེ་དེའི་དུས་སུ་དེར་གྱུར་པའི་ཕྱིར).

**Following Section:** p395 (page 283) opens with an opponent turning the p393 thesis around. Mandhātṛ, as a sentient being, would at the present time have to be a sentient being, since whatever is a Buddha is a Buddha at the present time. The answer is a parallel: take "the Buddha Ārya who is the respondent-become-Buddha". Next a ཁ་ཅིག claims that whatever is a person's resultant refuge (འབྲས་སྐྱབས) does not exist at that person's time. The reply: the Buddha Ārya would then not exist at the time of a contemporary accumulation-path bodhisattva, since it is that bodhisattva's resultant refuge. So p395 continues p394's second half directly.

**What this paragraph is doing:** It does two things in 550 characters. First (to ཞེས་ཟེར།) it reports, without answering, a further attack on the p393 claim that Mandhātṛ is now Śākyamuni. The attack converts the claim: if Mandhātṛ is Śākyamuni at Śākyamuni's time, then Śākyamuni is Mandhātṛ at Mandhātṛ's time. Śākyamuni would then be, at that time, his own cause. Second (from ཁ་ཅིག) it refutes a new opponent, who holds that the Buddha Ārya "of one continuum with the respondent in front of us" does not exist now. The refutation uses the doctrine of the resultant refuge: that person's ultimate resultant refuge, the Three Jewels, exists now, so that Buddha exists now.

**On the unanswered objection.** The first half ends ཞེས་ཟེར། with no ན and no reply. The spyi don records the objection and goes straight on to the next ཁ་ཅིག. Whether the objection is being left as a standing difficulty or the reply is being left to the mtha' dpyod cannot be settled from the spyi don alone. The mtha' dpyod does answer it (§4), so the second reading is the more likely, but that is an inference.

**On ཁྱོད་ཀྱི་རྒྱུ.** In the last reason of the first half, ཁྱོད (the subject, Śākyamuni) sits in the genitive and Śākyamuni is also the grammatical subject of the reason. Read literally, it says Śākyamuni is at that time a cause *of Śākyamuni*. That is exactly the self-causation the objector means to force. The mtha' dpyod's parallel wording (§4) confirms this reading.

## 3 · Structural Context (Dkar Chag)

- **Abhisamayālaṃkāra location:** the ten instructions (གདམས་ངག་བཅུ) of the first chapter, at the third of them: [སྒྲུབ་དང་བདེན་པ་རྣམས་དང་ནི། ། སངས་རྒྱས་ལ་སོགས་དཀོན་མཆོག་གསུམ། །](quote:p394-q10). The root text gives the instruction on the Three Jewels one line and nothing more. It raises no theory of refuge and says nothing about causal and resultant refuge, continua or time.
- **Haribhadra's commentary location:** at the aligned place Haribhadra glosses the Buddha jewel as an instruction to know [omniscience as not apprehended](quote:p394-q09). He then deals with the Dharma as lacking inherent nature, and with the Saṅgha as the twenty kinds of irreversible learners, the arhat of the Buddha jewel excepted. Everything in p394 is absent from him: the causal/resultant distinction, "one continuum", Mandhātṛ, and whether a future Buddha exists now. Nor do Gyaltsab Je's or Tsongkhapa's comments at the same lemma raise them; both use it to lay out the ten instructions and to introduce the twenty Saṅgha. The whole controversy is Tibetan, built on the Uttaratantra's account of refuge (which the spyi don cites a page earlier) rather than on the Ornament or its Indian commentary.
- **Text location:** the pack gives the path སྐྱབས་གསུམ་ལ་གདམས་པ། (opens p376, page 273) → གསུམ་པ་དགེ་འདུན་དཀོན་མཆོག་བཤད་པ། (opens p383, page 277) → ༢༽ སྐྱབས་སྤྱིའི་རྣམ་གཞག་བཤད་པ། (opens p388, page 280). p394 is the seventh paragraph of that general presentation of refuge. It falls in the debate section following the Uttaratantra verse on the three refuges, and before the spyi don turns to which refuge is ultimate (the Dharma-as-raft passage a few pages on).
- **Section type:** objection and response. It is a reported objection left unanswered, followed by a ཁ་ཅིག … ཟེར་ན refuted with a single consequence.
- **Argumentative role:** both halves defend the p393 package. That package holds that no Buddha is of a different continuum from a sentient being; that a past life of Śākyamuni (Mandhātṛ) is *now* Śākyamuni; and that one's resultant refuge can be something that already exists. The first half tests the identity-across-time claim against the causal relation. The second half turns the resultant-refuge doctrine against someone who takes "the Buddha of my own continuum" to be only future.

## 4 · Philosophical Exposition & Commentary

**Core meaning.** The paragraph is about how temporal qualifiers ("at the time of X", "at present") combine with identity across a single life-stream. Two claims are in play.

*(a) Mandhātṛ and Śākyamuni.* In p393 Chökyi Gyaltsen asserts that Mandhātṛ is at present Śākyamuni. The objector reasons that identity-at-a-time should convert, so Śākyamuni at Mandhātṛ's time was Mandhātṛ, and Mandhātṛ was a cause of Śākyamuni. Śākyamuni would then have been a cause of himself. The spyi don gives no answer. The mtha' dpyod, running the identical exchange, does. There the root is conceded (རྩ་བར་འདོད་ན), and so is the reason that Śākyamuni is at that time Śākyamuni's cause (རྟགས་ཁས). What is refused is the final step: [དེའི་དུས་སུ་སྟོན་པ་ཤཱཀྱ་ཐུབ་པ་དེ་ཁྱོད་ཀྱི་རྒྱུ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་ན། རྟགས་མ་གྲུབ་བོ། །](quote:p394-q01). The distinction is one of scope. "Śākyamuni is, at Mandhātṛ's time, a cause of Śākyamuni" puts the temporal qualifier on the predicate: what Śākyamuni *was* at that time is causally prior to what he is now. "At that time, Śākyamuni is your cause" puts Śākyamuni *as such* into that earlier time as an existent cause, and that is denied. The next debate in the mtha' dpyod makes the stakes explicit. The objector draws production from self (བདག་སྐྱེ): at that time Śākyamuni would arise from a cause one in nature with himself. The reply refuses that link, and insists that there is [no possible time at which Śākyamuni becomes his own cause, because he is selfless](quote:p394-q02). So the same person-series can be "Mandhātṛ" at one time and "Śākyamuni" at another. Because nothing in it is a self, this never amounts to one thing producing itself. I have reconstructed this scope reading from the pattern of what is conceded and what is denied; the texts do not name the distinction.

*(b) The respondent's Buddha.* The ཁ་ཅིག says the Buddha Ārya of one continuum with the respondent does not exist now. The spyi don does not give his reason. The mtha' dpyod does: [the Buddha is future, since the respondent will only awaken in the future](quote:p394-q03). Its reply is a parallel. By the same reasoning the Buddha of one continuum with *any* accumulation-path bodhisattva would not exist now, then no Buddha Ārya at all would exist now, and then every present person would have to be a sentient being. The spyi don's reply is shorter and doctrinal. The ultimate resultant refuge of that person, the Three Jewels, exists now; so the Buddha of one continuum with him exists now. This works only because of the p393 thesis, restated a few lines earlier: [a Buddha Ārya must be of one continuum with an accumulation-path bodhisattva, because every sentient being is](quote:p394-q05). "The Buddha of one continuum with the respondent" is therefore not a private future Buddha. It is any Buddha Ārya, Śākyamuni included, and Buddhas exist now. The mtha' dpyod makes the same bridge explicit with Mandhātṛ himself: Śākyamuni is [both a causal and a resultant refuge](quote:p394-q04), the latter because he is Mandhātṛ's ultimate attainment (ཐོབ་བྱ་མཐར་ཐུག). p395 then distinguishes this from "the Buddha who is the respondent-become-Buddha", which does not yet exist. That distinction is what keeps the position from collapsing into the absurdity the mtha' dpyod pins on the opponent.

**Historical debate.**

- *Chökyi Gyaltsen* (tier 1). The spyi don and the mtha' dpyod run the same two debates in the same order and nearly the same words. The mtha' dpyod is the fuller of the two: it supplies the reply to the first half and the opponent's reason in the second.
- *The Uttaratantra framework as the spyi don reads it.* The spyi don's own gloss on the Uttaratantra verse defines the terms: [the resultant refuge is the Buddha one is oneself to attain; the causal refuge is a Buddha already accomplished in another's continuum](quote:p394-q06). The paragraph's closing reason uses the first half of that gloss, "one's own attainment". On this account "resultant" names a relation to the practitioner, not a time; it does not mean "not yet existing".
- *Jamyang Shepa* (tier 5; Drepung Gomang). He agrees on precisely the Mandhātṛ case: [Śākyamuni is the resultant refuge of Prabhāsa and Mandhātṛ, being their ultimate attainment, and still a causal refuge for his disciples](quote:p394-q07). So the two monastic houses do not divide here; both treat resultant refuge as something already existing in the person of Śākyamuni.
- *Ngok Loden Sherab* (Sangphu, 11th c.). His vocabulary is older and differently weighted. Those seeking definite goodness go for refuge to [the Three Jewels that can arise in their own continuum in the future](quote:p394-q08), while those seeking higher rebirth go to the jewels already accomplished in another continuum. His "future, own-continuum" jewels are exactly what the ཁ་ཅིག takes the respondent's Buddha to be. I found no passage in which Ngok draws the ཁ་ཅིག's conclusion (that the Buddha of one continuum with the respondent does not now exist). The link is my inference about where the intuition comes from, not an attribution of the refuted view to Ngok.
- *Indian sources.* Haribhadra is silent (see §3), and the Ornament gives only the one-line instruction. Gyaltsab Je's རྣམ་བཤད་སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན and Tsongkhapa's ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་ཕྲེང do not take up the question at this lemma either.

**Logical reasoning.** Both halves hinge on the same move: an expression of the form "X at the time of Y" does not place X-as-such at that time. In (a) that move blocks the slide from "Śākyamuni was at that time a cause of Śākyamuni" to self-production. In (b) it blocks the slide from "the respondent will become Buddha in the future" to "the Buddha of one continuum with the respondent is future". This works because "one continuum" is taken so widely (every sentient being with every Buddha) that the Buddha in question need not be the respondent's future Buddhahood at all.

**Sera Jey specificity.** The distinctive element is the p393 thesis on which the second half depends: there is no Buddha of a different continuum from a sentient being. It has a consequence the spyi don accepts without comment. Its own Uttaratantra gloss speaks of a causal refuge "accomplished in another continuum" (གཞན་རྒྱུད), yet the debate denies any Buddha "of a different continuum" (རྒྱུད་ཐ་དད). The text evidently does not treat "another continuum" and "a different continuum" as equivalent, but it never says how they differ (see §5). On the Mandhātṛ case itself Gomang (Jamyang Shepa) concurs.

## 5 · Bones of Contention 🦴

**What is disputed?** Two things. (1) Whether identity across lives (Mandhātṛ *is now* Śākyamuni) commits one to self-causation once the temporal clauses are converted. (2) Whether "the Buddha Ārya of one continuum with the respondent" is something future and non-existent, or something that exists now.

**Who argues what?**

- *The objector of the first half* (unnamed; a ཁ་ཅིག carried over from p393). He holds that "A is B at B's time" converts to "B is A at A's time", and that this makes Śākyamuni his own cause. The mtha' dpyod pushes the same objector on to production from self ([q02](quote:p394-q02)).
- *Chökyi Gyaltsen.* The spyi don leaves that objection unanswered. The mtha' dpyod concedes the conversion and the causal claim with the temporal qualifier on the predicate, and denies that Śākyamuni *at that time* is a cause of himself ([q01](quote:p394-q01)). This is a genuine difference in how much the two books of the same author say, though not a difference of doctrine. A reader who had only the spyi don would not know which link Chökyi Gyaltsen denies.
- *The ཁ་ཅིག of the second half.* He reasons from "the respondent will awaken in the future" to "his Buddha is future" ([q03](quote:p394-q03)). The underlying picture, that resultant refuge means jewels yet to arise in one's own continuum, is closer to Ngok Loden Sherab's wording ([q08](quote:p394-q08)) than to the spyi don's. I have not found it stated as a thesis by any named master.
- *Chökyi Gyaltsen, and Jamyang Shepa with him.* The resultant refuge is one's ultimate attainment, and Śākyamuni is already that for Mandhātṛ ([q04](quote:p394-q04), [q07](quote:p394-q07)).

**A tension left open.** The spyi don defines the causal refuge as a Buddha "accomplished in another's continuum" ([q06](quote:p394-q06)). Yet a few lines later it refutes the view that the causal refuge is jewels "of a different continuum" from the bodhisattva, on the ground that no such Buddha exists ([q05](quote:p394-q05)). The mtha' dpyod refutes the same view in almost the same words. The two can be reconciled only if གཞན་རྒྱུད here means something like "someone else's mental continuum, as a present stream" while རྒྱུད་གཅིག / རྒྱུད་ཐ་དད track the wider relation that lets Indra, Mandhātṛ and every sentient being count as "one continuum" with a Buddha. Neither text states this. I mark it as unresolved rather than supply a gloss.

**Why does it matter?** The resultant refuge is what a Mahāyāna practitioner commits to attaining. If it were only future, that practitioner's refuge would have no existing object, and "going for refuge to the resultant jewels" would be refuge in nothing. The first half matters as a guard: making Mandhātṛ *now* Śākyamuni must not smuggle in a persisting self that produces itself.

**Resolution (Sera Jey).** Mandhātṛ is at present Śākyamuni, and Śākyamuni was at Mandhātṛ's time a cause of Śākyamuni. It does not follow that Śākyamuni at that time was his own cause, and no production from self follows, because the person is selfless. The Buddha of one continuum with the respondent exists now, since the respondent's ultimate resultant refuge exists now. What does not yet exist is the Buddha who is the respondent-become-Buddha (p395).

## 6 · Key Terminology & Technical Concepts

- **རྒྱུད་གཅིག / རྒྱུད་ཐ་དད (one continuum / different continuum):** as used in p393–p395, a relation wide enough that every sentient being is "one continuum" with every Buddha Ārya, and Indra and Mandhātṛ with Śākyamuni.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *ekasaṃtāna / bhinnasaṃtāna* (the Tibetan here stretches the ordinary sense).
  - **How understood here:** the second half turns on it. "The Buddha of one continuum with the respondent" can be Śākyamuni, so it exists now.
  - **Debates:** its relation to the spyi don's own གཞན་རྒྱུད་ལ་གྲུབ་ཟིན་པ is unstated (§5).
- **འབྲས་སྐྱབས་མཐར་ཐུག (ultimate resultant refuge):** the Three Jewels as the final attainment (ཐོབ་བྱ་མཐར་ཐུག) of a given person.
  - **How understood here:** a relation to a practitioner, not a date. Śākyamuni is already Mandhātṛ's resultant refuge.
  - **Debates:** Ngok frames the resultant jewels as those that "can arise in one's own continuum in the future". Chökyi Gyaltsen and Jamyang Shepa both allow a presently existing Buddha to be one.
- **རྒྱུའི་སྐྱབས (causal refuge):** in the spyi don's gloss, a Buddha already accomplished. The mtha' dpyod argues it is compatible with being someone's resultant refuge.
- **X ཀྱི་དུས་སུ (at the time of X):** the temporal qualifier whose scope the first half turns on. "A is, at X's time, B's cause" is conceded; "at X's time, A is A's cause" is not.
- **བདག་སྐྱེ (production from self):** the unwanted consequence the mtha' dpyod draws out of the first half's objection and refutes by selflessness (བདག་མེད).
- **མདུན་གྱི་སྐྱེས་བུ་ལན་འདེབས་པ་པོ (the person in front of us, the respondent):** the debate-ground's stock example of an ordinary sentient being who will later awaken.

*Sources not located.* The mtha' dpyod elsewhere cites a sūtra line identifying the Buddha with Mandhātṛ in a past life (མདོ་ལས། … རྒྱལ་པོ་ང་ལས་ནུས་སུ་གྱུར་ཏོ). A search of the Vinaya volumes of the Kangyur found Mandhātṛ narratives but not that wording, so the scriptural basis of p393's premise is not given here. The paragraph itself quotes nothing canonical.
