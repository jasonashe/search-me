# The five eyes named — how Haribhadra's gloss is divided into name, meaning and manner

## 1 · Passage & Context

**Source Text:**

> གཉིས་པ། དེའི་དོན་འཆད་པ་ལ། སྤྱན་ལྔ་དང་ནི། ཞེས་གསུངས། དེ་ཉིད་ཚིག་དོན་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་འཆད་པ་ལ། ཤའི་མིག་དང་། ཞེས་སོགས་གསུངས། འདི་ལ་ཚིག་དོན་དངོས་དང་། མཇུག་བསྡུ་བ་གཉིས་ལས། དང་པོ་ལ། མིང་གི་སྒོ་ནས་གདམས་པ་དང་། དོན་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་གདམས་པ་དང་། ཇི་ལྟར་གདམས་པའི་ཚུལ་གསུམ་ལས། དང་པོ་ནི། འདི་ལྟ་སྟེ། ཤའི་མིག་དང་། ལྷའི་མིག་དང་། ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་མིག་དང་། ཆོས་ཀྱི་མིག་དང་། སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་མིག་གོ ། ཞེས་གསུངས་པའི་དོན་འཆད་པ་ལ། ཤའི་མིག་དང་རྣམ་པར་སྨིན་པ་ལས་བྱུང་བའི་ལྷའི་དང་ཤེས་རབ་དང་ཆོས་དང་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན། ཞེས་གསུངས།

**Preceding Section:** p404 opens the seventh of the ten instructions (གདམས་ངག་བཅུ). It asks how the instruction on the five eyes should be explained — the eyes being, in its own phrase, the cause that lets a bodhisattva practise without depending on anyone else (སྒྲུབ་པ་གཞན་གྱི་དྲིང་ལ་མི་འཇོག་པའི་རྒྱུ). It then divides the topic three ways: how the sūtra teaches it, how the śāstra explains it, and an analysis (མཐའ་དཔྱད་པ). Under the first it gives the sūtra. Śāriputra is told that there are bodhisattvas who wish to attain and purify the five eyes. The five are listed, and Śāriputra asks what the purified flesh eye is.

**Following Section:** p406 begins the second member of the division announced here, "by way of meaning", and says it has five parts, one for each eye. Its first part pairs the sūtra passage on the flesh eye with Haribhadra's gloss on it: the flesh eye sees a hundred leagues, and so on up to a great trichiliocosm. The gloss is གྲངས་ཇི་ལྟ་བ་བཞིན་དུ་དངོས་པོ་སོ་སོར་ངེས་པ་དང་།. The divine, wisdom, dharma and buddha eyes follow in the next paragraphs, each paired the same way.

**What this paragraph is doing:** it is a hinge. It moves from sūtra to śāstra, first to the root line and then to Haribhadra's gloss on that line. It divides Haribhadra's gloss into its parts — the word-meaning proper (name, meaning, manner) and a conclusion. It then carries out the first part only: it sets the sūtra's bare list of five eyes beside the opening words of Haribhadra's sentence, which name the same five. Nothing is argued here. What the paragraph contributes is a reading of how Haribhadra's single long sentence is built.

>
> [Haribhadra] says: "the flesh eye, and the divine [eye] born of ripening (རྣམ་པར་སྨིན་པ་ལས་བྱུང་བ), and the wisdom, dharma and buddha eyes (སྤྱན)."

**Two words for "eye".** The sūtra says མིག throughout. Haribhadra uses the honorific སྤྱན and lets that one noun, at the end, govern all five genitives. The yig cha's own later analysis turns this lexical difference into a technical one (§4, §6).

**A variant in the lemma.** The yig cha cites Haribhadra as ཤའི་མིག་དང་. Haribhadra's text has bare ཤའི་ — in the Tengyur edition of the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ, [ཤའི་དང། རྣམ་པར་སྨིན་པ་ལས་བྱུང་བའི་ལྷའི་དང། …སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན](quote:p405-q03) — and the Commentaries edition reads the same. So does Chökyi Gyaltsen's own companion mtha' dpyod, which cites the lemma as [ཤའི་དང་རྣམ་པར་སྨིན་པ་ལས་བྱུང་བའི།](quote:p405-q14). The paragraph's མིག may have been carried over from the sūtra line it has just quoted. For its sense, see §5. I note the difference and do not propose a reading.

**A paraphrase in the neighbour.** The sūtra as p404 gives it has the bodhisattvas "wishing" (འདོད་པ) to attain and purify the eyes. In the Twenty-five Thousand as the Kangyur has it, they simply [attain and purify the five eyes](quote:p405-q02), with no "wishing". The Hundred Thousand and Eighteen Thousand read the same at the corresponding place. The wording with འདོད་པ was not located in any of the three. The list of five that this paragraph quotes matches the Kangyur exactly.

## 3 · Structural Context (Dkar Chag)

- **Abhisamayālaṃkāra location:** the verse on the ten instructions in chapter one. The five eyes are the seventh instruction: [ལམ་ནི་ཡོངས་སུ་འཛིན་པ་དང། །སྤྱན་ལྔ་དང་ནི་མངོན་ཤེས་ཀྱི། །ཡོན་ཏན་དྲུག་དང་མཐོང་ལམ་དང། །བསྒོམ་ཞེས་བྱ་ལ་གདམས་ངག་ནི། །བཅུ་ཡི་བདག་ཉིད་ཤེས་པར་བྱ། །](quote:p405-q01) (Tengyur, Toh. 3786). The instructions are the second of the ten topics that characterise the knowledge of all aspects. The pack gives no lemma for this paragraph. I have placed it myself: the paragraph quotes སྤྱན་ལྔ་དང་ནི། as its root text, and that string occurs in the *Ornament* only in this verse.
- **Haribhadra's commentary location:** the gloss on the ten instructions in the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ (Tengyur, Toh. 3793). There the five eyes take a single clause of one long sentence: [ཤའི་དང། རྣམ་པར་སྨིན་པ་ལས་བྱུང་བའི་ལྷའི་དང། ཤེས་རབ་དང་ཆོས་དང་སངས་རྒྱས་ཀྱི་སྤྱན་གྲངས་ཇི་ལྟ་བ་བཞིན་དུ་དངོས་པོ་སོ་སོར་ངེས་པ་དང། … ཡུལ་ཅན་རྣམས་དེ་བཞིན་ཉིད་དུ་གཅིག་པ་ཉིད་དུ་བསྒྲུབ་པོ་ཞེས་སློབ་པ་སྤྱན་ལྔ་ལ་འདོམས་པ་དང།](quote:p405-q03). I read the passage around it. Haribhadra does not divide the clause at all, and says nothing about its grammar. He does not say whether the five items after the names are their objects or their functions, and he does not explain why only the divine eye is qualified. All three moves in this paragraph are Tibetan analysis of an unannotated Indian sentence. The one Indian source that bears on the qualifier is Vimuktisena, not Haribhadra (§4).
- **Text location:** སྐབས་དང་པོའི་སྤྱི་དོན།, under སྤྱན་ལྔ་ལ་གདམས་པ། (opened at p404). This paragraph is the second of p404's three divisions, "how the śāstra explains it". Within that, it opens དང་པོ་ཚིག་དོན་དངོས།. The pack's outline marks ༢༽ དོན་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་གདམས་པ as opening here too. More precisely, it is only announced here: the paragraph finishes the first member (name), and the second begins in p406 with གཉིས་པ་ལ་ལྔ་ལས།. The division is closed a few paragraphs on, where the text assigns the tail of Haribhadra's sentence to the remaining members: [གསུམ་པ་སྟོན་པ་ལ། དེ་བཞིན་ཉིད་དུ་རོ་གཅིག་པ་ཉིད་དུ་སྒྲུབ་བོ་ཞེས་སློབ་པ། ཞེས་གསུངས། གཉིས་པ་སྟོན་པ་ལ། སྤྱན་ལྔ་ལ་འདོམས་པ་དང་། ཞེས་གསུངས།](quote:p405-q04). (The "second" there is the conclusion, the second of the two parts named in this paragraph.) After it comes p404's third division, the མཐའ་དཔྱད་པ, with definitions, divisions, boundaries, causes and purpose.
- **Section type:** preliminary exposition — a ས་བཅད that divides up a commentarial sentence, followed by the first of its pieces.
- **Argumentative role:** none in the polemical sense. What it does is set up the structure. By deciding here which of Haribhadra's words are names and which are meanings, the yig cha fixes what the later definitions have to explain. The flesh eye's definition, for instance, must account for "seeing things individually fixed in number".

## 4 · Philosophical Exposition & Commentary

**Core meaning.** Haribhadra's clause is one compressed sentence with three layers. First comes a list of five names; then five phrases, which the yig cha takes as their meanings ("fixed in number", "death and birth of all beings", "non-conceptual toward all phenomena", "realising all ārya persons", "fully awakening to all phenomena in all aspects"); finally a manner of training: the eyes and their objects are to be accomplished as one in suchness. The yig cha makes those three layers the three members of its ཚིག་དོན. This paragraph deals only with the first. It lays the sūtra's list of names beside Haribhadra's list of names, as if to say: these five words are what Haribhadra's first five words point to.

**Where the name/meaning split comes from.** The division did not originate with Chökyi Gyaltsen. Tsongkhapa's ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་ཕྲེང reads Haribhadra the same way, in so many words: [སྔ་མ་ལྔ་མིང་དང་ཕྱི་མ་ལྔ་དོན་ཡིན་པས་མིང་དོན་གྲངས་བཞིན་དུ་སྦྱར་རོ། །](quote:p405-q05) — "the first five are names and the latter five are meanings, so pair name with meaning in order." Tsongkhapa frames the topic as [what is instructed and the manner of instructing it](quote:p405-q05). Chökyi Gyaltsen turns this into a three-member division. His "manner" member picks up Tsongkhapa's "manner of instructing". Gyaltsab Je does the pairing without naming it: [ཤའི་སྤྱན་གྲངས་བཞིན་དུ་དངོས་པོ་སོ་སོར་ངེས་པ་… ལྷའི་སྤྱན་ནི་སེམས་ཅན་ཐམས་ཅད་ཀྱི་འཆི་འཕོ་དང་སྐྱེ་བ་ཇི་ལྟ་བ་བཞིན་དུ་ཤེས་པ](quote:p405-q06). Panchen Sonam Drakpa's Drepung Loseling commentary does the same, and calls each meaning the eye's "function" (བྱེད་ལས): [ཤའི་སྤྱན་གྲངས་ཇི་ལྟ་བ་བཞིན་དུ་… མཐོང་བའི་བྱེད་ལས་དང་ལྡན།](quote:p405-q07). So the structural reading this paragraph sets up is common to the whole Gelug line, and Tsongkhapa states it most explicitly.

**Why only the divine eye is "born of ripening".** In Haribhadra's clause only the second eye has a qualifier. The yig cha repeats it here without comment. The explanation comes later in the same text, where Chökyi Gyaltsen distinguishes the divine eye among the five eyes from the divine-eye clairvoyance among the six: [འོ་ན། ལྷའི་མིག་གི་མངོན་ཤེས་དང་། ལྷའི་སྤྱན་ལ་ཁྱད་པར་ཅི་ཡོད་ཅེ་ན་ཁྱད་པར་ཡོད་དེ། དང་པོ་ཚེ་འདིར་བསམ་གཏན་སྒོམ་པའི་སྟོབས་ལས་བྱུང་། གཉིས་པ་དེ་རང་གི་རྒྱུ་སྔོན་གྱི་དགེ་བ་ཟག་བཅས་ཀྱི་ལས་ཀྱི་རྣམ་པར་སྨིན་པ་ལས་བྱུང་བའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p405-q09). The clairvoyance arises from meditative stabilisation in this life; the eye arises from the ripening of past contaminated virtue. He supports this with Haribhadra's two contrasting phrases, "divine eye arisen from formation" in the clairvoyance list and "divine [eye] born of ripening" in this one, and with Ārya Vimuktisena. Vimuktisena is the Indian source. In his commentary on the Twenty-five Thousand (Toh. 3787; the passage is quoted here from the ཉི་ཁྲི་སྣང་བ edition in the Commentaries collection) he says: [མིག་འདི་གཉིས་ཀྱི་ཁྱད་པར་ནི་འདི་ཡིན་ཏེ། སྤྱན་ལྔས་བསྡུས་པའི་ལྷའི་སྤྱན་གང་ཡིན་པ་ནི་རྣམ་པར་སྨིན་པ་ལས་བྱུང་བ་འདི་ཡིན་ལ། མངོན་པར་ཤེས་པས་བསྡུས་པ་འདི་ནི་བསམ་གཏན་མངོན་པར་འདུས་བྱས་པ་ལས་བྱུང་བ་ཡིན་ནོ། །](quote:p405-q08). Haribhadra's qualifier is his compressed version of Vimuktisena's distinction. Gyaltsab and Panchen both gloss it the same way, as the ripening of (contaminated) virtuous karma.

**Historical debate.** The one qualifier raises two further questions, and the Tibetan textbooks answer them differently (see §5). (a) Is the flesh eye also born of ripening? Vimuktisena explains its limited range by saying that [དེ་ནི་རྣམ་པར་སྨིན་པ་ལས་བྱུང་བ་ཉིད་ཀྱིས་ཡུལ་སོ་སོར་ངེས་པ་ཉིད་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་རམ། ཤའི་མིག་ནི་བདག་པོའི་འབྲས་བུའི་ཡུལ་ཅན་ཡིན་ལ།](quote:p405-q10) — its object is fixed because it is born of ripening, or else because the flesh eye has dominant results as its objects. (b) Are the "eyes" physical organs or awarenesses? Tsongkhapa, explaining Vimuktisena's second reason, calls the flesh eye [མིག་དབང་ཁྱད་པར་ཅན་ཞིག](quote:p405-q11) — a special eye-faculty.

**Logical reasoning.** This yig cha settles (b) with a two-level definition. The member of the five eyes (ཤའི་སྤྱན) is an awareness. The organ (ཤའི་མིག) is its uncommon dominant condition: [སྤྱན་ལྔའི་ནང་ཚན་གང་ཞིག རང་གི་ཐུན་མོང་མ་ཡིན་པའི་བདག་རྐྱེན་དུ་གྱུར་པའི་ཤའི་མིག་ལ་བརྟེན་ནས་དཔག་ཚད་བརྒྱའི་གཟུགས་ཕྲ་རགས་མངོན་སུམ་དུ་མཐོང་བའི་རིག་པ། ཤའི་སྤྱན་གྱི་མཚན་ཉིད།](quote:p405-q12). An opponent argues that the organ, as a flesh eye, must then be a consciousness. Chökyi Gyaltsen replies that the organ is not a subject at all: [ཤའི་མིག་ཡུལ་ཅན་མ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p405-q13). On that basis, Tsongkhapa's "eye-faculty" is the organ and not the eye in the list of five, and Vimuktisena's reasons explain the range of the organ that supports the awareness. The companion mtha' dpyod compresses the whole instruction into a single syllogism: the eyes and their objects are [བདེན་སྟོང་ངོ་བོ་གཅིག་ཏུ་གྲུབ](quote:p405-q14), one in being empty of true existence. That is the "manner" member, which this paragraph announces and leaves for later.

**Sera Jey specificity.** On the structural point the Gelug textbooks agree, so there is nothing distinctively Sera Jey about this paragraph. Where the yig cha is distinctive is in what it later builds on the paragraph's two words, མིག and སྤྱན: organ versus awareness, with the organ as dominant condition. Jamyang Shepa at Gomang draws the same line against opponents he calls ignorant (§5). The Drepung Loseling commentary of Panchen Sonam Drakpa, at least in the passage located, pairs names with functions and does not raise the organ question.

## 5 · Bones of Contention 🦴

- **What is disputed?** Nothing inside the paragraph. It lays out a division and argues nothing. But the lemma it quotes carries two live disputes, which the yig cha's own analysis takes up a few paragraphs later. (1) How far does "born of ripening" reach — does it cover the flesh eye as well, and does it make these eyes karmic ripening results in the Abhidharma sense? (2) Are the flesh and divine "eyes" of the list physical sense-organs or awarenesses?
- **Who argues what?** On (1), Vimuktisena makes the flesh eye's range depend on its being [born of ripening](quote:p405-q10). Gyaltsab, Panchen and Chökyi Gyaltsen confine the qualifier to the divine eye and gloss it as the ripening of past virtue ([q06](quote:p405-q06), [q07](quote:p405-q07), [q09](quote:p405-q09)). Jamyang Shepa's ཕར་ཕྱིན་མཐའ་དཔྱོད་རིན་ཆེན་སྒྲོན་མེ attacks those who, following Vimuktisena, make every flesh eye a ripening result and also count it as a path: [བྱང་སེམས་ཚོགས་ལམ་པའི་རྒྱུད་ཀྱི་ཤའི་སྤྱན་ཆོས་ཅན། ལུང་མ་བསྟན་ཡིན་པར་ཐལ། རྣམ་སྨིན་གྱི་འབྲས་བུ་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p405-q15). If it were an Abhidharma ripening result it would be ethically neutral, yet a path-consciousness is virtuous. On (2), Tsongkhapa's wording calls the flesh eye [an eye-faculty](quote:p405-q11). Jamyang Shepa refutes those who hold that the flesh and divine eyes are [མིག་དབང་གཟུགས་ཅན](quote:p405-q16), physical eye-organs, citing the Bṛhaṭṭīkā, which calls the flesh eye a consciousness. Chökyi Gyaltsen gives the eye (སྤྱན) as an awareness and the organ (མིག) as its dominant condition ([q12](quote:p405-q12), [q13](quote:p405-q13)). This is a Sera Jey–Gomang convergence against an unnamed third view, not a Sera–Drepung split. Neither text names the opponent, so I do not identify one.
- **Why does it matter?** The instruction's "manner" is to train in the eyes and their objects as one in suchness. That requires the eyes to be awarenesses of a path, able to realise, and not ethically neutral organs. If "flesh eye" in Haribhadra's list meant the organ, the thing being trained would be something that, on Chökyi Gyaltsen's own account, [is not a subject](quote:p405-q13).
- **Resolution:** Sera Jey's position, stated later in this same text: the five eyes are awarenesses. The flesh eye relies on the flesh-eye organ as its uncommon dominant condition. "Born of ripening" belongs to the divine eye and separates it from the divine-eye clairvoyance produced by meditation. This leaves an unresolved friction in the paragraph itself. It quotes Haribhadra as ཤའི་མིག་དང་, while Haribhadra's text and Chökyi Gyaltsen's own mtha' dpyod read bare ཤའི་ (§2). On the yig cha's own terms, the bare reading is the one that fits: the governing noun is Haribhadra's final སྤྱན, and ཤའི་མིག is the organ. How the ཤའི་མིག reading got into the paragraph I cannot tell from the sources.

## 6 · Key Terminology & Technical Concepts

- **སྤྱན་ལྔ (the five eyes):** flesh, divine, wisdom, dharma and buddha eyes — the seventh of the ten instructions.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *pañca cakṣūṃṣi*.
  - **How understood here:** an instruction, because the sūtra shows their conventional power to see and then teaches that they and their objects are one in suchness. Gyaltsab and p404 explain it as the cause of practising without depending on others.
  - **Debates:** whether members of the list are organs or awarenesses (§5).
- **ཤའི་མིག / ཤའི་སྤྱན (flesh eye as organ / as eye of the list):** the sūtra's མིག and Haribhadra's སྤྱན, which this yig cha separates into a dominant condition and the awareness it supports.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *māṃsa-cakṣus*.
  - **How understood here:** its "meaning" in Haribhadra's gloss is seeing things fixed individually in number, from a hundred leagues up to a trichiliocosm (p406).
  - **Debates:** whether its limited range comes from ripening or from its being the subject of dominant results (Vimuktisena gives both, as alternatives; Tsongkhapa takes them as two reasons).
- **རྣམ་པར་སྨིན་པ་ལས་བྱུང་བའི་ལྷའི་སྤྱན (divine eye born of ripening):** the divine eye of the five-eye list. It is distinguished from ལྷའི་མིག་གི་མངོན་ཤེས, which arises from meditation in this life.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *vipākaja divya-cakṣus* (as against *abhisaṃskārika*).
  - **How understood here:** born of the ripening of past contaminated virtue; the qualifier is there to tell it apart from the clairvoyance.
  - **Debates:** whether "ripening" here means an Abhidharma ripening result (and so neutral) — Jamyang Shepa denies it for the flesh eye as path.
- **མིང་གི་སྒོ་ནས་གདམས་པ / དོན་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་གདམས་པ / ཇི་ལྟར་གདམས་པའི་ཚུལ (instruction by name / by meaning / manner of instruction):** Chökyi Gyaltsen's three-way reading of Haribhadra's clause, which builds on Tsongkhapa's pairing of the first five items (names) with the next five (meanings).
  - **How understood here:** the names are matched to the sūtra's list; the meanings to the sūtra's description of each eye; the manner to "accomplish as one in suchness".
  - **Debates:** none located; Gyaltsab and Panchen pair names and functions the same way without naming the division.
