# How the six clairvoyances are instructed — "primordially pacified", and what it means to say so

## 1 · Passage & Context

**Source Text:**

> གསུམ་པ་སྟོན་པ་ལ། གཟོད་མ་ནས་ཞི་བ་ཉིད་དུ་སྦྱར་བར་བྱའོ། ། ཞེས་གསུངས། བཞི་པ་སྟོན་པ་ལ། མངོན་པར་ཤེས་པ་དྲུག་ལ་འདོམས་པ་དང་། ཞེས་གསུངས། གཞུང་དོན་ནི་བྱང་ཆེན་མངོན་ཤེས་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་ཐོབ་བོ། ། ཞེས་སོགས་ཀྱི་མདོ་ཆོས་ཅན༑ མངོན་པར་ཤེས་པ་དྲུག་ལ་གདམས་པ་ཡིན་ཏེ། མངོན་པར་ཤེས་པ་དྲུག་གིས་ཐ་སྙད་དུ་ཡུལ་གཟིགས་པའི་ནུས་པ་བསྟན་ནས། དེ་དྲུག་པོ་དོན་དམ་པར་མེད་ཅེས་འདོམས་པའི་མདོ་ཚིག་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར།

**Preceding Section:** p415 opens the commentary on the eighth of the ten instructions, the six clairvoyances, and divides it four ways: instruction by name (མིང་གི་སྒོ་ནས་གདམས་པ), instruction by meaning (དོན་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་གདམས་པ), the manner of instructing (ཇི་ལྟར་གདམས་པའི་ཚུལ), and the conclusion (མཇུག་བསྡུ་བ). It then quotes Haribhadra's list of the six names — རྫུ་འཕྲུལ, ལྷའི་རྣ་བ, གཞན་གྱི་སེམས་ཤེས་པ, སྔོན་གྱི་གནས་རྗེས་སུ་དྲན་པ, the divine eye "arisen from formative effort", and ཟག་པ་ཟད་པ་ཤེས་པ. p416 is the second division, and it is long: for each of the six in turn it quotes the Twenty-Five-Thousand sūtra passage that teaches it (the bodhisattva shakes the great earth, becoming one becomes many; hears the sounds of gods and men; knows others' minds as they are; recollects former lives; sees beings die and be reborn; manifests the knowledge of non-arising without falling to the śrāvaka or pratyekabuddha grounds), then pairs each with the phrase of Haribhadra that explains it (ས་གཡོ་བ་ལ་སོགས་པ, སྒྲ་ཆུང་ངུ་དང་ཅིག་ཤོས་ཐོས་པ, and so on), and closes with the sūtra's summary that the bodhisattva practising the perfection of wisdom completes and purifies the six.

**Following Section:** p418 opens the མཐའ་དཔྱད — the analytical part — under six heads: the support of the clairvoyances, their nature, their boundaries (ས་མཚམས), cause, purpose and distinctions. It settles the first at once from the ཀུན་བཏུས (the first five arise in dependence on an actual concentration, the sixth on the fourth concentration at its utmost, none on the formless absorptions), then gives definitions of clairvoyance in general and of miraculous power and the divine ear in particular, with their fourfold and threefold divisions.

**What this paragraph is doing.** It finishes the textual commentary (the part the gser phreng tradition would call the སྦྱོར་བ) before the analysis begins. Its first two sentences complete the fourfold division announced in p415 by supplying Haribhadra's phrases for the third and fourth heads: "to be applied as primordially pacified" is the *manner* of the instruction; "instructing in the six clairvoyances" is the *conclusion*. Its last sentence is the གཞུང་དོན, a single syllogism that compresses the whole sūtra passage into its meaning: these sūtra words instruct in the six clairvoyances because they first show the six's power to see their objects conventionally and then instruct that the six do not exist ultimately. The two limbs of that reason are, in effect, p416 and the third head of p417 restated.

## 3 · Structural Context (Dkar Chag)

- **Abhisamayālaṃkāra location:** chapter one, the instructions (གདམས་ངག) that are the second of the ten topics of the knowledge of all aspects. The pack names no lemma for this paragraph, but the root verse is not in doubt: it is the verse that lists the ten instructions, [སྒྲུབ་དང་བདེན་པ་རྣམས་དང་ནི། … སྤྱན་ལྔ་དང་ནི་མངོན་ཤེས་ཀྱི། ། ཡོན་ཏན་དྲུག་དང་མཐོང་ལམ་དང་། …](quote:p417-q01), where the six clairvoyances are the eighth item and are named only as "the qualities of clairvoyance, six". Maitreya says nothing about *how* they are instructed; everything this paragraph argues comes from Haribhadra and the sūtra, not from the root.
- **Haribhadra's commentary location:** the འགྲེལ་པ་དོན་གསལ's running gloss on the ten instructions, immediately after the five eyes and before the path of seeing. The whole gloss on this instruction is [one sentence](quote:p417-q02): the six names, their six functions, "are to be realised as primordially pacified", and "instructing in the six clairvoyances". The yig cha's four heads in p415–p417 are precisely these four pieces of Haribhadra's sentence, taken in order — which is why p417 has so little of its own to say before its syllogism. What Haribhadra does *not* do is say what "primordially pacified" means, or introduce the two truths. The words ཐ་སྙད and དོན་དམ appear nowhere in his gloss. Nor does he raise any of the six questions p418 is about to spend many paragraphs on — the support of the clairvoyances, their boundaries, their relation to the concentrations. That whole analytical apparatus is drawn from Asaṅga's ཀུན་བཏུས and from the Tibetan commentaries, not from the Indian commentary on this line.
- **Text location:** སྐབས་དང་པོའི་སྤྱི་དོན།, within མངོན་ཤེས་དྲུག་ལ་གདམས་པ། (opening at p415). The pack's outline puts p417 under the following head, མཐའ་དཔྱད་པ་ལ། › ༡༽ མངོན་ཤེས་ཀྱི་རྟེན།, "opening at p417". Read against the text, that is one paragraph early: the words མཐའ་དཔྱད་པ་ལ། and the six-way division begin p418, and p417 belongs entirely to the fourfold division of the textual commentary announced in p415. I take p417 as the last part of the textual commentary (the third and fourth heads, plus the gloss on the text's meaning), not as the start of the analysis.
- **Section type:** textual commentary (སྦྱོར་བ, "application" of Haribhadra to the sūtra), closing with a summarising syllogism. There is no opponent here.
- **Argumentative role:** it fixes, before the analysis, what the sūtra passage *is* — an instruction — and what that instruction consists in. The two-truths framing is put in place here so that p418's analysis of the clairvoyances as conventional phenomena (supports, definitions, divisions) can go ahead without anyone mistaking it for an assertion that they are truly established.

## 4 · Philosophical Exposition & Commentary

**Core meaning.** The sūtra passage p416 went through describes what a bodhisattva can *do*: shake the earth, hear every sound, read minds, remember lives, see death and rebirth, destroy the contaminations. Why is that an *instruction* (གདམས་ངག) rather than a eulogy? Chökyi Gyaltsen's answer is in the two-limbed reason. The sūtra first shows the six's power to see their objects conventionally, and then teaches that the six do not exist ultimately. The instruction lies in the second limb, qualifying the first: a bodhisattva is to cultivate these powers while never taking them, or the objects they see, as ultimately real.

**The sūtra itself supplies the second limb.** It is tempting to think the "ultimately non-existent" clause has been read into the sūtra from Haribhadra. It has not. In the Twenty-Five-Thousand itself, the passage the yig cha summarises as its subject, [མངོན་པར་ཤེས་པའི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་རབ་ཏུ་འཐོབ་སྟེ། རྫུ་འཕྲུལ་གྱི་རྣམ་པ་དུ་མ་ཉམས་སུ་མྱོང་བར་བྱེད་དོ།](quote:p417-q04), runs on through the list of miracles and then turns: [རྫུ་འཕྲུལ་དེས་རློམ་སེམས་སུ་འགྱུར་བ་མེད་དོ། །དེ་ཅིའི་ཕྱིར་ཞེ་ན། རང་བཞིན་གྱིས་སྟོང་པ་དང། རང་བཞིན་གྱིས་དབེན་པ་དང། རང་བཞིན་གྱིས་མ་སྐྱེས་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p417-q05) — no conceit arises from that miracle, because it is empty by nature, isolated by nature, unborn by nature. The yig cha's reason is a fair two-part précis of the sūtra: what the power does, then why it is not to be grasped. (I quote the sūtra from the Kangyur's Pañcaviṃśatisāhasrikā, part 1; the yig cha's own citation in its subject, …ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ་ཐོབ་བོ།, is an abbreviation, not a verbatim quotation of this or of any sūtra text located.)

**From "primordially pacified" to "ultimately non-existent".** Haribhadra's word is གཟོད་མ་ནས་ཞི་བ་ཉིད, not དོན་དམ་པར་མེད. The step from one to the other is taken explicitly by Dharmakīrtiśrī (གསེར་གླིང་པ), in his sub-commentary on Haribhadra preserved in the Tengyur: [གཟོད་མ་ནས་ཞི་བ་ཉིད་དུ་ཞེས་བྱ་བ་ནི་དེ་ཁོ་ན་ཉིད་དུ་ན་རང་བཞིན་གྱིས་མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས་པ་ཉིད་ཡིན་པའི་ཕྱིར་རོ།](quote:p417-q06) — "primordially pacified" because *in reality* (དེ་ཁོ་ན་ཉིད་དུ) they are naturally beyond sorrow. That is the only Indian gloss on Haribhadra's phrase that I located, and it already carries the restriction to the ultimate that the Tibetan commentaries make explicit.

**The Gelug lineage of the syllogism.** Tsongkhapa's ལེགས་བཤད་གསེར་ཕྲེང gives the reading every later Gelug text follows: the clairvoyances are [to be generated in the continuum conventionally, and ultimately realised as primordially pacified of elaboration](quote:p417-q07) (…ཐ་སྙད་དུ་རྒྱུད་ལ་བསྐྱེད་ཅིང་དོན་དམ་པར་དེ་དག་རྣམས་གཟོད་མ་ནས་སྤྲོས་པ་ཉེ་བར་ཞི་བ་ཉིད་དུ་རྟོགས་ཏེ་བསྒྲུབ་བོ). His gloss adds སྤྲོས་པ་ཉེ་བར་ཞི་བ, which ties Haribhadra's ཞི་བ to the Madhyamaka pacification of elaborations. Gyaltsab Je's རྣམ་བཤད་སྙིང་པོའི་རྒྱན casts it as the syllogism the yig cha imitates: [མངོན་པར་ཤེས་པ་དྲུག་པོ་རྣམས་ཐ་སྙད་དུ་རྒྱུད་ལ་ཇི་ལྟར་འབྱུང་བ་དང་། དོན་དམ་པར་གཟོད་མ་ནས་ཞི་བ་ཉིད་དུ་རྟོགས་པར་བྱའོ། ། ཞེས་སྟོན་པའི་ཚུལ་གྱིས་འདོམས་པའི་ཕྱིར།](quote:p417-q08). Both are tier 2, within the same Gelug transmission, and neither is in any tension with p417.

**Chökyi Gyaltsen's two formulations.** In his own debate manual on chapter one, Chökyi Gyaltsen states the application more sharply than here: [མངོན་ཤེས་དྲུག་ཐ་སྙད་ཙམ་དུ་ཡོད་ཅིང་། དོན་དམ་པར་མེད་པར་རྟོགས་པར་གྱིས་ཤིག](quote:p417-q09) — the six "exist merely conventionally" (ཐ་སྙད་ཙམ་དུ་ཡོད), and one is to realise that they do not exist ultimately. The སྤྱི་དོན's version in p417 replaces "merely conventionally existent" with "the power to see objects conventionally". That makes the first limb a summary of p416's catalogue of functions rather than a bare ontological statement. Both keep Tsongkhapa's two truths, and neither keeps Gyaltsab's "how they arise in the continuum".

**Logical reasoning.** The pervasion the syllogism relies on is that any sūtra-speech teaching the qualities of a path so that one realises the path-qualities are empty is an instruction (གདམས་ངག). This is the frame the yig cha has used throughout the instructions. For the first of them, the nature of practice, its གཞུང་དོན reads [ཡུལ་བདེན་པ་གཉིས་ལས་མི་འདའ་བར་ཡུལ་ཅན་ཐབས་ཤེས་ཟུང་འབྲེལ་གྱི་སྒོ་ནས་ཉམས་སུ་ལོངས་ཤིག](quote:p417-q15). For the five eyes, immediately before this, it reads [སྤྱན་ལྔ་ཡུལ་ཡུལ་ཅན་དང་བཅས་པ་རྣམས་བདེན་སྟོང་རོ་གཅིག་པ་ཉིད་དུ་ཐོབ་བོ](quote:p417-q16). Each time the sūtra is an instruction because it attaches emptiness of truth to something conventionally taught. That is why p417 needs no defence of its reason. The only thing that could be questioned is whether "ultimately non-existent" is Haribhadra's meaning, and Dharmakīrtiśrī and the sūtra together answer that.

**Sera Jey specificity.** On this syllogism there is no distinctive Sera Jey position that I could find. The Drepung Loseling textbook of Panchen Sonam Drakpa [reproduces Gyaltsab's reason almost word for word](quote:p417-q13). Jamyang Shepa's Gomang textbook reads [ཐ་སྙད་དུ་མངོན་ཤེས་དྲུག་གི་མཐུ་ནུས་བསྐྱེད་པ་དང་། དོན་དམ་པར་གདོད་ནས་ཞི་བ་ཉིད་དུ་རྟོགས་པར་བྱ](quote:p417-q14), with the *power* of the six as the conventional limb, which is interestingly closer to Chökyi Gyaltsen's ནུས་པ than to Gyaltsab's འབྱུང་བ. Jamyang Shāntipa's [མེ་ལོང](quote:p417-q10) turns the instruction into two imperatives, realise them as ultimately unestablished and cultivate them conventionally. All of these are tier-5 texts at a distance from this curriculum, and all agree with p417. The differences between the houses here are differences of wording, not of view.

## 5 · Bones of Contention 🦴

p417 does not open with a ཁ་ཅིག and argues with nobody. It is the settled Gelug reading, and within the four monastic textbooks located there is no disagreement about it. But it is not the only reading the Indian commentaries offer, and the differences there are real.

- **What is disputed?** What the instruction on the clairvoyances actually *consists in*: an ontological qualification (they do not exist ultimately), a psychological one (do not take pride in them or apprehend them), or an idealist one (they are nothing but mind).
- **Who argues what?**
  - *The Gelug reading* — Tsongkhapa, Gyaltsab Je, Chökyi Gyaltsen, Panchen Sonam Drakpa, Jamyang Shepa. The instruction is a two-truths qualification: conventionally they arise, or are to be generated, or have power; ultimately they are pacified, or do not exist. Dharmakīrtiśrī's ["in reality, naturally nirvāṇa"](quote:p417-q06) is the Indian authority closest to it.
  - *Vimuktisena* (tier 4, an Indian commentary on the *Ornament* at some remove from this curriculum) puts the weight elsewhere. For him the instruction on miraculous power is [the blocking of the mind that apprehends it](quote:p417-q11) (དེའི་དམིགས་པའི་སེམས་བཀག་པ), and he cites the sūtra's "does not apprehend the miracle… by which one would become conceited". That is the རློམ་སེམས clause of [the sūtra](quote:p417-q05) read for its practical sense, not its ontological one. It is compatible with the Gelug reading but not the same claim: it locates the instruction in the practitioner's attitude, not in the status of the object.
  - *Dharmamitra* (tier 4) gives a different reason altogether: what makes it an instruction is the certainty that [all of these are of the nature of the aspects of this or that mind](quote:p417-q12) (འདི་རྣམས་ཐམས་ཅད་ཉིད་ནི་དེ་དང་དེའི་སེམས་ཀྱི་རྣམ་པའི་བདག་ཉིད་དོ). That is a mind-only account of the clairvoyances. Taken at face value, it is incompatible with a Prāsaṅgika reading in which the conventional clairvoyances and their objects are not reducible to mind.
- **Why does it matter?** The yig cha's whole apparatus in p418 onward treats the clairvoyances as conventionally real mental qualities with supports, definitions and divisions. That is safe only if the instruction is read as Tsongkhapa reads it, with the conventional left standing and only true existence negated. On Dharmamitra's reading the conventional analysis would itself be an analysis of mental aspects. On Vimuktisena's, the sūtra would be warning against attitudes, not stating a Madhyamaka thesis.
- **Resolution.** Sera Jey follows Tsongkhapa and Gyaltsab. Chökyi Gyaltsen does not argue the point with the Indian alternatives; he states the Gelug syllogism and moves on to the analysis. His grounds, reconstructed from the sources above rather than stated by him, would be that the sūtra itself gives the reason for non-attachment as natural emptiness, and that Haribhadra's gloss, as Dharmakīrtiśrī reads it, is about what is so "in reality". The evidence for this resolution rests on the root sūtra and a Tengyur sub-commentary. The yig cha tradition's own silence about Dharmamitra and Vimuktisena here should be noted: nobody in the Gelug texts located argues against them on this point.

## 6 · Key Terminology & Technical Concepts

- **གཟོད་མ་ནས་ཞི་བ་ཉིད་:** "pacified from the very beginning", Haribhadra's qualification of all six clairvoyances.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** presumably *ādiśānta*. No Sanskrit text was consulted here. Dharmakīrtiśrī glosses it with རང་བཞིན་གྱིས་མྱ་ངན་ལས་འདས་པ, the term Prajñāpāramitā literature habitually pairs with it.
  - **How understood here:** as ultimate non-existence. p417 renders it དོན་དམ་པར་མེད; Tsongkhapa adds "of elaboration" (སྤྲོས་པ་ཉེ་བར་ཞི་བ).
  - **Debates:** whether it is realised (རྟོགས་པར་བྱ, Haribhadra as located) or applied (སྦྱར་བར་བྱ, the yig cha's citation); see section 2.
- **མངོན་པར་ཤེས་པ་དྲུག་:** the six clairvoyances — miraculous power, divine ear, knowledge of others' minds, recollection of former abodes, the divine eye, and knowledge of the exhaustion of contaminations.
  - **Sanskrit equivalent:** *ṣaḍ abhijñāḥ*.
  - **How understood here:** Haribhadra's gloss gives the sixth as abandoning *both* afflictive and knowledge obscurations. Tsongkhapa's gser phreng notes that śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas also have the actual clairvoyance, but that the commentary here explains it as exhausting the contaminations of both obscurations. He cites a text he calls དག་ལྡན (not identified here) for its presence in bodhisattvas from the eighth ground.
  - **Debates:** its boundaries and supports are the business of p418 and following, not of this paragraph.
- **ཐ་སྙད་དུ་ཡུལ་གཟིགས་པའི་ནུས་པ་:** "the power to see objects conventionally".
  - **How understood here:** Chökyi Gyaltsen's own wording for the conventional limb, in place of Gyaltsab's "how they arise in the continuum". The honorific གཟིགས reflects that the clairvoyances in question are the bodhisattva's and, completed, the Buddha's. The phrase fits the first five, which do see objects; for the exhaustion of contaminations, "seeing objects" has to be taken broadly.
  - **Debates:** Jamyang Shepa's "power" (མཐུ་ནུས) is the nearest parallel. No text located comments on the choice.
- **གཞུང་དོན་:** "the meaning of the text".
  - **How understood here:** the yig cha's label for the summarising syllogism that closes its textual commentary on each instruction, after it has matched sūtra passages to Haribhadra's phrases. It marks the end of the textual commentary and the point where the analysis (མཐའ་དཔྱད) will begin.
